Let T(p) denote its full F-pure threshold under a declared conventional local/global interpretation. Let L be the characteristic-zero log canonical threshold of the corresponding characteristic-zero polynomial under the matching convention.

## 3. Standard full F-pure threshold

One standard definition applicable here is local at the coordinate maximal ideal. For a field k of prime characteristic p, use R = k[x,y], m = (x,y), and, for every integer e ≥ 1, q = p^e. The Frobenius power m^[q] is the ideal generated by x^q and y^q; it is not the ordinary ideal power m^q.

For a nonzero polynomial f in m, the full local F-pure threshold is the real supremum

    sup { N / p^e : e ≥ 1, N ∈ ℕ, f^N ∉ (x^(p^e), y^(p^e)) }.

All exponents e and all admissible N occur. Using e ≥ 0 is another conventional indexing choice when its equivalence is justified. A formulation via the limit of the maxima of admissible N, via the infimum of containment ratios, or via F-purity of pairs is acceptable with a justified equivalence to the full threshold in this setting. The indexing, endpoint rounding, and normalization must be checked rather than assumed.

Primary definition reference: Karen E. Smith and Adela Vraciu, *Values of the F-pure threshold for homogeneous polynomials*, Journal of the London Mathematical Society 108 (2023), 1004–1035, **Section 2, Definition 2.1**, [publisher full text](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1112/jlms.12774). The definition itself is stated for f in a polynomial ring over a field of characteristic p and explicitly specifies the coordinate maximal ideal and Frobenius powers. Only this general-definition material is relied upon; the paper's specialized homogeneous-polynomial results are not assumed to apply to the present polynomial. Web lookup displayed Definition 2.1 and its explanatory context at extracted lines 131–140.

## 5. Characteristic-zero “log threshold”

In this context the conventional meaning is the **log canonical threshold**. For the complex polynomial, its local analytic definition at a point is the supremum of positive real c for which |f|^(-2c) is integrable on some neighborhood of that point, with respect to ordinary Lebesgue measure on the underlying real space. The global version requires local integrability throughout the affine space. The factor 2 belongs to this complex convention; a real-variable integral or a changed exponent normalization cannot be substituted silently.

An algebraic definition via log resolutions or log-canonical pairs is also standard. If used, all multiplicities, discrepancies, divisors, and local/global restrictions must have their conventional meaning. A symbol set equal by definition to a guessed numerical value is not a formalization of the log canonical threshold.

Primary definition reference: Mircea Mustaţă, *Minimal exponent and Hodge filtrations*, ZAG seminar, 19 May 2020, **slides 3–4**, [author's seminar slides](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/cheltsov/zag/pdf/ZAG19052020.pdf). These give the analytic definition, distinguish local from global integrability, and give the log-resolution formulation. Web lookup returned a 23-page PDF; extracted lines 17–80 correspond to the definition slides. No numerical specialization of these definitions is asserted in this criteria document.

A proof of A must bridge its Lean target to the genuine characteristic-zero invariant under the matching local/global convention. A disproof logically independent of A need not compute L, but must describe that limitation accurately; substituting an arbitrary real for L must not be presented as a definition of L. The first limit is over prime numbers p tending to infinity; it is not over all integers treated as valid field characteristics, not over Frobenius exponents e for a fixed p, and not merely over the residue class in B.

    For every ε > 0 there exists M such that, for every prime p ≥ M,

Naturals used as p must carry actual primality hypotheses when constructing the field and characteristic instances.

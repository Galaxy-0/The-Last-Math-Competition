Scope. The original English and Chinese statements each assert a conjunction: (A) convergence of the prime-characteristic F-pure threshold to the characteristic-zero log threshold, and (B) equality with 5/6 for every prime congruent to 2 modulo 3. Disproving B is sufficient. The verified witness is p=5, which is prime, exceeds 3, and is 2 modulo 3. No exact value of the threshold or value/truth of A is claimed.

Underlying objects. Plane K is Mathlib's genuine MvPolynomial (Fin 2) K over a field, x and y are its two indeterminates, and cusp is x^2+y^3. originPower K q is the ideal span of x^q and y^q, with no finite cutoff. The definition fpt is the real supremum of n/p^e over all natural e and n with f^n outside originPower K (p^e). This is exactly the standard polynomial-at-origin definition in Smith–Vraciu, Definition 2.1, https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1112/jlms.12774. No imported theorem about this particular polynomial is used.

All-level argument. In every coefficient field,
 (x^2+y^3)^4 = x^5(x^3+4xy^3) + y^5(6x^4y+4x^2y^4+y^7).
Thus f^4 belongs to (x^5,y^5). In characteristic 5, applying the 5^e-th power map shows f^(4*5^e) belongs to (x^(5^(e+1)),y^(5^(e+1))) for every e≥0. Ideals absorb all higher powers of f. Every admissible n at level e+1 therefore satisfies n<4*5^e, giving n/5^(e+1)≤4/5. Level e=0 permits only n=0. The admissible set contains zero and is bounded above by 4/5. Its genuine real supremum is at most 4/5, which is strictly below 5/6. Lean proves the bound for every field K of characteristic 5, so the prime-field counterexample requires no coefficient-extension bridge.

Indexing and existence. allLevels_eq_positive explicitly proves equality of the defining sets when e≥0 is replaced by e≥1, provided f is in the first ideal and the relevant ideals are proper. The e=0 contribution is only zero, already contributed by n=0 at e=1. fpt_eq_positive_levels applies this to the cusp for every positive p. thresholdAt_eq_positive_levels applies it at every maximal ideal containing f. one_not_mem and thresholdAt_set_nonempty establish nonemptiness. origin_values_bddAbove and the all-level admissible bound establish the origin set's boundedness at p=5. thresholdAt_admissible_le_one establishes boundedness by 1 at every maximal ideal containing f, for every positive p. Thus no default value of an empty or unbounded real supremum is used in the counterexample or in any local term of its global infimum.

Global convention. bracketPower I q is the actual ideal generated by all qth powers of all elements of I. thresholdAt p f I is the all-level supremum using these bracket powers. globalFpt is the infimum of thresholdAt over every maximal ideal I containing f, not merely rational points. We adopt this standard equivalent characterization for the prime-field polynomial rings occurring in the final global theorem. The characterization follows from Blickle–Mustaţă–Smith, F-thresholds of hypersurfaces, §3 and Proposition 3.2(iii–iv), https://arxiv.org/pdf/0705.1210. That source assumes F-finite regular rings. The rings F_p[x,y] used here are polynomial rings over finite prime fields and satisfy those hypotheses. The algebraic inequalities in Lean hold over any characteristic-five field for the displayed infimum; we do not claim that this reference alone identifies the displayed infimum with an ordinary global F-pure threshold over arbitrary non-F-finite fields.

Global bridge actually checked. originIdeal is the kernel of the constant-coefficient evaluation homomorphism; Lean proves it is maximal and contains the cusp. Each originPower(q) is contained in bracketPower(originIdeal,q), so its nonmembership set contains the latter nonmembership set. The origin supremum is bounded and both sets are nonempty; hence thresholdAt(originIdeal)≤fpt(origin). Every thresholdAt is nonnegative, and the global infimum has the origin term as a member, so globalFpt≤thresholdAt(originIdeal). The checked theorem globalFpt_le_origin_fpt combines these inequalities. No equality of global and origin thresholds is asserted or needed.

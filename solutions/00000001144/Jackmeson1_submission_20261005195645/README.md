# Disprove conjecture 00000001144: the Feingold–Frenkel hyperbolic Weyl group has growth rate ≤ 19/10, not 2

- **Witness.** The Feingold–Frenkel matrix A₁⁺⁺ = [[2,−1,0],[−1,2,−2],[0,−2,2]], proved to be a hyperbolic generalized Cartan matrix (indecomposable and symmetric; quadratic form indefinite and positive semidefinite on every proper principal submatrix; det = −2).
- **Weyl group.** Built as the subgroup of GL₃(ℤ) generated by the reflections rᵢ(αⱼ) = αⱼ − aᵢⱼαᵢ on the root lattice; the word-length balls with respect to {rᵢ} exhaust exactly this group.
- **Counting.** From rᵢ² = 1 and r₀r₂ = r₂r₀, every word reduces to one avoiding `ii` and `20`; counting these gives |B_k| ≤ 13(k+1)(5/3)^k, so the sphere and ball growth rates are ≤ 19/10 and neither sequence of k-th roots tends to 2.
- **Refutation.** "Every hyperbolic Weyl group has growth rate 2" is false. The true rate is ≈ 1.3247 (the plastic number, consistent with W ≅ PGL₂(ℤ); numerics only).
- **Definitions (retrieved).** Hyperbolicity in Wikipedia's form for symmetrizable matrices; Kac's subdiagram definition is supported by a lemma listing the principal minors (not proved equivalent in Lean). Sources: Wikipedia (Cartan matrix, Kac–Moody algebra, Growth rate) and arXiv 1003.0564, 0805.3018, 0910.4103.
- **Not refuted.** The existential reading (true: the rank-3 matrix with all off-diagonal entries −2 has rate 2). "At most 2" fails numerically (simply laced K₄, ≈ 2.303) but is not formalized.
- **Main theorem.** `C1144.disproof`.

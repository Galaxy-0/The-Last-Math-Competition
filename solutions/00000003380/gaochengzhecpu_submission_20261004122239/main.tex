\begin{abstract}
The noncommutative complex algebra $M_2(\C)\times M_2(\C)$ has an
automorphism exchanging its two components. This automorphism moves a
central idempotent, whereas every inner automorphism fixes the center
pointwise. It is therefore outer, even though it preserves the usual
matrix adjoint. The complete counterexample is verified in Lean.
\end{abstract}

The first explicit assertion in both languages of the source is that
every automorphism of a noncommutative algebra is inner. There is no
simplicity or factor hypothesis. We refute this universal assertion.
An inner automorphism of a unital algebra is a map $a\mapsto uau^{-1}$
for a unit $u$ of that algebra.

\begin{theorem}
Let $\A=M_2(\C)\times M_2(\C)$, with componentwise algebra operations.
The map
\[
 \sigma:\A\longrightarrow\A,\qquad \sigma(X,Y)=(Y,X),
\]
is a complex algebra automorphism, preserves the componentwise adjoint,
and is not inner. The algebra $\A$ is noncommutative.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Component exchange preserves addition, multiplication, the unit $(I,I)$,
and complex scalar multiplication. It is its own inverse. Moreover,
$\sigma(X^*,Y^*)=(Y^*,X^*)=\sigma(X,Y)^*$.

The element $e=(I,0)$ is a central idempotent: $e^2=e$ and, for every
$(X,Y)\in\A$,
\[
 e(X,Y)=(X,0)=(X,Y)e.
\]
Consequently every unit $u\in\A$ satisfies
\[
 ueu^{-1}=euu^{-1}=e.
\]
But $\sigma(e)=(0,I)\ne(I,0)=e$, so no unit of $\A$ can implement
$\sigma$ by conjugation. In particular no unitary of $\A$ can do so.

For noncommutativity, take
\[
 P=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then $PQ=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$ and
$QP=\operatorname{diag}(0,1)$ differ. Hence $(P,0)$ and $(Q,0)$
do not commute in $\A$.
\end{proof}

The implementing unit must belong to the algebra whose automorphism is
being considered. A permutation matrix in a larger ambient matrix
algebra would not establish innerness in $\A$.

\section*{Formal verification and scope}
The file \texttt{lean/Main.lean} uses actual $2\times2$ complex matrices
and their product algebra. The object \texttt{exchange} has type
$\A\simeq_{\C}\A$: it is a complete algebra equivalence, constructed
from the library's product ring equivalence and the checked scalar
compatibility. The standard matrix conjugate transpose defines the
componentwise adjoint; its preservation is proved explicitly.

The predicate \texttt{IsInner} quantifies over actual algebra units and
all elements, with the formula $\varphi(a)=uau^{-1}$. Lean checks the
central idempotent, its movement by exchange, its invariance under
\emph{every} such conjugation, and the actual products witnessing
noncommutativity. The theorem \texttt{conjecture\_false} directly negates
the universal assertion that every complex algebra with a noncommuting
pair has only inner complex algebra automorphisms.

The proof does not assume the counterexample is outer, substitute an
unrelated numerical invariant, or weaken innerness to conjugation by
particular matrices. It proves failure even when all invertible
implementers are allowed, and thus covers the more restrictive unitary
meaning as well. It does not dispute innerness results that impose
additional hypotheses such as a central simple algebra. No conclusion
about the source's unspecified ``measure of innerness'' is needed:
its first asserted universal clause already fails.

## Semantic audit
The conjecture is a four-clause conjunction; the submission refutes (C2), (C3), (C4) and honestly does not refute (C1) (which it confirms computationally: π(B₆) = 4, so by monotonicity and the ≤ 4 bound the infinite binary tree has π = 4, consistent with the claim). The refutations are mathematically correct. (C3): the single edge `K₂` is a tree with Δ = 1 and π(K₂) = 2 > 0 = 2 log_b 1 for every base b > 1 — a decisive, elementary counterexample; and even under the charitable restriction Δ ≥ 2, the path `P₄` has π = 3 > 2 = 2 log₂ 2 (also for bases e and 10), proved both in Lean (`thue_P4`, `claimDeg2_false`) and by my own computation. The passage from rational bases (formalized as `LogBound p q := p^k ≤ Δ²·q^k`, exactly equivalent to `k ≤ 2 log_{p/q} Δ`) to all real bases is the one-line monotonicity argument (`claim_mono`), which is correct. (C2): the submission includes a complete proof of the Brešar–Grytczuk–Klavžar–Niwczyk–Peterin theorem π(T) ≤ 4 (Thue's infinite square-free ternary word → Kündgen–Pelsmajer insertion of the letter 4 → depth colouring → folding lemma for path colour sequences), which I verified numerically at every step; hence π is bounded on trees, refuting unboundedness. (C4): with π ≤ 4 and 2 log Δ → ∞ the bound cannot be attained asymptotically, and subtrees of the binary tree have Δ ≤ 3 so they cannot even be an asymptotic family in Δ.

The Lean formalization is faithful: `IsPath` covers all simple paths (matching the official definition "the color sequence of any path to contain no square factor"), `HasSquare` is the standard square-factor predicate, `IsThueNumber` is the least number of colours (uniqueness proved), `IsTree` is the textbook unique-paths definition, and `Claim p q` is the literal clause for base p/q. `conjecture_00000003481_false` refutes it for every rational base p > q via K₂.

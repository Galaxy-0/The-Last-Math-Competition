The formalization retains the prime cutoff, strict fractional-part comparison, denominator and scaling literally. `primesLE N` enumerates exactly natural primes no larger than N; `primeCDF N α t` is the source's count divided by N; `primeKS N α` takes the supremum of the absolute discrepancy over the conventional KS domain t in [0,1]; `scaledKS N α` is N times that supremum. The convention t in [0,1] is disclosed rather than inferred as an extra source fact. If the omitted domain were intended to be every real t, the supremum in the informal source would be infinite rather than a real KS statistic. We do not use Lean's arbitrary supremum value on an unbounded real set to disguise that issue.

At t=1 every fractional part is strictly below 1, so the statistic is at least 1 - #(primes<=N)/N. The checked finite prime-count estimate #(primes<=N) <= N/2+2 therefore implies scaledKS N α >= N/2-2 for N>=2, uniformly for every real α. No asymptotic prime theorem, exceptional-alpha exclusion or alpha=0 specialization is needed.

M1 proves measurability of this same supremum, using its proved equality to a countable rational supremum. For t>0 a finite gap below t preserves the finite count when a rational q approaches t from below; at t=0 the count and discrepancy are zero. Boundedness/nonemptiness lemmas establish that both real suprema are genuine. This equality is a proved dependency, not a assumed measurability bridge.

For every sequence μ of real probability measures, including measures that depend arbitrarily on N, `laws μ N := Measure.map (scaledKS N) (μ N)` is proved to be a probability measure. The test integral is transported along this actual map. Thus the conclusion concerns the full law of the actual statistic, rather than a count-only proxy or a selected alpha.

`WeaklyConverges` uses the usual bounded continuous test definition, with all continuous real tests satisfying 0<=f<=1. For probabilities this normalized formulation is equivalent to the version allowing all bounded real continuous tests: given a bound |g|<=C, choose C>0 and f=(g+C)/(2C); then 0<=f<=1 and ∫g=2C∫f-C since every law and the target have mass 1. Conversely normalized tests are bounded continuous tests. For these nonnegative bounded tests the nonnegative lower integral of ENNReal.ofReal(f) is exactly the ordinary nonnegative test expectation, finite and bounded by 1. ENNReal and real convergence agree on this finite range. This is a stated standard test definition, and the proof does not assume an unproved implication from a separately defined convergence proxy.

The function test(x)=1/(1+|x|) is continuous, strictly positive everywhere and normalized. Uniform escape bounds every genuine law's expectation by ENNReal.ofReal(1/(N/2-1)) for N>=4, which tends to zero. Under any real probability measure ν its support is all of ℝ, and its integral is strictly positive. Full weak convergence would require convergence for this particular member of its full test class, contradicting uniqueness of limits.

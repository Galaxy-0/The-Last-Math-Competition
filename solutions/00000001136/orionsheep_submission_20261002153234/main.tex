\begin{abstract}
We disprove TLMC conjecture 00000001136, which asserts that a Bott--Samelson
variety $BS(s_1,\dots,s_k)$ is Gorenstein \emph{if and only if} every letter of
the word $s_1\cdots s_k$ occurs equally often.  The longest element
$w_0 \in S_3$ provides a reduced word $s_1s_2s_1$ with letter counts $(2,1)$;
nevertheless $BS(s_1,s_2,s_1)$ is Gorenstein, because every Bott--Samelson
variety is an iterated $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle, hence smooth projective, and
smoothness implies the Gorenstein property.  The ``only if'' direction fails.
\end{abstract}

\section{The conjecture}

We quote the original text verbatim.

\begin{conj*}
\emph{Definition:} Complete intersection property of Bott--Samelson varieties
means some power of the canonical bundle is very ample and the cohomology ring
is completely determined by Chern classes.
\emph{Conjecture:} $BS(s_1,\dots,s_k)$ is Gorenstein if and only if each letter
of the word $s_1\cdots s_k$ occurs equally often; and in the Gorenstein case,
the shift of the dualizing sheaf is given by reversal of the word.
\end{conj*}

\section{The geometric input: smoothness implies Gorenstein}

\begin{obs}
For a reduced word $s_1,\dots,s_k$, the Bott--Samelson variety
$BS(s_1,\dots,s_k)$ is an iterated $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle (Demazure,
\emph{D\'esingularisation des vari\'et\'es de Schubert g\'en\'eralis\'ees},
Ann.\ Sci.\ ENS 7 (1974)).  In particular it is smooth and projective.
\end{obs}

\begin{obs}
Every smooth variety is Gorenstein: smoothness means all local rings are
regular local rings, and regular local rings are Gorenstein (standard
commutative algebra; e.g.\ Matsumura, \emph{Commutative Ring Theory}, \S19).
Equivalently, the dualizing sheaf of a smooth variety is a line bundle (the
canonical bundle).
\end{obs}

Consequently \emph{every} Bott--Samelson variety $BS(s_1,\dots,s_k)$ built from
a reduced word is Gorenstein, independently of how often each letter occurs in
the word.

\section{The counterexample}

Let $w = s_1s_2s_1$, a word in the simple reflections of the symmetric group
$S_3$ (letters applied left to right).  Direct computation:
\[
(s_1s_2s_1)(1) = 3,\qquad (s_1s_2s_1)(2) = 2,\qquad (s_1s_2s_1)(3) = 1,
\]
so $s_1s_2s_1 = w_0 = (3,2,1)$, the longest element of $S_3$.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The word is reduced.}  Its length is $3$, and the Coxeter length
of $w_0$ (the number of inversions of the one-line notation $(3,2,1)$) is
$\binom{3}{2} = 3$.  Since $s_1s_2s_1 = w_0$ and the lengths agree, the word is
a reduced expression.
\item \textbf{The letter counts are unequal.}  $s_1$ occurs $2$ times, $s_2$
occurs $1$ time: $(2,1)$ with $2 \neq 1$.
\item \textbf{Nevertheless $BS(s_1,s_2,s_1)$ is Gorenstein}, by Observations 1
and 2: it is an iterated $\mathbb{P}^1$-bundle, hence smooth projective, hence
Gorenstein.
\end{itemize}

Thus $BS(s_1,s_2,s_1)$ is Gorenstein although the letters of $s_1s_2s_1$ do not
occur equally often: the ``only if'' direction of Conjecture 00000001136 fails,
and the conjecture is \textbf{disproved}.

\begin{obs}[Robustness]
The longest element $w_0 \in S_3$ has exactly two reduced expressions,
$s_1s_2s_1$ and $s_2s_1s_2$, with letter counts $(2,1)$ and $(1,2)$; no
rewording of $w_0$ satisfies the conjecture's hypothesis.  Worse, since
\emph{every} Bott--Samelson variety is Gorenstein, the conjecture's ``only if''
direction would force every reduced word to have equally often occurring
letters, which fails already for $s_1$ (counts $(1,0)$) in $S_3$.  With the
``iff'' false, the claimed ``reversal of the word'' description of the dualizing
shift is moot (for a smooth variety the dualizing sheaf is simply the canonical
bundle).
\end{obs}

of $w_0$ in $S_3$.
\item \texttt{lean4/Main.lean} formalizes the disproof in bare Lean~4 core

$[1,2,1]$ is verified to evaluate to $w_0$ and to be reduced, and the main
theorem \texttt{conjecture\_1136\_false} shows that for \emph{any} predicate
$G$ of Gorenstein-ness satisfying the classical hypothesis
$\forall w,\, G\,w$ (every Bott--Samelson variety is Gorenstein), the
conjecture's biconditional $\forall w,\, G\,w \leftrightarrow
\texttt{EqualCounts}\ w$ is false, witnessed by $[1,2,1]$.

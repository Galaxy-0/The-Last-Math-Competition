The asserted finite list of parameter pairs $(m,n)$ for which $B(m,n)$ is
finite does not exist.  For every integer $m\ge2$, the Burnside group
$B(m,2)$ is finite.  Thus there are infinitely many distinct such parameter
pairs, even when both parameters are at least $2$.

\paragraph{The actual Burnside groups.}
By definition, $B(m,2)$ is the free group on $m$ generators, modulo the
normal subgroup generated by the squares of all its elements.  Equivalently,
it is the group presented by the $m$ generators and the relations
$w^2=1$ for all words $w$; these relations are not restricted to individual
generators.

Every element $x$ in $B(m,2)$ satisfies $x^2=1$, hence $x^{-1}=x$.
For any two elements $x,y$ this gives
\[
 xy=(xy)^{-1}=y^{-1}x^{-1}=yx.
\]
The group is therefore abelian.  If its generators are $a_1,\ldots,a_m$,
every word can be rearranged, inverse letters replaced by the same generators,
and pairs of equal generators removed.  Every element is consequently
represented by one of
\[
 a_1^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots a_m^{\varepsilon_m},
 \qquad (\varepsilon_1,\ldots,\varepsilon_m)\in\{0,1\}^m.
\]
There are $2^m$ words in this list.  Surjectivity of this list onto the actual
Burnside group proves that $B(m,2)$ is finite.  Neither injectivity of the list
nor an exact order formula is required.

Now let $P$ be any proposed finite list of all finite-Burnside parameter
pairs.  Choose $m\ge2$ larger than every first coordinate in $P$.
Then $B(m,2)$ is finite, yet $(m,2)\notin P$.  This contradicts completeness
of $P$ and disproves the finite-list conjunct, hence the conjecture as stated.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The first clause of the source discusses large odd exponents, but the
finite-list clause explicitly concerns the complete list of $(m,n)$ for
which $B(m,n)$ is finite.  It contains no restriction to large odd exponents
and does not fix $m$.  The argument refutes that literal clause in both the
English and Chinese text.  It does not assert that the possible exponents
for one fixed $m$ are infinite, or that finitely many formulas cannot describe
an infinite family of parameter pairs.  No claim about the other clauses is
needed.  The example avoids both the rank-one and the exponent-one boundary
cases.

\paragraph{Formal construction and verification.}
The Lean 4.19.0 file imports only \texttt{Std} and constructs the actual
presentation.  Its words are all finite lists of signed generators over an
arbitrary basis type $A$.  The quotient congruence is generated by free
cancellation and the relation $ww=1$ for every finite word $w$.  The file
proves that concatenation and inverse words descend to the quotient, verifies
the group axioms, and proves that every quotient element squares to the
identity.

The theorem \texttt{burnside\_\allowbreak{}universal\_\allowbreak{}property} proves the defining
existence and uniqueness of a homomorphism into every exponent-two group
for every assignment of the generators.  Therefore the construction has
the required Burnside universal property, rather than merely exhibiting one
finite model of some of the relations.

For an arbitrary finite generating list, \texttt{subsetProducts} recursively
lists the products of its subsets.  The file proves that this list contains
the identity and is closed under multiplication by every listed generator,
using the proved exponent-two commutativity law.  Induction on all signed
words, followed by induction over quotient representatives, establishes
\texttt{every\_\allowbreak{}burnside\_\allowbreak{}element\_\allowbreak{}covered}.  For the basis
$A=\operatorname{Fin}(m)$, the covering list has exactly $2^m$ entries and
contains every group element for every natural $m$; it may have repetitions.

Finally,
\begin{center}
\texttt{Conjecture8657.conjecture8657\_counterexample}
\end{center}
takes an arbitrary finite list of parameter pairs and constructs a missing
pair $(m,2)$ with $m\ge2$ and with finite Burnside group.
The theorem \texttt{no\_\allowbreak{}complete\_\allowbreak{}finite\_\allowbreak{}parameter\_\allowbreak{}list} gives the explicit
negation.  The reported axioms are only the standard \texttt{propext} and
\texttt{Quot.sound}.  There are no \texttt{sorry}, \texttt{admit},
\texttt{native\_decide}, or custom axioms.

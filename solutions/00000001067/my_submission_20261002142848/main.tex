\title{Disproof of TLMC Conjecture 00000001067:\\

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
TLMC conjecture 00000001067 asserts that the maximal $B_2$ set of $\mathbb{F}_p$
has size $\lceil\sqrt p\rceil+O(1)$ and that the $O(1)$ term equals $0$ for
$p\equiv 3\pmod 4$.  Complete exhaustive enumeration shows that for
$p=11\equiv 3\pmod 4$ the maximal $B_2$ subset of $\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z}$ has
$3$ elements, while $\lceil\sqrt{11}\rceil=4$; likewise $M(19)=4<5=\lceil\sqrt{19}\rceil$.
The exactness clause is therefore false.  The counterexample $p=11$ is fully
machine-checked in Lean~4 with zero axioms and zero \texttt{sorry}.
\end{abstract}

\section{The conjecture, quoted verbatim}

The conjecture text reads, character for character:
\begin{quote}
``Definition: A B\_h set (unique h-fold sums). Conjecture: The maximal B\_2 set
in F\_p has size $\lceil\sqrt p\rceil$ + O(1), and the O(1) term equals 0 for
p $\equiv$ 3 mod 4 (an exact B\_2 result).''
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Correspondence.}  For $h=2$ in $\mathbb{F}_p$, ``unique 2-fold sums''
(up to permutation of summands, the only non-degenerate reading) is the Sidon
condition: $A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ is $B_2$ iff the sums $a+b\pmod p$
over all unordered pairs with repetition $a\le b$, $a,b\in A$, are pairwise
distinct.  ``Maximal'' is read as maximum cardinality
$M(p)=\max\{|A|: A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\text{ is }B_2\}$, and the
$O(1)=0$ clause asserts $M(p)=\lceil\sqrt p\rceil$ exactly for $p\equiv3\pmod4$.
The attack uses precisely these objects; nothing is redefined.

\section{The counterexample}

A $B_2$ set of size $k$ in $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$ has $k(k+1)/2$ distinct
unordered pair sums, all lying in a set of $p$ residues.  Exhaustive enumeration
of \emph{all} subsets gives:
\[
M(11)=3<4=\lceil\sqrt{11}\rceil,\qquad M(19)=4<5=\lceil\sqrt{19}\rceil,
\qquad 11\equiv 19\equiv 3\pmod 4 .
\]
Concretely: for $p=11$, all $\binom{11}{4}=330$ four-subsets of
$\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z}$ fail the Sidon condition (each would need its
$10$ pair sums distinct among $11$ residues; none is), while
$A=\{0,1,3\}$ is $B_2$ with pair sums $0,1,3,2,4,6$ --- all distinct mod $11$;
hence $M(11)=3$.  For $p=19$, all $\binom{19}{5}=11628$ five-subsets fail and
$\{0,1,3,7\}$ achieves $4$ (pair sums $0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8,10,14$, distinct mod
$19$), so $M(19)=4$.  Since both primes are $\equiv3\pmod4$,
the clause ``the $O(1)$ term equals $0$ for $p\equiv3\pmod4$'' is false; the
conjecture's highlighted ``exact $B_2$ result'' does not hold.  (The weaker
asymptotic part $\lceil\sqrt p\rceil+O(1)$ is not contradicted; the deviation is
$1$ in both examples.)  One counterexample suffices: the conjecture is
\textbf{FALSE}.

\subsection*{Boundary behavior}

Exact recomputation for primes $p\equiv3\pmod4$ shows the exactness fails
erratically, and is already forced by the Erd\H{o}s--Tur\'an bound
$M(p)\le\sqrt p+\tfrac12$ at $p=11,19$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccccccc}
\toprule
$p$ & 3 & 7 & 11 & 19 & 23 & 31 & 43 & 47 & 59\\
\midrule
$M(p)$ & 2 & 3 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 6 & 6 & 7\\
$\lceil\sqrt p\rceil$ & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 5 & 6 & 7 & 7 & 8\\
$M(p)=\lceil\sqrt p\rceil$? & yes & yes & \textbf{no} & \textbf{no} & yes & yes
  & \textbf{no} & \textbf{no} & \textbf{no}\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section{Lean 4 certificate (zero axioms)}

Subsets of $\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z}$ are encoded as $11$-bit masks
(bit $i$ set $\iff i\in A$); \texttt{isB2 11 n} is the Sidon condition
(distinctness of all $a+b \bmod 11$, $a\le b$).  \texttt{Main.lean} proves:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{check11}: an exhaustive check over all $2^{11}=2048$ masks that no
  mask with $\ge4$ elements is $B_2$, discharged by pure kernel reduction
  (\texttt{rfl});
\item \texttt{allRange\_spec}: bridge lemma from the Boolean bounded check to a
  $\forall$-statement (induction with core lemmas only);
\item \texttt{no\_ge4\_B2\_Z11}:
  $\forall n<2^{11},\ \texttt{isB2}\ 11\ n=\texttt{true}\to \texttt{maskPop}\ 11\ n\le 3$;
\item \texttt{witness\_B2\_Z11}: $\{0,1,3\}$ (mask $11$) is a $3$-element $B_2$ set;
\item \texttt{disproof\_1067}:
  $11\%4=3\ \wedge\ (\forall n<2^{11},\ B_2(n)\to|n|\le3)\ \wedge\ B_2(\{0,1,3\})\
  \wedge\ |\{0,1,3\}|=3\ \wedge\ \lceil\sqrt{11}\rceil=4$.
\end{itemize}
\texttt{Check.lean} runs \texttt{\#print axioms} on every theorem: each reports
``does not depend on any axioms'' (no \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice},
\texttt{Quot.sound}, \texttt{sorryAx}); \texttt{native\_decide} is not used.

\section{Reproduction}

\begin{enumerate}

      exhaustive enumeration over all $k$-subsets of $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$
      yields $M(11)=3$, $M(19)=4$, and the boundary table above.
\item \texttt{cd lean4 \&\& lake build \&\& lake env lean Check.lean} ---
      compiles the certificate and prints the axiom audit.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Conjecture 00000000048 -- FALSE.}

\textbf{Filed claim addressed.} Every tree with $m$ edges has an injective vertex labeling into $[0,Cm\log m]$ whose edge differences are distinct primes.

\section*{Proof}
Consider the tree $K_2$, consisting of two vertices joined by one edge. Here $m=1$, so for every real constant $C$,
\[Cm\log m=C\log 1=0.\]
The allowed interval is the singleton $[0,0]=\{0\}$. Every labeling into this interval assigns zero to both vertices, hence is not injective. Its only edge difference is zero, which is not prime. Thus no choice of $C$ can make the universal statement true.

This uses the explicit quantifier ``every tree.'' It does not refute a version restricted to $m\ge2$ or sufficiently large $m$, nor a version replacing $\log m$ by $\log(2+m)$.

\section*{Formalization scope}
Lean proves that the actual simple graph on Fin 2 is a tree with one edge, and proves that no injective real labeling into the filed interval exists, for every real C.

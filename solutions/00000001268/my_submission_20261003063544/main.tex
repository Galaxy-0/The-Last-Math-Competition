\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Definition:} the palindromic defect is the count of defect
letters of rich-in-palindromes words. \textit{Conjecture:} every
infinite word can be repaired to a rich word by at most one letter
insertion; and the minimality of the repair position is uniquely given
by the defect center.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

A word of length $n$ is rich when it has $n+1$ distinct palindromic
factors; an infinite word is rich when all its factors are rich. The
periodic word $(abc)^\infty$ admits no single-letter repair.

\textbf{Step 1: no long palindromic factors.} The letters of
$(abc)^\infty$ are 3-periodic. A palindromic factor of length $L' + 2$
at offset $p$ repeats letters at distances $L'+1$ (first/last) and
$L'$ (second/second-to-last). Writing $L' = 3k, 3k+1$ or $3k+2$ and
reducing each repetition equation by $3k$, the first distance yields a
letter repetition at distance $1$ (resp.\ $2$) when $L' = 3k+1$
(resp.\ $3k+2$); when $L' = 3k$ the second distance $3k$ yields a
distance-$2$ repetition. All contradict the certified lemma that the
3-periodic word never repeats a letter at distance $1$ or $2$. Hence
every nonempty palindromic factor is a single letter: exactly $4$
distinct palindromic factors $\{\varepsilon, a, b, c\}$.

\textbf{Step 2: insertions do not help.} After one letter inserted at
any position $p$, the letters at positions $p+1+d$ of the repaired
word are still the abc-periodic letters at $p+d$, so the repaired word
contains purely abc-periodic factors of every length --- e.g.\ length
$5$ (one of $abcab$, $bcabc$, $cabca$).

\textbf{Step 3: those factors are not rich.} An abc-periodic factor of
length $5$ has at most $4$ distinct palindromic factors, but richness
for length $5$ requires $6$: $4 < 6$.

For every insertion position and every inserted letter the repaired
word still contains a non-rich factor: no single insertion repairs
$(abc)^\infty$, and the defect-center clause is vacuous.

\section{Verification}

\texttt{reproduce.py} brute-forces the palindromic factors of
$(abc)^\infty$ up to length $8$ (exactly $\{\varepsilon, a, b, c\}$),
checks all $9$ single-letter insertions (three letters, three gap
positions) for a remaining non-rich factor, and re-derives the
non-richness of the abc-periodic words of lengths $5$ and $6$. The
Lean package (core Lean~4, v4.33.1) certifies the 3-periodicity
machinery, the universal no-long-palindrome theorem, and the
post-insertion window facts; all nine audited theorems report
\texttt{does not depend on any axioms}.

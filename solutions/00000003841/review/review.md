## Result
For \(n\ge1\), a real exponent \(c\) is admissible for the plactic monoid \(P_n\) exactly when \(c\ge n\). Thus the least/optimal exponent is \(c(n)=n\), which is at least 1 and unbounded in \(n\).

## Proof
The \(n\) letter classes generate \(P_n\), so a homomorphism is determined by \(n\) target elements. This gives at most \(|M|^n\) homomorphisms to any finite monoid \(M\), proving admissibility of every \(c\ge n\).

For the lower bound, both Knuth relations have the same underlying three factors, so every assignment of letters to a commutative monoid descends to \(P_n\). In \(\mathbb Z/p\), every nonzero assignment extends to a surjective homomorphism. There are \(p^n-1\) such assignments. If \(c<n\), choosing a sufficiently large prime gives \(p^n-1\le p^c\le p^n/2\), impossible for \(p\ge3\), \(n\ge1\). Hence \(c\ge n\) is necessary.

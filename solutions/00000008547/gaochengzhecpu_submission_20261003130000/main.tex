The assertion that every geometric Peck lattice has a symmetric
characteristic polynomial is false.  The five-element diamond lattice
$M_3$ is a counterexample: it is geometric, Peck, and strictly Sperner,
but its characteristic polynomial is
\[
\chi_{M_3}(t)=t^2-3t+2.
\]
Its constant and leading coefficients differ.  Thus its coefficients
are not symmetric about half its rank, which is $1$.

\paragraph{Conventions and scope.}
A finite lattice is geometric if it is atomistic and upper semimodular.
We use the standard Peck conditions: rank symmetry, rank unimodality,
and the strong Sperner property~\cite{stanley}.
The source instead says ``rank-symmetric strictly Sperner.''
Our example satisfies this condition too: its unique maximum antichain
is its middle rank.  We disprove the universal characteristic-polynomial
assertion, which is one conjunct of the conjecture; no claim about the
infinite Young lattice is needed.

\paragraph{The lattice and its geometric property.}
Let
\[
M_3=\{0,a,b,c,1\},\qquad
0<a<1,\quad 0<b<1,\quad 0<c<1,
\]
with $a,b,c$ pairwise incomparable.  Two distinct atoms have meet $0$
and join $1$; all other meets and joins are forced by comparability.
Every element is the join of the atoms below it, using the empty join
for $0$, so the lattice is atomistic.  Its rank function has values
$0,1,1,1,2$.

To verify upper semimodularity, suppose $x$ covers $x\wedge y$.
If $x\leq y$, the premise is impossible.  If $y<x$, then
$x\wedge y=y$ and $x\vee y=x$, so the conclusion repeats the premise.
The only remaining case consists of two distinct atoms.  Their meet
is $0$, their join is $1$, and $1$ covers $y$.
Hence $M_3$ is geometric.

\paragraph{Both Sperner requirements.}
The rank sizes are $(1,3,1)$, so they are symmetric and unimodal.
Any antichain containing $0$ or $1$ is a singleton; every other
antichain is a subset of $\{a,b,c\}$.  Consequently the largest
antichain has size $3$, and it is uniquely the middle rank.
In particular the lattice is strictly Sperner.

A union of two antichains cannot contain all five elements: the chain
$0<a<1$ would then contribute three comparable elements to two
antichains.  The upper bound $4$ is attained by
$\{0,a,b,c\}$, the union of the bottom and middle ranks.
A union of three antichains has size at most $5$, attained by the
whole lattice.  For more antichains the same bound is attained by
allowing empty antichains.  These bounds are exactly the sums of the
largest $k$ rank sizes.  Thus $M_3$ is strongly Sperner, and hence
Peck also in the standard sense.

\paragraph{The actual characteristic polynomial.}
Write $\mu(x)=\mu(0,x)$.  The defining M\"obius recurrence is
\[
\sum_{y\leq x}\mu(y)=
\begin{cases}1&x=0,\\0&x\ne0.\end{cases}
\]
It gives $\mu(0)=1$, $\mu(a)=\mu(b)=\mu(c)=-1$, and
$\mu(1)=-(1-1-1-1)=2$.  Therefore the defining sum gives
\[
\chi_{M_3}(t)=\sum_{x\in M_3}\mu(x)t^{2-\operatorname{rk}(x)}
=t^2-3t+2.
\]
The coefficient sequence in ascending degree is $(2,-3,1)$, so it
is not palindromic.  If ``symmetric'' were instead intended as a
reflection of values or roots about $1$, that also fails:
$\chi(2)=0$ whereas $\chi(0)=2$.

\paragraph{Formal verification and exhaustive scope.}
The accompanying Lean 4.19.0 file uses only \texttt{Std}.  Its type
\texttt{LatticeData} includes the order, bottom, top, meet, join, and
rank on five elements.  It checks the partial-order and lattice laws,
the cover-based rank rule, atomisticity, and upper semimodularity.

Every subset is encoded by its five membership bits, as an element of
\texttt{Fin 32}.  The theorem
\texttt{every\_prop\_subset\_encoded} covers arbitrary
proposition-valued subsets as well.  Chains and antichains use the
actual order, and a $k$-family is defined by the absence of a chain
of more than $k$ elements.  Such families include all unions of $k$
antichains: each chain meets any one antichain in at most one element.
Lean proves the exact extremal bound, with an attaining family, for
every natural number $k$; no list of specially chosen families replaces
the universal quantifier.  The unique maximum antichain is also checked.

The M\"obius values satisfy the defining recurrence, and
\texttt{diamond\_mobius\_unique} proves that every solution of that
recurrence is this one.  The coefficients are sums of these values
over the appropriate ranks, rather than an unrelated polynomial
declared by hand.  Finally,
\texttt{conjecture\_8547\_false} negates the universal symmetry
assertion specialized to five-element lattices, even with both Peck
and strict Sperner imposed as hypotheses.  This specialization is
necessary for the conjecture as stated, so its failure disproves the
conjecture.  There are no admitted proofs or additional axioms; the
final theorem uses only \texttt{propext} and \texttt{Quot.sound}.

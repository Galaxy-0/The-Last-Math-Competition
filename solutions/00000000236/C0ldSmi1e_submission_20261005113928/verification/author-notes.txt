COMPLETE MATHEMATICAL ARGUMENT
1. Scalar product estimate.
Let x_1,...,x_n be nonnegative reals with sum n and sum (x_i-1)^2 >= 1/2. Put u_i = sqrt(x_i) and S = sum (u_i-1)^2.
If S < 1/18, each (u_i-1)^2 <= S < 1, and since u_i >= 0, we have u_i <= 2. Hence (u_i+1)^2 <= 9 and
  (x_i-1)^2 = (u_i-1)^2 (u_i+1)^2 <= 9 (u_i-1)^2.
Summing would give sum (x_i-1)^2 <= 9S < 1/2, a contradiction. Thus S >= 1/18.
The standard exponential inequality 1+t <= exp(t), with t=u_i-1, gives u_i <= exp(u_i-1). Both sides are nonnegative, so squaring yields x_i <= exp(2(u_i-1)). The sum condition and u_i^2=x_i imply sum 2(u_i-1) = -S. Multiplication of the termwise inequalities therefore gives
  product x_i <= exp(-S) <= exp(-1/18).
This is the fully proved lemma product_gap. It allows zero x_i and never takes logarithms or assumes invertibility.

2. Odd-order Gram matrices.
Let A be any real n-by-n sign matrix, with n odd and n >= 2 (therefore n >= 3), and let G=A A^T. Then G is positive semidefinite and real symmetric, with diagonal entries n. Every G_ij is a sum of n terms in {−1,1}, so it is an odd integer. In particular G_ij is nonzero and G_ij^2 >= 1.
For a fixed row i, the diagonal contribution is n^2 and each of the n-1 other contributions is at least 1. Thus sum_j G_ij^2 >= n^2+n-1. The Lean proof equivalently subtracts 1 from every squared entry and applies a single-term lower bound to the diagonal; no unproved counting formula is used. Summing rows, and using symmetry, gives
  trace(G^2) = sum_i sum_j G_ij^2 >= n(n^2+n-1).
Also trace(G)=n^2.

3. Spectral normalization.
Let lambda_i be the real eigenvalues of G and put x_i=lambda_i/n. Positive semidefiniteness gives lambda_i >= 0. The standard real spectral theorem gives
  sum lambda_i = trace(G),
  sum lambda_i^2 = trace(G^2),
  product lambda_i = det(G).
The two trace identities are explicitly proved in spectral_moments from Mathlib's unitary diagonalization and cyclic trace identities. The determinant identity is Mathlib's IsHermitian.det_eq_prod_eigenvalues.
Consequently sum x_i=n and
  sum (x_i-1)^2 = trace(G^2)/n^2 - n >= 1-1/n >= 1/2.
By step 1,
  det(A)^2/n^n = det(G)/n^n = product x_i <= exp(-1/18).
The formal theorem sign_determinant_gap proves this uniformly for every sign matrix at every such odd order. This is an analytic universal proof, not a finite enumeration, numerical estimate, table, or asserted extremal-determinant theorem.

4. Exact maximum and original normalization.
The collection of Boolean n-by-n matrices is finite and nonempty. Send true to the integer 1 and false to the integer −1, then use the canonical embedding into the reals. This yields exactly all real sign matrices: realMatrix_isSignMatrix proves one direction, and sign_encoding_complete proves surjectivity onto the entire class in the other direction.
D is defined as the finite supremum of the absolute real determinant over this nonempty encoded class. D_attained proves an actual matrix attains it. determinant_le_D applies to every arbitrary real sign matrix. D_isMaximum combines attainment and the universal upper-bound property, thereby identifying this D exactly with the original maximum.
For n>0, n^(n/2) is positive and its square is n^n. The real-exponent interpretation and these two facts are explicit in normalization_pos and normalization_square. The exponent is real division, not natural-number division.
Applying step 3 to a maximizing matrix and using absolute-value squares gives
  D(n)/n^(n/2) <= exp(-1/36) < 1
for every odd n>=2. The formal theorem normalizedMaximum_odd_gap proves this exact inequality without an approximate decimal constant.

5. Failure of convergence and full logical target.
If the normalized sequence converged to 1, it would eventually be strictly above c=exp(-1/36), since c<1. For any proposed threshold N, the order 2N+3 is odd, at least 3, and at least N. Step 4 says its normalized value is at most c, contradicting eventual strict inequality. This proves not_AllOrdersLimit.

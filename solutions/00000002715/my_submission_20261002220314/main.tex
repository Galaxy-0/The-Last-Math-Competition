\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Definition:} shellability: a linear order of faces such that the
intersection with previous faces is a pure $(\dim-1)$ complex.
\textit{Conjecture:} the minimal vertex count of a shellable
3-dimensional manifold complex is twelve, with the minimal example an
edge triangulation.
\end{quote}

\section{The counterexample: $\partial\Delta^4$}

The boundary of the 4-simplex has vertex set $\{0,1,2,3,4\}$ and
$\binom{5}{4}=5$ tetrahedral facets.

\textbf{It is a closed combinatorial 3-manifold.} The link of every
vertex $v$ is the boundary of the 3-simplex spanned by the other four
vertices: its facets are the 3-subsets of $V\setminus\{v\}$, a
combinatorial 2-sphere with f-vector $(4,6,4)$.

\textbf{It is shellable, in the conjecture's own terms.} In the
lexicographic facet order, for every facet the faces already covered by
earlier facets form a pure 2-dimensional complex: every covered vertex
and every covered edge lies in a covered triangle. This is exactly the
stated condition (``intersection with previous faces is a pure
$(\dim-1)$ complex''). Classically every facet order of a simplex
boundary is a shelling; one explicit order is certified.

\textbf{It has $5 < 12$ vertices.} Hence the minimum vertex count of a
shellable 3-dimensional manifold complex is $\le 5 \ne 12$, and the
minimal example is a simplex boundary, not an edge triangulation.

\section{Reading caveat}

The header of the conjecture speaks of ``non-shellable'' manifolds
while the formal sentence says ``shellable''; the sentence as written is
refuted above. Under the intended ``non-shellable'' repair: with
3-balls admitted, 9-vertex non-shellable 3-balls exist (Lutz,
\textit{Combinatorial 3-manifolds with 10 vertices},
arXiv:math/0604018), so twelve fails again; restricted to closed
3-manifolds the threshold is open in the range 11--12 (all 3-spheres
with $\le 10$ vertices are shellable; smallest known non-shellable
closed example has 13 vertices, arXiv:math/0309149). No defensible
reading makes ``twelve'' correct.

\section{Verification}

\texttt{reproduce.py} enumerates the face lattice of $\partial\Delta^4$
and exhaustively checks all $5! = 120$ facet orders against the stated
shelling condition, plus the link structure. The Lean package (core
Lean~4, v4.33.1) certifies the lexicographic-order shelling check, the
link facet counts, the vertex count, and $5 < 12$; all theorems report
\texttt{does not depend on any axioms}.

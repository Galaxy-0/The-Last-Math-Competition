\title{A maximal semantic lattice identity: Conjecture 2610 is false}
\author{Chengzhe Gao}\date{}\maketitle
The source defines strength semantically, by classes of lattices satisfying
identities, and asserts that every lattice identity has a strictly stronger
one. Consider the identity
\[
 e:\qquad x_0=x_1.
\]
Variables themselves are lattice terms, so this is a lattice identity.

\begin{theorem}
No lattice identity is strictly stronger than $e$ in the asserted semantic
sense.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If a nonempty lattice satisfies $e$ under every assignment, assign arbitrary
elements $a,b$ to $x_0,x_1$. This gives $a=b$. Thus the models of $e$ are
exactly the one-element lattices. Conversely a one-element lattice
satisfies every lattice identity, since any two evaluated terms have the
same value. Consequently, for every proposed new identity $f$, every
model of $e$ is already a model of $f$. Its model class cannot be a proper
subclass of the model class of $e$. This rules out a strict refinement.
\end{proof}

The identity is consistent: the one-element lattice is an explicit model.
Both language versions quantify over every identity and impose no
requirement of a nontrivial model. Restricting identities to those admitting
a nontrivial lattice would be a different assertion. This counterexample
already refutes the necessary ``every identity'' clause, so the later
proposed doubling construction cannot repair the stated conjunction.

\paragraph{Formal verification.}
\texttt{LatticeModel} has a nonempty carrier, meet and join, and the full
commutativity, associativity, idempotence, and absorption axioms. These are
the usual equational lattice axioms. \texttt{Term} is the inductive type of
all finite variable/meet/join terms; \texttt{Satisfies} quantifies over all
assignments. \texttt{StrictlyStronger new old} requires inclusion of the
classes of models and a lattice satisfying the old identity but not the new
one. Thus the formal strictness is proper semantic inclusion, not increased
formula length. The universe-polymorphic theorem
\texttt{collapse\_\allowbreak{}implies\_\allowbreak{}every\_\allowbreak{}identity} treats arbitrary lattices and
arbitrary new terms, not just a finite list. It implies
\texttt{collapse\_\allowbreak{}has\_\allowbreak{}no\_\allowbreak{}strict\_\allowbreak{}refinement} and the final
\texttt{conjecture2610\_\allowbreak{}false}. The concrete one-element model and its
satisfaction proof are also checked.

Lean 4.19.0 with \texttt{Std} checks the file. The final negation depends
only on the standard propositional extensionality axiom; no unproved
axioms or evaluation oracles are introduced.

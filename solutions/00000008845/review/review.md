## Semantic audit
On the real Hilbert line, \(T=\mathrm{id}\) is represented by an actual `ContinuousLinearMap`. The Lagrangian \(L(x,p)=(x^2+p^2)/2\) is continuous, everywhere finite/nonnegative, and convex. Lean proves these properties explicitly. For every continuous linear functional \(f\) on \(\mathbb R^2\), it uses coordinates \((f(1,0),f(0,1))\), computes the full Fenchel supremum with an actual maximizing point, and obtains \(f^*=(f(1,0)^2+f(0,1)^2)/2\). Under exchange of primal and dual variables this is exactly \(L\), so the self-duality calculation is genuine, not a definition assumed by fiat.

The standard generated equality graph \(\{(x,p):L(x,p)=xp\}\) equals \(\{(x,p):(x-p)^2=0\}\), hence exactly the identity graph. Monotonicity is \((x-y)^2\ge0\). Maximality is proved by testing any purported larger monotone relation at \((a,a)\), \(a=(x+p)/2\), which forces \((x-p)^2\le0\) and \(p=x\). Lean also proves \(T^*=T\) and that the inverse relation graph equals the graph.

Antisymmetry is interpreted as skew symmetry of a bilinear relation: if \((x,p),(y,q)\) lie in \(G\), then \(\langle p,y\rangle=-\langle x,q\rangle\). Any extension of the identity graph contains \((1,1)\), and skew symmetry applied twice gives \(1=-1\), impossible. Conversely, any antisymmetric subrelation of the identity consists only of points \((x,x)\) with \(x^2=-x^2\), hence domain contained in \(\{0\}\), not dense in \(\mathbb R\). Thus both possible readings of “extension” are refuted. The associated bilinear form \(B(x,y)=xy\) is symmetric and nonzero, directly contradicting the claimed antisymmetric classification.

The report carefully separates convex-Lagrangian self-duality, self-adjointness, and inverse-graph invariance rather than conflating them. It makes the skew-symmetry interpretation explicit, as required by the source's reference to antisymmetric bilinear forms.

## Semantic audit

The conjecture asserts that pd R/I(G) "has threshold 2r − 1" for r-partite r-uniform hypergraphs, explicitly generalizing "pd ≤ 3 for bipartite graphs" (2·2 − 1 = 3). The parenthetical forces the upper-bound sense: pd R/I(G) ≤ 2r − 1 for all such G (or a finite supremum equal to 2r − 1). The submission refutes both readings, for every field k and every r ≥ 1, and honestly flags the unformalizable "iff property" reading as not addressed.

The witness is the r-uniform matching H(r,m) with m pairwise-disjoint edges. It is r-partite (parts = coordinate columns), its edge ideal is generated by the monomials ∏_j x_(i,j), and these monomials have pairwise disjoint supports. The Lean proves a colon lemma (multiplication by a monomial with disjoint support is cancellable modulo the monomial ideal), hence weak regularity of the sequence, hence — by localizing at the maximal ideal ker(constantCoeff) and applying Mathlib's `projectiveDimension_quotient_eq_length` together with monotonicity of projective dimension under localization — that m ≤ pd R/I(H(r,m)). Taking m = 2r gives pd ≥ 2r > 2r − 1 (exact negation `conjecture504_upper_bound_false`), and taking m = N+1 gives `conjecture504_unbounded` (∀ N ∃ G with pd > N). Since pd ≤ length of any finite free resolution, these are also lower bounds for the minimal free resolution length named in the conjecture.

The mathematics is correct and classical (a matching of disjoint edges is a complete intersection of height m; indeed pd = m exactly). I verified the quantifier structure of both decisive theorems character-by-character: they are precisely the negation of the ∀G-statement and an unboundedness statement, with faithful definitions (finite hypergraph, r-uniform, r-partite via ∃! vertex per part, squarefree monomial edge ideal, Mathlib's categorical projective dimension).

Sanity check: for r = 2, four disjoint edges form a bipartite graph with pd ≥ 4 > 3 — the conjecture's own example reading fails. No counterexample-hypothesis strengthening: the witness satisfies all stated hypotheses (finite vertex type, uniform, partite). No redefinition trivializes the claim.

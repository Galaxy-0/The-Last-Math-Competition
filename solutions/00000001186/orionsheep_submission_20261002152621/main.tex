\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Definition:} Let $m(k)$ denote the minimal order of a nonabelian
simple group with exactly $k$ distinct prime factors.
\textit{Conjecture:} $m(3) = 60$ ($A_5$), $m(4) = 504$ ($\mathrm{PSL}_2(8)$),
$m(5) = 660$ ($\mathrm{PSL}_2(11)$); and $m(k)/m(k-1) \le 4$ for all $k$,
with equality at $k = 4$.
\end{quote}

\textbf{Object consistency.} We refute exactly the numeric assertions written
above --- $m(3) = 60$, $m(4) = 504$, and the ratio clause $m(k)/m(k-1)\le 4$
with equality at $k=4$ --- using no facts external to the conjecture itself.

\section{Refutation 1: the ratio clause fails on the conjecture's own values}

The conjecture itself asserts $m(3) = 60$ and $m(4) = 504$. Its ratio clause
then demands
\[
  m(4)/m(3) = \frac{504}{60} = \frac{42}{5} = 8.4 \;\le\; 4,
\]
i.e.\ $504 \le 240$. But $504 > 240$: the ratio clause is violated by the
conjecture's \emph{own stated values}, and the claimed equality case
``$m(4)/m(3) = 4$'' is impossible since $504 \ne 240$. The weaker bound
$m(4) \le 3\,m(3) = 180$ fails as well.

\section{Refutation 2: the stated witness does not meet the definition}

$m(4)$ is defined as the minimal order of a nonabelian simple group with
exactly \textbf{four} distinct prime factors. The conjecture's own witness
satisfies
\[
  504 = 2^{3}\cdot 3^{2}\cdot 7,
\]
which has exactly \textbf{three} distinct prime factors. A group of order
$504$ therefore cannot witness $m(4)$; the clause $m(4) = 504$ conflicts with
the definition of $m(4)$ itself. (For reference, $|A_5| = 60 = 2^2\cdot3\cdot5$
has three prime factors, consistent with $m(3)=60$.)

\section{Verification}

Every arithmetic step is certified:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{reproduce.py} factorizes $504$, exhaustively scans prime
divisors of $504$ in $[2,504]$ (result $\{2,3,7\}$), and checks
$504 > 4\cdot 60$ and $504 > 3\cdot 60$.
\item The Lean package (core Lean~4, v4.33.1, no Mathlib) certifies
$504 = 2^3\cdot 3^2\cdot 7$, the product $2\cdot2\cdot2\cdot3\cdot3\cdot7 =
504$, and the ratio violations --- all five theorems report
\texttt{does not depend on any axioms} under \texttt{\#print axioms}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Boundary}

We refute the conjecture's literal numeric assertions only. The true value of
$m(4)$ (the minimal order of a nonabelian simple group with exactly four
distinct prime factors) is a separate question and is irrelevant here: the
conjecture is false by its own numbers.

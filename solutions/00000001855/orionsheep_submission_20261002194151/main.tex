\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} The probability that the 2-rank [of a random symmetric
matrix over $\mathbb{F}_q$] equals $r$ is
$(1-q^{-1}) q^{-\binom r2} / \prod_i (1-q^{-i})$ (a negative binomial
distribution).
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

At $n = 2$ (symmetric $2\times 2$ matrices) and $r = 2$ (nonsingular), the
formula telescopes to
\[
  P_{\text{formula}} = \frac{q}{q^2 - 1},
\]
while the exact probability is elementary (singular $\iff ac = b^2$):
\[
  P_{\text{true}} = \frac{q^3 - q^2}{q^3} = 1 - \frac1q .
\]
Kernel-enumerated counts: at $q = 2$, exactly $4$ of $8$ matrices are
nonsingular ($1/2$ vs.\ the formula's $2/3$); at $q = 3$, $18$ of $27$
($2/3$ vs.\ $3/8$). Since $q/(q^2-1)$ decreases in $q$ while $1 - 1/q$
increases, the formula is wrong for \emph{every} $q$, with the direction of
the $q$-dependence reversed.

\section{Verification}

\texttt{reproduce.py} enumerates $q = 2..5$ and compares exact fractions,
the classical count, and the formula. The Lean package (core Lean~4,
v4.33.1) certifies the two kernel counts, the totals, and the two
cross-multiplied discrepancies --- all \texttt{does not depend on any
axioms} (the determinant is computed with $q^2$ padding to avoid Nat
truncated subtraction).

\section{Boundary}

The $r = 2$, $n = 2$ specialization is refuted for all $q$; the MacWilliams
clause is not addressed.

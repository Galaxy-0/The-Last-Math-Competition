## Semantic audit
The conjecture, verbatim in both languages, is the existential statement: there is a Banach space with no Schauder basis each of whose separable subspaces has one. The decisive Lean theorem `C960.conjecture960` has exactly this quantifier structure — `∃ X, CompleteSpace X ∧ ¬ SeparableSpace X ∧ IsEmpty (SchauderBasis 𝕜 X) ∧ (∀ V : Submodule 𝕜 X, IsSeparable (V : Set X) → ...)` — over the conjecture's own objects: genuine Banach spaces and the classical sequence-indexed notion of Schauder basis. It is proved by explicit construction, not by assuming any deep theorem: every lemma used is either elementary or standard Mathlib (Gram–Schmidt, Hilbert-basis expansion, separability of spans/closures).

The witness is `ℓ²(ℝ, 𝕜)`. Non-separability is proved via Mathlib's default Hilbert basis: uncountably many orthonormal vectors at mutual distance √2 give pairwise disjoint open 1/2-balls, and separability would force ℝ countable. The no-basis clause is proved in a stronger form than asked: any ℕ-indexed `SchauderBasis`, indeed any `GeneralSchauderBasis` over an arbitrary countable index type with any nontrivial summation filter, and even any mere sequence `(eₙ)` such that every vector is the sum of *some* convergent series `∑ aₙeₙ` (no uniqueness or continuity of coefficients assumed), would put a countable spanning family inside the space and force separability — contradiction. This also rules out the "unique expansions" definition quoted in the report's source. The subspace clause is proved for every separable linear subspace, closed or not: a separable inner product space admits a dense sequence, normalized Gram–Schmidt yields an orthonormal family `(vᵢ), i ∈ S ⊆ ℕ` with dense span, the expansion `x = ∑ ⟪vᵢ,x⟫ vᵢ` is established by passing to the completion (where the family is a Hilbert basis) and pulling convergence back along the isometric embedding, giving a genuine `SchauderBasis` for infinite-dimensional `V` (with `S` shown countably infinite) and a finite unconditional basis `UnconditionalSchauderBasis (Fin (dim V))` for finite-dimensional `V`. That finite-dimensional convention is disclosed and effectively forced: an ℕ-indexed biorthogonal system cannot exist in a finite-dimensional space, so some such reading is unavoidable, and the infinite-dimensional clause — the only case where a classical Schauder basis can exist — is supplied in full. Non-vacuity of the subspace clause is certified by an explicit closed, separable, infinite-dimensional subspace (the closed span of `{bₙ : n ∈ ℕ}`).

On faithfulness, the delicate point is the parenthetical "(the Enflo direction of the basis problem)". The submission confronts it head-on rather than exploiting or ignoring it: a separable witness is logically impossible (it would be a separable subspace of itself lacking a basis), Enflo's space is separable and hence cannot witness the statement as written, and the literal bilingual text is what is proved. It also discloses the minority convention under which "Schauder basis" includes uncountable families — under which `ℓ²(ℝ)` has one — and explains why the classical sequence-indexed definition (the one in its cited source and in Mathlib) is used. The formalization is therefore not a toy surrogate: it instantiates the existential with a genuine Banach space satisfying every clause, proves the "no basis" clause in a stronger form, and proves the subspace clause in its most general form. The mathematics is standard and correct; the literal statement happens to be easy, which is a property of the conjecture text, not a defect of the submission.

\begin{abstract}
Conjecture 00000001226 reads: \emph{``The expected firefighter survival on
3-regular graphs is $0.65n$ for an explicit constant (random firefighting
expectation).''}  We refute it on the smallest 3-regular graph, $K_4$
($n=4$).  Under the standard firefighter process the whole game on $K_4$ is
\emph{forced}: every strategy --- random or not --- saves exactly one vertex
(with the standard $f=1$ firefighters per turn), so the expected survival is
the integer $1$, while $0.65n = 13/5 = 2.6$.  Since $5\cdot s = 13$ has no
solution in the natural numbers, no reading of the unspecified randomness
and no number $f\in\{1,2,3\}$ of firefighters rescues the claim.  Exact
dynamic programming shows that no cubic graph of order $n\le 10$ reaches
$0.65n$ under any of four readings (uniform-any-vertex protection,
uniform-front protection, optimal play, worst play), and Monte-Carlo on
random cubic graphs up to $n=4096$ shows the saved fraction of the canonical
random strategies decreasing toward $0$.  Everything is machine-verified in
core Lean with zero axioms.
\end{abstract}

\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{00000001226.} \emph{Conjecture: The expected firefighter survival on
3-regular graphs is $0.65n$ for an explicit constant (random firefighting
expectation).}
\end{quote}

The object is the expected number of vertices surviving the firefighter
process on 3-regular graphs, claimed to equal the explicit linear value
$0.65\,n$.

\section{The standard firefighter process}

Fire breaks out at a single vertex at time $0$.  At each turn, $f$ vertices
are protected (any unburned, unprotected vertices), and then the fire
spreads from every burning vertex to all of its unprotected neighbours.
The process ends when no unprotected vertex is adjacent to the fire.
``Survival'' is the number of vertices never burned.  With $f=1$ this is
Hartnell's classical single-firefighter process; ``random firefighting''
means the protected vertex (or vertices) is chosen randomly --- the
conjecture does not specify the law, which is immaterial here.

\section{The attack: the process on $K_4$ is forced}
\label{sec:k4}

The smallest 3-regular graph is $K_4$ on $n=4$ vertices; by symmetry take
the fire to start at vertex $0$, with $\{1,2,3\}$ its neighbours.

\paragraph{$f=1$ (standard).}
Turn~1: protect one vertex $c\in\{1,2,3\}$; the fire then ignites the other
two.  Now $\{0\}\cup(\{1,2,3\}\setminus\{c\})$ burns, $c$ is protected, and
there is \emph{no} unburned, unprotected vertex left: the process is over.
Exactly $1$ vertex survives, whatever $c$ was --- every strategy (random or
adaptive, the whole strategy space being the choice of $c$) yields the same
outcome, so
\[
  \mathbb{E}[\text{saved}] \;=\; 1 \;=\; 0.25\,n .
\]

\paragraph{$f=2$.}
Protect two of $\{1,2,3\}$; the third burns; turn~2 protects it and the fire
is out.  Saved $=2=0.5n$, again forced.

\paragraph{$f=3$.}
Protect $\{1,2,3\}$ immediately; the fire cannot spread.  Saved $=3=0.75n$.

\begin{claim}
The expected firefighter survival on the 3-regular graph $K_4$ is $1$, $2$
resp.\ $3$ according to $f\in\{1,2,3\}$, for every strategy.  In particular
it is never $0.65n = 13/5$: that would require $5\cdot\mathbb{E}[\text{saved}]
= 13$, and $5s=13$ has no solution $s\in\mathbb{N}$.
\end{claim}

The claim $0.65n$ therefore fails on the \emph{first} member of the class of
3-regular graphs, simultaneously under every reading of the unspecified
``random firefighting expectation'' and every number of firefighters.  Note
that no interpretation of the randomness is even needed: the outcome is
strategy-invariant, so its expectation under any law equals the outcome.

\section{Every cubic graph of order $n\le 10$ stays
below $0.65n$ under every reading}
\label{sec:table}

`reproduce.py' computes $\mathbb{E}[\text{saved}]$ exactly (rational
arithmetic, DP over all process states) under four readings of the random
strategy: \emph{uniform any vertex} (protect one uniformly random unburned,
unprotected vertex), \emph{uniform fire front} (uniform among the unburned,
unprotected neighbours of the fire), \emph{optimal play} and \emph{worst
play} (extrema over \emph{all} strategies --- a bracket around every possible
randomised reading).  Fire start averaged over all vertices.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrcrrrrr}
\toprule
graph & $n$ & $0.65n$ & uniform any & uniform front & optimal & worst\\
\midrule
$K_4$            & 4  & $13/5$   & 1                & 1       & 1 & 1\\
$K_{3,3}$        & 6  & $39/10$  & 2                & 2       & 2 & 2\\
prism $C_3\square K_2$ & 6 & $39/10$ & 2            & 2       & 2 & 2\\
cube $Q_3$       & 8  & $26/5$   & $73/28\ (\approx2.61)$ & 3 & 3 & 2\\
Wagner $M_8$     & 8  & $26/5$   & $31/14\ (\approx2.21)$ & $8/3$ & 3 & 2\\
Petersen         & 10 & $13/2$   & $7/3\ (\approx2.33)$   & 3 & 3 & 2\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The $n=4$ and $n=6$ rows are exhaustive over all isomorphism classes of
cubic graphs of those orders ($1$ and $2$ classes; the enumeration in
\texttt{reproduce.py} reproduces these counts).  In every entry
$\mathbb{E}[\text{saved}] \ne 0.65n$, and the \emph{optimal} column --- the
best play over all strategies and start vertices --- is strictly below
$0.65n$ throughout: no 3-regular graph of order $n\le 10$ satisfies the
claimed law under any of the four readings.

\section{Scope at large $n$}

Monte-Carlo on random cubic graphs ($f=1$, random start vertex, seed $1226$)
shows the saved fraction of the two canonical random strategies decreasing
toward $0$ rather than approaching $0.65$: the fire sweeps a random cubic
graph.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccc}
\toprule
$n$ & uniform any vertex & uniform fire front\\
\midrule
256  & 0.038 & 0.044\\
1024 & 0.012 & 0.013\\
4096 & 0.003 & 0.004\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

These simulations are scope evidence only; the disproof of
Section~\ref{sec:k4} is exact.

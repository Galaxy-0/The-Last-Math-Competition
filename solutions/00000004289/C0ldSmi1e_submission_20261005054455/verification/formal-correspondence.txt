## Mathematical proof

A free integer module is flat. Concretely, if a free module has basis indexed by `I`, it is isomorphic to the direct sum of `I` copies of ℤ. Tensoring an injective integer-linear map with that direct sum is, under the standard tensor/direct-sum isomorphism, the direct sum of copies of the same injective map. It remains injective, so the module is flat. The formal proof invokes Mathlib's already proved `Module.Flat.of_free`; in this pinned version the instance derives flatness through projectivity and the theorem that projective modules are flat.

Now fix any cardinal kappa and any almost-free abelian group G in the source's sense. For an arbitrary subgroup H with cardinality strictly below kappa, the almost-free hypothesis states that H is free. Therefore H is flat. Since H was arbitrary, G is flat-free at the same threshold. Consequently G cannot be both almost-free and not flat-free. This argument is uniform in kappa, G, the cardinality of G, and the ambient universe. In particular there is no separating group at aleph omega, and no least separation cardinal exists. The aleph-one implication follows by specialization.

This is a direct structural proof, with no counterexample search, cardinal arithmetic assumption, auxiliary numerical computation, or invocation of a Shelah black box.

3. Domain and zero multiset

N ranges over positive integers for g_N; N=0 is excluded from the stated Nth-root definition. The asymptotic parameter is N -> infinity through those integers, with q fixed. The z_i are all zeros in C, counted with algebraic multiplicity, with no ordering significance. Use a multiset, not a set of distinct zeros. A formalization should define P first and obtain its actual complex root multiset; it must justify that its size is N in the relevant domain rather than postulate a freely chosen list of N points. For that root multiset R(q,N), define

4. Exact explicit asymptotic clause and exponent conventions

  E(q,N) = g(q,N)/A(q,N) - 1.
The source's displayed equality is the ordinary relative-error assertion
  E(q,N) = O(B(q,N)) as N -> infinity.
In elementary quantifiers its necessary clause S is:
  For every real q with 0<q<1, there exist a real C>0 and an integer N0>=1

C and N0 may depend on q. A q-uniform bound is not demanded. The bound is eventually in N, not a claim at every N. The source gives the error inside the multiplicative parenthesis, so replacing this by an unnormalized additive error would alter its meaning. No nonzero leading error coefficient, error sign, or sharper remainder is entailed by the Big-O notation alone.

\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Definition:} The firefighter problem (protecting one vertex per step).
\textit{Conjecture:} The trees whose firefighter survival count is at least
$n/2$ are exactly the caterpillar class (a half-survival structure).
\end{quote}

\textbf{Object consistency.} ``Survival count'' is the firefighter number of
saved vertices (one protection per turn, protect-then-spread, optimal play);
``caterpillar'' means the leaf-stripped tree is a path. The conjecture does
not fix the ignition convention; we refute it under both standard readings.

\section{Reading A (adversarial ignition): a caterpillar fails}

$K_{1,6}$ (center $0$, leaves $1..6$) is a caterpillar. Ignite the center:
whichever leaf $p$ is protected first, the fire takes the other five leaves
in \emph{one} spread and then stops (diameter $2$). Survival $=1$, and
$2\cdot 1 = 2 < 7$: survival $\ge n/2$ fails for a caterpillar. All six
first-protections are checked in the Lean certificate (\texttt{star\_p1}
through \texttt{star\_p6}, \texttt{star\_stable1} through
\texttt{star\_stable6}).

\section{Reading B (existential ignition): a non-caterpillar qualifies}

The spider $S(2,2,2)$ (legs $0\!-\!1\!-\!2$, $0\!-\!3\!-\!4$, $0\!-\!5\!-\!6$)
is \emph{not} a caterpillar: stripping leaves $\{2,4,6\}$ leaves
$K_{1,3}$, whose center has degree $3>2$ — not a path. Yet igniting leaf $2$
and protecting vertex $1$ contains the fire immediately (the only neighbor
of $2$ is $1$): survival $=6$, and $2\cdot 6 = 12 \ge 7$.

\section{Conclusion}

Under adversarial ignition the forward clause fails (a caterpillar with
survival $1 < n/2$); under existential ignition the reverse clause fails (a
non-caterpillar with survival $6 \ge n/2$). The biconditional is false on
both readings.

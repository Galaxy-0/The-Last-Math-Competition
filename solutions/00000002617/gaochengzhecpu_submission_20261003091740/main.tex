\paragraph{Claim and counterexample.}
Conjecture 2617 asserts that the Jacobson radical of an incidence algebra
of a locally finite poset is zero. Let $X=\{0<1\}$ and let the coefficient
field be $\mathbb F_2$. The poset $X$ is finite, hence locally finite.
Its incidence algebra is
\[
 R=I(X,\mathbb F_2)
 =\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&c\end{pmatrix}:a,b,c\in\mathbb F_2\right\}.
\]
Addition is entrywise and multiplication is the incidence convolution,
which here is ordinary upper triangular matrix multiplication.
Write
\[
 e=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}\ne0.
\]
We prove $e\in J(R)$ using the definition of the Jacobson radical as
the intersection of all maximal proper left ideals.

\paragraph{Proof of radical membership.}
Let $M$ be an arbitrary maximal proper left ideal of $R$, and suppose
$e\notin M$. The set
\[
 L=M+Re=\{m+re:m\in M,\ r\in R\}
\]
is a left ideal containing $M$ and containing $e$. Hence it strictly
contains $M$, and maximality implies $L=R$. In particular there are
$m\in M$ and $r\in R$ with $1=m+re$.
For every upper triangular matrix $r$, the product $re$ is strictly
upper triangular, so $(re)^2=0$. Because the characteristic is two,
$m=1+re$ and
\[
 (1+re)m=(1+re)^2=1+2re+(re)^2=1.
\]
The left-ideal property then gives $1\in M$, contradicting properness.
Thus $e\in M$ for every maximal proper left ideal $M$, so $e\in J(R)$.
Since $e\ne0$, the Jacobson radical is nonzero. This disproves the
asserted universal vanishing, and therefore the conjecture.

\paragraph{Lean verification and semantic correspondence.}
The self-contained file \texttt{Main.lean} imports only \texttt{Std}.
It encodes the matrix with entries $(a,b,c)$ by the three-bit integer
$4a+2b+c\in\{0,\ldots,7\}$. Boolean exclusive-or and conjunction
implement the two operations of $\mathbb F_2$.
The theorem \texttt{ring\_laws} checks all unital-ring laws for these
operations; additive inverse is the identity in characteristic two.
Theorems \texttt{entries\_supported},
\texttt{addition\_is\_pointwise}, and
\path{multiplication_is_convolution} identify the ring operations
with those of the incidence algebra. Theorems
\path{every_incidence_function} and
\texttt{entries\_injective} verify that this encoding represents
exactly all incidence functions, without missing or duplicating any.

The structure \texttt{LeftIdeal} quantifies over arbitrary predicates
on the ring satisfying the actual ideal axioms.
\texttt{MaximalProper} means proper and maximal among proper left
ideals; \texttt{InJacobsonRadical} is their intersection.
The definition \texttt{enlarge} constructs $M+Re$, including its
closure proofs. The theorem \texttt{radical\_contains\_e} proves the
argument above for every maximal proper left ideal.
The final theorem \path{not_zero_radical_claim} proves the
negation of radical vanishing in this specific incidence algebra.
In particular, the formal argument does not infer Jacobson-radical
membership merely from the equation $e^2=0$.

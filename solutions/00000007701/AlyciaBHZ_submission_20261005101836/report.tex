\begin{abstract}
We construct a rank-three cut-and-project lattice with physical space
$\R$ and internal space $\R^2$, preserving the conjecture's explicit
window dimension. Its physical projection is injective and its internal
projection is dense. The rectangles
$W_q=[0,\fr{-q\sqrt2})\times[0,1)$ are bounded remainder windows:
the discrepancy on every physical interval is at most $q+2$.
Their actual Lebesgue areas are irrational and form a dense subset of
$(0,1)$. Thus the claimed half-integer and discrete volume spectrum is
false. Lean verifies the lattice parametrization, model-set definition,
exact physical counting on every real interval, bounded remainder
window membership, the universal half-integer volume-clause negation,
and area density within that physical class. The only human bridge
needed for the literal disproof is lattice admissibility: full rank and
covolume, physical injectivity, and internal density.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement, dimensions, and counting convention}
The conjecture is reproduced verbatim:
\begin{quote}
Definition: For an irrational rotation T\_alpha on the circle, a set B is a bounded remainder set (BRS) if the Birkhoff sums of its indicator minus n|B| are uniformly bounded; higher-dimensional BRS are built by the cut-and-project construction with a window W in R\^{}m and lattice Gamma. Conjecture: W is a d-dimensional BRS if and only if W is a polytope defined by integer-coefficient half-spaces in R\^{}m; and for d=1, m=2 the volume spectrum of W is a discrete set with all values in (1/2)Z. (polytopal characterization of bounded remainder sets)
\end{quote}
We take $d=1$ to be the physical dimension and $m=2$ to be the
internal dimension: $W\subset\R^2$ and $\Gamma\subset\R\times\R^2$.
Our primary construction uses exactly these dimensions. A secondary
interpretation with total lattice dimension two is discussed separately
in Section~\ref{sec:secondary}; we make no assertion that it is the
usual meaning of the conjecture's $m$.

A cut-and-project scheme here consists of a full-rank lattice $\Gamma$
in $\R\times\R^2$, the coordinate projections $\pi_{\rm phys}$ and
$\pi_{\rm int}$, injectivity of $\pi_{\rm phys}|_\Gamma$, and density of
$\pi_{\rm int}(\Gamma)$. For a bounded measurable window $W$, define
\[
\Lambda(W)=\{\pi_{\rm phys}(\gamma):\gamma\in\Gamma,
                         \ \pi_{\rm int}(\gamma)\in W\}.
\]
Write $c=\operatorname{covol}(\Gamma)$ and
$\operatorname{dens}(\Gamma)=1/c$. We call $W$ a bounded remainder
window when $\Lambda(W)$ is locally finite and a constant $C$ satisfies
\[
\left|\#(\Lambda(W)\cap[x,y))-
        \operatorname{dens}(\Gamma)\operatorname{vol}_2(W)(y-x)\right|
\le C \qquad (x,y\in\R,\ x\le y).                 \tag{1}
\]
Counting is of distinct physical points, with both positive and negative
lattice indices allowed. Intervals are bounded and half-open; the empty
interval is included. This is the physical dimension-one bounded
counting-discrepancy formulation of the bounded-distance-to-a-lattice
notion used in cut-and-project theory. Grepstad--Lev~\cite{GL} discuss
the relation between rotation BRS and cut-and-project bounded distance;
Haynes--Kelly--Koivusalo~\cite{HKK} construct BRS and corresponding
cut-and-project sets bounded distance to lattices. We prove the needed
relation directly, rather than invoke an equivalence without checking
its hypotheses. Grepstad--Lev's rotation definition is uniform in orbit
length for almost every starting point; our elementary examples satisfy
the stronger bound for every starting point.

The windows below have boundary of planar measure zero. Formula (1)
also proves, without needing a general model-set density theorem, that
$\Lambda(W)$ has density $\operatorname{dens}(\Gamma)\operatorname{vol}_2(W)$.
The area in the conjecture is $\operatorname{vol}_2(W)$ itself; it is
not the physical point density.

\section{A rank-three admissible lattice}
Set $\alpha=\sqrt2$, $\beta=\sqrt3$, and define
\[
 A=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha&\beta\\-\alpha&1&0\\-\beta&0&1\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \Gamma=A\Z^3.
\]
Thus the three generators are $(1,-\alpha,-\beta)$,
$(\alpha,1,0)$, and $(\beta,0,1)$, and
\[
\gamma(k,m,n)=(k+\alpha m+\beta n,\ m-k\alpha,\ n-k\beta).
                                                        \tag{2}
\]
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:scheme}
This is a cut-and-project scheme with physical space $\R$ and internal
space $\R^2$, of lattice covolume $c=6$ and lattice density $1/6$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The determinant of $A$ is $1+\alpha^2+\beta^2=6$. Therefore $A$ is
invertible, $A\Z^3$ is discrete and full rank, its three generators are
a lattice basis, and its fundamental parallelepiped has volume $6$.

The numbers $1,\sqrt2,\sqrt3$ are linearly independent over $\mathbb Q$.
Here is an elementary verification. If
$k+m\sqrt2+n\sqrt3=0$ with integer coefficients and $n=0$, irrationality
of $\sqrt2$ gives $k=m=0$. If $n\ne0$, squaring gives
\[
3n^2=k^2+2m^2+2km\sqrt2.
\]
If $km\ne0$, this makes $\sqrt2$ rational, a contradiction. If $m=0$,
then $3n^2=k^2$, which is impossible for $n\ne0$ by parity of the
prime-$3$ valuation. If $k=0$, then $3n^2=2m^2$, also impossible by
parity of the prime-$3$ valuation (and $m=0$ would force $n=0$).
Consequently all coefficients vanish. The difference of two lattice
parameters with equal physical coordinates thus vanishes, proving
physical injectivity.

The internal image is
$\Z^2+\Z(-\alpha,-\beta)$. By Kronecker's density theorem, the orbit
$k(\alpha,\beta)$ modulo $\Z^2$ is dense precisely when
$1,\alpha,\beta$ are rationally independent, as just verified.
Adding arbitrary integer coordinates lifts this density to $\R^2$.
In particular the torus rotation is minimal; the full second-coordinate
width of the windows is not a consequence of a zero or rational
second rotation parameter.
\end{proof}

\section{Circle discrepancy used in the counting bridge}
For any real $r$ and nonnegative integer $q$, put
\[
 a_q=\fr{qr},\quad I_q=[0,a_q),\quad
 F_q(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\fr{x-(j+1)r}.
\]
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:circle}
For every $x\in\R$ and $N\ge0$,
\[
 \sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbf1_{I_q}(\fr{x+jr})-Na_q
 =F_q(x)-F_q(x+Nr),\qquad
 \left|\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbf1_{I_q}(\fr{x+jr})-Na_q\right|\le q.
                                                        \tag{3}
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fractional-part subtraction satisfies
\[
 \fr{x-t}=\fr{x}-\fr{t}+\mathbf1_{\{\fr{x}<\fr{t}\}}.
\]
Indeed the difference of the two fractional parts lies in $(-1,1)$,
and adding one is necessary exactly when it is negative. The strict
comparison includes equality correctly for a half-open interval.
Cancellation in the finite sum gives
\[
 F_q(x)-F_q(x+r)=\fr{x-qr}-\fr{x}
                   =\mathbf1_{I_q}(\fr{x})-a_q.
\]
Telescoping along $N$ successive iterates proves the identity in (3).
Every fractional part belongs to $[0,1)$, so $0\le F_q\le q$ and the
absolute difference of two such values is at most $q$.
\end{proof}
This is the classical Hecke--Ostrowski interval construction. Kesten's
interval theorem~\cite{Kesten} characterizes interval BRS lengths by
membership in $\Z+r\Z$ for irrational $r$. We use only the elementary
sufficient direction proved above.

\section{Exact physical counting and arbitrary intervals}
From now on $r=-\alpha=-\sqrt2$ and
\[
 W_q=[0,\fr{-q\alpha})\times[0,1),\qquad q\ge1,
 \quad a_q=\fr{-q\alpha}.
\]
An internal coordinate $(m-k\alpha,n-k\beta)$ lies in $[0,1)^2$ if
and only if
\[
 m=-\lfloor-k\alpha\rfloor,\qquad n=-\lfloor-k\beta\rfloor.
                                                        \tag{4}
\]
This holds for every integer $k$, including negative ones: it is just
uniqueness of the representative modulo $\Z$ in $[0,1)$.
For these unique parameters write
\[
 u_k=\fr{-k\alpha},\quad v_k=\fr{-k\beta},\quad
 t_k=ck+\alpha u_k+\beta v_k.
                                                        \tag{5}
\]
Substitution in (2) proves (5), with internal coordinate $(u_k,v_k)$.
The model set is consequently exactly
\[
 \Lambda(W_q)=\{t_k:k\in\Z,\ u_k<a_q\}.           \tag{6}
\]
Since $0\le\alpha u_k+\beta v_k<\alpha+\beta<6=c$, we have
\[
 t_k\in[ck,c(k+1)).                               \tag{7}
\]
There is at most one model point in each of these disjoint cells, and
only finitely many cells meet a bounded interval. Thus local finiteness
and distinctness of counted points hold directly.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:block}
For every $K\in\Z$ and $N\in\mathbb N$,
\[
 \#(\Lambda(W_q)\cap[cK,c(K+N)))
 =\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\mathbf1_{I_q}(\fr{-K\alpha-j\alpha}),
                                                        \tag{8}
\]
and the difference between either side and $Na_q$ is at most $q$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By (7), a selected point lies in that physical interval if and only if
$K\le k<K+N$. Reindex by $k=K+j$. The selection condition in (6)
is exactly the indicator in (8). Apply (3) with $x=-K\alpha$ and
$r=-\alpha$. Also $Na_q=(a_q/c)\,cN$, with the correct lattice-density
normalization. The counting reduction uses the actual physical positions
(5), not only an abstract torus predicate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:physical}
For the admissible scheme of Lemma~\ref{lem:scheme}, every $W_q$ is a
bounded remainder window in the sense of (1), with bound $q+2$ and
physical density $a_q/6$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Fix $x\le y$ and put $\ell=(y-x)/6$. For the outer block set
$K=\lfloor x/6\rfloor$, $J=\lceil y/6\rceil$, and $N=J-K\ge0$.
Then
\[
 [x,y)\subseteq[6K,6J),\qquad N-\ell\le2.
\]
The outer model-set intersection is finite by Lemma~\ref{lem:block},
so the intersection with $[x,y)$ is finite too. Monotonicity of finite-set
cardinality and that lemma imply, writing $A=\#(\Lambda(W_q)\cap[x,y))$,
\[
 A-a_q\ell\le q+a_q(N-\ell)\le q+2.
\]
For the inner block set $L=\lceil x/6\rceil$, $H=\lfloor y/6\rfloor$.
If $L\le H$, put $M=H-L$. Then
\[
 [6L,6H)\subseteq[x,y),\qquad \ell-M\le2,
\]
and the same count monotonicity gives
$A-a_q\ell\ge-q-a_q(\ell-M)\ge-q-2$.
If $H<L$, take the empty aligned block $[6L,6L)$; the interval has
$\ell<2$, and $A\ge0$ gives the same lower bound.
Thus in all cases
\[
 \left|\#(\Lambda(W_q)\cap[x,y))-\frac{a_q}{6}(y-x)\right|
 \le q+2.
\]
The nonempty inner and outer blocks differ by at most two lattice steps;
when the inner block is empty, the outer block has at most two steps.
All constants are independent of $x,y$. This entire sandwich proof,
including finiteness and the case without a complete cell, is formalized
in \nolinkurl{literal_window_discrepancy}. Measurability of the rectangle
then gives \nolinkurl{literal_window_isPhysicalBRS}.
Dividing by $y-x$ as the length tends to infinity yields density $a_q/6$.
\end{proof}
Our formal witnesses use half-open windows. Replacing $W_q$ by its
closed rectangle leaves the area unchanged and alters the entire model
set by finitely many points. Indeed each boundary coordinate equation
$m-k\alpha\in\{0,a_q\}$ or $n-k\beta\in\{0,1\}$ fixes at most one
integer $k$, by irrationality, and the remaining bounded coordinate
allows only finitely many integers. Thus a closed-window convention also
gives bounded discrepancy. The same observation handles endpoint choices
for physical intervals, with an additional count error of at most two.

\section{Irrational area and the nondiscrete spectrum}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:negation}
In the physical-$\R$, internal-$\R^2$ scheme (2),
\[
 \neg\bigl(\forall W\text{ a bounded remainder window},\quad
                   \operatorname{vol}_2(W)\in\tfrac12\Z\bigr).
\]
Furthermore its BRS area spectrum is not discrete. In particular the
conjecture's conjunction is false under its literal dimensions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Product Lebesgue measure gives
$\operatorname{vol}_2(W_q)=a_q=\fr{-q\sqrt2}$.
For $q\ge1$, the number $-q\sqrt2$ is irrational; subtracting its integer
floor preserves irrationality. Hence $a_q$ is irrational, lies strictly
between zero and one, and cannot equal an integer divided by two.
Theorem~\ref{thm:physical} supplies the required membership in the
cut-and-project BRS class, already for $q=1$. The Lean headline theorem
\nolinkurl{literal_volume_clause_false} states precisely this universal
negation over \nolinkurl{IsPhysicalBRS}.

The positive multiples of an irrational circle element are dense in
$\R/\Z$. Thus for every $0\le a<b\le1$, some $q\ge1$ has
$a<\fr{-q\sqrt2}<b$. The source proves this as an ordinary real
open-interval statement, using Mathlib's circle-orbit density and unique
representatives in $[0,1)$. The declaration
\nolinkurl{literal_physical_brs_areas_dense} includes physical BRS
membership of every witness in this density statement. The areas of this family are therefore dense
in $(0,1)$, with closure $[0,1]$. If a spectrum point $v\in(0,1)$
were isolated, a sufficiently small interval $(v,v+\varepsilon)$ would
contain no spectrum point, contradicting this density. This proves
nondiscreteness of the full physical BRS area spectrum. We do not assert
that its full closure is $[0,1]$, since other windows can have larger area.
\end{proof}
No polytopal classification is needed to negate the asserted conjunction.
As an additional human observation, a bounded planar polytope defined
by half-spaces with both integer normals and integer offsets has rational
vertices (solve the active linear equations), hence rational area by
triangulation. The irrational area of $W_q$ excludes that reading of the
geometric characterization, even up to null sets. If arbitrary real
offsets are permitted, this particular geometric argument does not apply;
the independent area-spectrum contradiction still holds.

\section{Precise formalization boundary}
The single Lean file imports Mathlib, uses actual Lebesgue volume, and
contains the following definitions:
\begin{lstlisting}
Index3 = Int * (Int * Int)
Ambient3 = Real * (Real * Real)
gamma alpha beta (k,m,n) =
  (k + alpha*m + beta*n, (m-k*alpha, n-k*beta))
lattice alpha beta = Set.range (gamma alpha beta)
phys p = p.1
internal p = p.2
modelSet alpha beta W =
  phys '' {p in lattice alpha beta | internal p in W}
covol alpha beta = 1 + alpha^2 + beta^2
physicalCount alpha beta W x y =
  (modelSet alpha beta W intersect Set.Ico x y).ncard
\end{lstlisting}
The actual identifiers for the first two types use subscript $3$.
\nolinkurl{IsPhysicalBRS} states measurability, finiteness of every counted
intersection, and (1) for all real $x\le y$, using the actual set
cardinality and actual product Lebesgue volume.
The covolume expression is the determinant calculated in
Lemma~\ref{lem:scheme}; its interpretation as lattice covolume is a
human verification, not an assumed theorem hidden in the definition.

The formal bridge includes:

The all-interval formal results, beyond the aligned-block bridge, are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \nolinkurl{literal_window_discrepancy}: explicit finiteness and
bound $q+2$ on every real half-open physical interval, by inner/outer
aligned-block inclusion and \nolinkurl{Set.ncard_le_ncard}.
\item \nolinkurl{literal_window_isPhysicalBRS}: membership of each

\item \nolinkurl{literal_volume_clause_false}: the headline negation
\begin{lstlisting}

  IsPhysicalBRS (sqrt 2) (sqrt 3) W ->
  exists k : Int, (volume W).toReal = k / 2)
\end{lstlisting}
\item \nolinkurl{literal_physical_brs_areas_dense}: every open subinterval

\end{itemize}

The only human bridge required for the literal-dimension disproof is
Lemma~\ref{lem:scheme}: the displayed image of $\Z^3$ is a discrete
full-rank lattice of covolume $6$, its physical projection restricted to
the lattice is injective, and its internal projection is dense in
$\R^2$. No Lean theorem establishes these admissibility properties, and
no additional axiom assumes them. The all-real-interval counting argument,
BRS membership, universal half-integer negation, density in the
physical BRS class, and topological nondiscreteness are Lean theorems. The optional closed-window and
rational-polytope observations remain human arguments and are not needed
for that negation.

\section{Secondary internal-line reading}\label{sec:secondary}
If one instead chooses a physical line and an internal line (total
lattice dimension two), the circle results remain a separate
counterexample. For every irrational rotation parameter $r$,
$[0,\fr{qr})$ is a circle BRS with irrational length for $q\ge1$,
and these lengths are dense in $(0,1)$. The source retains
\nolinkurl{IsBRS}, \nolinkurl{interval_counterexamples},
\nolinkurl{circle_volume_clause_false}, and
\nolinkurl{interval_volumes_dense} for this reading.

For a concrete internal-line scheme, put $a=\fr{\sqrt2}\in(0,1)$ and
\[
 \Gamma_a=\{(k-am,m-ak):k,m\in\Z\}\subset\R\times\R.
\]
Its determinant is $c_a=1-a^2>0$, its physical projection is injective
by irrationality, and its internal image $\Z+a\Z$ is dense.
For $W=[0,a)$ there is a selected point precisely when
$m=\lceil ak\rceil$ and $\fr{-ak}<a$. Its physical coordinate is
$t_k=c_a k-a\fr{-ak}$, a displacement of less than $a$ from $c_a k$.
The circle sum with rotation $-a$ and interval length
$a=\fr{-(-a)}$ has discrepancy at most one: its centered indicator is
$\fr{x-a}-\fr{x}$, a coboundary for rotation $-a$ with transfer function
$-\fr{x}$. Comparing physical intervals with the grid changes only a
bounded number of indices within distance $a/c_a$ of their endpoints.
Thus this secondary model also has bounded physical discrepancy and
irrational internal length. This paragraph is a human proof.

The source also retains the supporting two-torus predicate
\nolinkurl{IsBRS}$ _2$ and \nolinkurl{rectangle_isBRS} for translation by
$(r,s)$. That predicate is a Birkhoff-sum definition, and is not itself
identified with a cut-and-project scheme. The rank-three counting proof
in Sections 2--4 supplies the required primary bridge.

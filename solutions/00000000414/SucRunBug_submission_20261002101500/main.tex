\textbf{Conjecture 00000000414 -- FALSE.}

\textbf{Filed claim addressed.} The probability that a uniformly started hook walk visits cell $(i,j)$ differs from its content $j-i$ by at most $n^{-1/2}$.

\section*{Proof}
Use the one-row Young diagram $(3)$, with cells $(1,1),(1,2),(1,3)$ and $n=3$. The hook walk moves strictly right whenever it is not at the terminal cell. From column one it chooses columns two or three with equal probabilities; from column two its next cell is column three. Every starting cell therefore leads to column three, including a start at column three itself. Its visitation probability is exactly one.

The content of the terminal cell is $3-1=2$. The absolute deviation is $|1-2|=1$, but $3^{-1/2}<1$. The stated bound fails.

This is the ordinary, unnormalized content appearing in the filed statement. Replacing the content by a rescaled statistic would change the conjecture.

\section*{Formalization scope}
Lean evaluates the actual three-state hook-walk hitting recursion and its uniform-start probability, and proves the strict numerical violation with real powers.

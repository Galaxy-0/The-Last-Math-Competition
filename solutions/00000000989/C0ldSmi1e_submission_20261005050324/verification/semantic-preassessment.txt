Standard terminology and precise references
1. In positive-map theory, a map between complex matrix algebras is positive when it preserves positive semidefinite matrices; it is completely positive when every finite matrix ampliation is positive. Decomposable means P = S + transpose ∘ T with S and T completely positive. “Indecomposable” means a positive map that is not decomposable. The summands need not be nonzero. These conventions concern a cone of maps, with no trace-preserving or unital constraint. Exact primary source: Alexander Müller-Hermes, Decomposable Pauli diagonal maps and Tensor Squares of Qubit Maps, arXiv:2006.14543v1, §1, first three definitional sentences, HTML lines 94–96: https://arxiv.org/html/2006.14543#S1 . The same paper’s §2, lines 211–218, distinguishes semidefinite positivity from entrywise positivity, specifies transpose in a fixed basis, and gives C_L = (id ⊗ L)(omega).

2. For Phi: M_n(C) -> M_m(C), the Choi matrix is the block matrix assembled from the Phi(E_ij), up to exchanging the tensor factors. Its size is nm by nm. Its ordinary complex matrix rank is the Choi rank; for a completely positive map it is also the minimal Kraus length. Exact primary source: Mark Girard et al., On the mixed-unitary rank of quantum channels, March 31, 2020 author manuscript, §1, printed page 2, equations (1)–(3) and the following sentence (PDF zero-based page 1): https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~watrous/Papers/MixedUnitaryRank.pdf . The initial channel discussion adds trace preservation, whereas the stated Choi/Kraus notions apply to completely positive maps themselves.

Omissions and faithful conventions
• Domain/codomain: “dimension d” does not literally specify both. The natural square-system convention is Phi_d: M_d(C) -> M_d(C), motivated also by the d²-sized Choi matrix implicit in the rank expression. This is an interpretation to disclose. Here d is the Hilbert-space dimension (matrix side length), not the complex vector-space dimension of M_d, which is d². If rectangular maps are used instead, both dimensions must be given and the relation to the source explained.
• Parameter range: d should be a positive integer. The statement does not specify d >= 2, d >= 3, all sufficiently large d, or an infinite subset; none may be silently inserted as original text. The conventional d >= 1 reading should be stated, and any stronger uniform result covering all positive d can remove reliance on the omitted lower cutoff. The zero-dimensional algebra is not a standard physical reading and natural-number subtraction at d=0 must not silently replace the written arithmetic.
• Family/explicitness: no family index or uniformity requirement is stated. An affirmative formalization must disclose its nonempty parameter set, dimension assignment, and explicit defining data, with every claimed member satisfying the requirements. A family over an empty type cannot satisfy the intended existence. A negative result must make the logical passage from absence of individual members to absence of a nonempty family explicit; “explicit” is not a freely assignable proposition that may be set false.
• Indecomposable: the positive-map usage above is the best-established reading in this context. Extremality of a cone ray instead asks that summands within a specified cone be nonnegative scalar multiples; extremality of a convex set concerns convex decompositions and requires the set to be specified. Direct-sum indecomposability instead concerns invariant blocks or algebraic direct sums and needs a separate definition. These are different mathematical predicates. The source supplies neither a cone/normalization for extremality nor a block structure. Replacing the positive-map definition by either alternative would change the problem and cannot be done silently.
• Rank: under the square convention, rank d²−1 is an exact corank-one condition on the Choi matrix. It is not the rank of Phi as a linear transformation M_d -> M_d, nor a freely stored integer attached to a map. The general matrix-size rank upper bound is d². The parenthesis “extremal Choi rank” supplies no specified optimization class; it therefore does not authorize changing “completely positive” to “positive,” changing the explicit number, or adding an extreme-ray hypothesis. The numerical equality should be retained and the parenthesis described as an unexplained label.
• No additional normalization: “completely positive map” alone does not mean a quantum channel. Trace preservation, unitality, positivity improvement, strict positivity, irreducibility, and nonzero summands are absent. Adding any as a hidden definitional restriction would be a semantic alteration.

Mandatory bridges for a later formal artifact
A. The quantified objects must be actual complex-linear maps on actual complex matrices, or be accompanied by a proved interpretation into them. A structure with unrelated fields named CP, ChoiRank, or Indecomposable is insufficient.
B. Matrix positivity must be the Hermitian positive-semidefinite property, with an explicit link to nonnegative quadratic forms (or an already verified equivalent library definition). If the formalization uses factorization or a custom real/imaginary implementation, the equivalence to complex matrix positivity must be proved. Entrywise nonnegativity and positivity of a scalar tag are different notions.
C. Complete positivity must quantify over the actual ampliations id_k ⊗ Phi for every positive k, or use a proved equivalent criterion. A block-matrix entrywise implementation must be identified with that ampliation, including its index order. Linearity cannot be assumed merely because the map is called linear in prose.
D. Decomposability must quantify over maps of the same source and target, with actual map addition and composition with actual matrix transpose. The transpose is complex-linear and differs from conjugate transpose. If an input-transpose convention replaces the cited output-transpose convention, establish the equivalence rather than relying on naming. Independently encoded map operations require correctness lemmas.
E. If Choi data enter the final theorem, define the matrix via standard matrix units and verify its indexing and complex rank. A normalized Choi convention is acceptable only with the nonzero scalar normalization and rank correspondence explained. A theorem that genuinely rules out a broader class need not prove unused Choi-theorem machinery, but the stated specialization must still concern the genuine Choi rank rather than an uninterpreted surrogate.
F. The final quantified theorem must visibly imply the chosen literal claim or its negation. Record the d-range, domain/codomain convention, and family nonemptiness at this bridge. Abstract logical lemmas are acceptable components, not substitutes for instantiating the actual matrix semantics.

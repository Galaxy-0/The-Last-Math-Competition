## Exact statement and definitions

Both supplied language versions explicitly define h(−d) as the class number of Q(√−d), an imaginary quadratic field. There is no squarefree restriction on d=n²+1, and h is not defined to be the class number of the order generated by √−d. The source's “−d” is a radicand label, not an assertion about a fundamental discriminant.

The four definitions in the frozen source are literal and adequate:

| Declaration | Meaning verified from the full definition |
| --- | --- |
| imaginaryRoot | For natural d, the complex number √d · i, using the nonnegative real square root. |
| quadraticField | The intermediate field of C over Q generated by the singleton containing imaginaryRoot d. |
| imaginaryClassNumber | NumberField.classNumber of precisely that field. |
| Conjecture | There exists a real c>0 such that for every natural n≥2, c·Real.log n is at most the real cast of imaginaryClassNumber(n²+1). |

Pinned Mathlib defines NumberField.classNumber as Fintype.card of ClassGroup of the full ring of integers. RingOfIntegers is the integral closure of Z in the field; ClassGroup is the ordinary group of invertible fractional ideals modulo principal fractional ideals. Mathlib proves the finite-class-group instance for number fields. This is not Nat.card applied to a potentially infinite surrogate, an order class number, or an uninterpreted placeholder.

The natural-number domain of n is the conventional reading of this statement. The disproof also gives integer counterexamples under any broader domain containing them. Real.log is the usual natural logarithm. The theorem rules out all positive real constants, which is stronger than merely ruling out an explicit one. No additional “explicitness” hypothesis or effective class-number computation is needed.

## Semantic audit
The conjecture asserts, for a fixed ideal I, that μ(I^[p^e]) is eventually (e ≥ e₀) a polynomial in p^e whose degree equals dim R − ht I and whose leading coefficient depends only on the initial ideal. This is a conjunction, and the submission refutes the degree clause — hence the conjunction — with the ideal I = (x) of R = k[x,y], k any field of characteristic p: dim R = 2, ht I = 1, so the predicted degree is 1, while I^[p^e] = (x^(p^e)) is principal, so μ(I^[p^e]) = 1 for every e, and any polynomial P with P(p^e) = μ(I^[p^e]) for all large e is the constant 1, of degree 0 ≠ 1. Since the counterexample is a principal ideal of a polynomial ring — the setting the conjecture's own "initial ideal" language presupposes — it satisfies every hypothesis the text states, under any quantifier order over I.

The formalization is faithful to the conjecture's own definitions, including its delicate one. `frobeniusPow I q` is the ideal generated by the q-th powers of all elements of I, and `frobeniusPow_eq_span_pow_gens` proves by span induction (using the freshman's-dream identity (a+b)^q = a^q + b^q in characteristic p) that for q = p^e this equals the ideal generated by the q-th powers of *any* generating set — exactly the conjecture's generator-wise definition, shown well-defined. μ is Mathlib's `Submodule.spanFinrank`, verified in the pool source to be the minimum cardinality of a generating set (the conjecture's "minimal number of generators"); dim and height are Mathlib's `ringKrullDim` and `Ideal.height`. The two ring facts are proved via Mathlib (dim k[x⁰,x¹] = dim k + 2 = 2; height of a principal ideal generated by a non-unit non-zero-divisor is 1, applied to X 0, which is a nonzerodivisor and non-unit because evaluation at 0 kills it). The interpolation lemma is correct: p ≥ 2 gives infinitely many distinct points p^e in the characteristic-zero coefficient field, so a polynomial equal to 1 on all of them is the constant 1; the degree conclusion is proved for both `natDegree` and `degree`, so it does not depend on the degree convention. The main theorem `conjecture_3905_false` is the exact negation of the conjunctive claim (no e₀ and P with the interpolation property and deg P + ht I = dim R), and `frobenius_mu_polynomial_is_one` proves the stronger fact that every interpolant is the constant 1.

The mathematics was independently sanity-checked: (x)^[q] = (x^q) is immediate from freshman's dream, a nonzero principal ideal has μ = 1, and dim k[x,y] = 2, ht(x) = 1 are standard. The report is honest about scope: the bare polynomiality clause holds in this example (the value is constantly 1) and is not refuted; nor is the leading-coefficient clause, nor readings restricted to ht I = dim R (e.g. m-primary ideals, where the predicted degree 0 would agree). Note the counterexample is robust: the same failure is visible for I = m in k[x,y] (μ(m^[q]) = binom(q+1,2), degree 2 ≠ dim − ht m = 0), so the degree clause as stated fails broadly, not by a degenerate accident. This repairs precisely the vacuity for which PR #286 was rejected (numeral identities with no ring, ideal, Frobenius power or μ defined in Lean).

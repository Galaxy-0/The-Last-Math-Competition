1. Faithful finite-dimensional scope
The reference to Bendixson's eigenvalue bounds, together with “antisymmetric part” and “normal matrix,” supports the conventional setting of finite square real matrices, with their eigenvalues evaluated over C. No dimension restriction, diagonalizability condition, or assumption of normality is stated. A real square matrix is also an admissible special case of a complex-matrix reading using the adjoint. The subsequent mention of a normal minimizer is a claimed conclusion; it does not grammatically impose normality on every matrix in the first assertion.

For a real matrix A, define the antisymmetric part explicitly as K=(A-A^T)/2. The natural default norm in eigenvalue bounds is the Euclidean induced operator norm (spectral norm). “Imaginary-part bandwidth of the spectrum” naturally denotes max_{lambda in spectrum(A)} Im(lambda) minus min_{lambda in spectrum(A)} Im(lambda). State that convention and the complex spectrum explicitly. Eigenvalue multiplicities do not affect either extreme. An alternative use of “bandwidth” is the maximum absolute imaginary part, or half the total width for real matrices. An alternative normalization of the antisymmetric component omits the factor 1/2. These are conventional factor differences and must be acknowledged rather than exploited as the entire objection.

For complex matrices the usual analogue is the skew-Hermitian part (A-A*)/2, or the Hermitian imaginary part (A-A*)/(2i). On real matrices, transpose and adjoint agree. A submission using real entries therefore can address the real interpretation and the conventional complex-adjoint interpretation without conflating a complex transpose with an adjoint. State normality as AA*=A*A (or AA^T=A^TA in the real case) wherever it is mentioned.

Conversely, the final report must not claim to refute Bendixson's bounding inequality merely by refuting an equality.

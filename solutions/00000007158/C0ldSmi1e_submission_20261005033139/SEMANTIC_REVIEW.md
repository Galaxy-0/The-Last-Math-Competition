**Mathematical and semantic assessment.** Both source versions explicitly state a bandwidth–norm equality. The report uses the standard finite real-matrix interpretation with complex eigenvalues, normalized antisymmetric part `(A - Aᵀ)/2`, full imaginary spectral width, and the Euclidean induced operator norm. Neither source imposes normality or diagonalizability. Mentioning a normal minimizer in the second clause does not impose normality on matrices in the first universal assertion. The report carefully confines its verdict to the literal equality and does not claim to refute Bendixson's containing-strip inequality or a statement with additional hypotheses.

For the displayed matrix `N = [[0,2],[0,0]]`, the determinant calculation `det(zI-N) = z²` is exact for every complex `z`. Invertibility holds precisely when `z` is nonzero. Thus the spectrum is exactly `{0}`, excluding every other possible eigenvalue; the proof does not infer the bandwidth from merely a selected eigenvalue. The imaginary maximum, minimum, full width, and maximum absolute imaginary part are all zero. Its normalized antisymmetric part is exactly `J = [[0,1],[-1,0]]`. On both real and complex Euclidean vector spaces this matrix preserves vector norms; equivalently `J*J = I`. Its Euclidean operator norm is exactly one. Therefore the proposed equality fails.

The formalization uses Mathlib's actual algebraic `spectrum`, not a substitute set named “spectrum.” `mem_spectrum_iff_eigenvector` independently bridges membership to a nonzero complex eigenvector, and `counterexample_spectrum` proves equality of the entire set with `{0}`. `imaginarySpectrum` is precisely the image under `Complex.im`. Although the general bandwidth definition uses `sSup` and `sInf`, the witness spectrum is a nonempty singleton, so these agree exactly with the maxima and minima in the report. No empty-spectrum or unbounded-set convention contributes to the contradiction.

The explicit `Matrix.L2OpNorm` scope selects the Euclidean operator norm. `norm_is_euclidean_operator_norm` identifies it with the continuous linear map norm of `Matrix.toEuclideanCLM`. I also inspected the corresponding pinned Mathlib definitions and lemmas: the map acts on `EuclideanSpace`, the scoped norm is its induced operator norm, and `l2_opNorm_conjTranspose_mul_self` gives the C-star norm identity used in both real and complex norm calculations. The proof does not use the entrywise maximum norm or an unjustified spectral-radius surrogate.

The convention claims in the report are supported. The radius is formally zero; positive factors on the norm side cannot erase the gap; an unhalved antisymmetric difference has norm two; and half the full zero width is still zero. Real entries make transpose and adjoint agree. The complex skew-Hermitian-part and Hermitian-imaginary-part norms are formally one; the latter differs by the scalar `-i`, whose modulus is one. The generic norm theorem uses only the zero-separation property, which every genuine norm satisfies, to establish inequality. The report's stronger prose that such a norm is positive on the nonzero matrix is the usual norm positivity property. The witness's failure of normality is also formally proved and is admissible because the statement includes no normality assumption.

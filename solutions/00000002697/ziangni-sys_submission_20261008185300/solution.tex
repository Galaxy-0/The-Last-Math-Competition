The logarithmic average of the pointwise Jacobian eigenvalues need not equal the Lyapunov exponent of the actual tangent evolution. A polynomial map of the affine plane provides a concrete counterexample; no independent cocycle certificate is assumed.

Define the actual map
\[
 F(x,y)=\bigl(1+x-6x^2+4x^3+(1-x)y,\ x^3-x^2+y\bigr).
\]
It has a two-cycle $p_0=(0,0)$ and $p_1=(1,0)$, since $F(p_0)=p_1$ and $F(p_1)=p_0$. Its true derivative is
\[
 DF(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}
 1-12x+12x^2-y&1-x\\3x^2-2x&1
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Consequently the Jacobians along the orbit alternate between
\[
 A=DF(p_0)=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 B=DF(p_1)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Each complex spectrum is exactly $\{1\}$: in both cases $\det(zI-DF)=(z-1)^2$. Thus every choice of pointwise Jacobian eigenvalues, and their logarithmic norm average along this orbit, gives zero.

However, $BA=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has an expanding eigenvalue
\[
 \rho=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}>2,\qquad \rho^2-3\rho+1=0.
\]
Let $v=(1,\rho-1)$ and $w=Av=(\rho,\rho-1)$. The actual tangent evolution $v_0=v$, $v_{n+1}=DF(p_n)v_n$ satisfies
\[
 Bw=\rho v,\qquad v_{2m}=\rho^m v,\qquad v_{2m+1}=\rho^m w.
\]
These identities follow from the actual derivative and orbit recurrence. The Zariski tangent spaces of the smooth affine plane are its usual two-dimensional tangent spaces, so this example also applies to the tangent-space wording of the source.

Use the maximum norm on the real plane. Then $\|v\|=\rho-1$ and $\|w\|=\rho$, both positive. For every $n$,
\[
 \log\|v_n\|=\left\lfloor\frac n2\right\rfloor\log\rho+r_n,
 \qquad r_n\in\{\log(\rho-1),\log\rho\}.
\]
The remainder is bounded, while $\lfloor n/2\rfloor/n\to1/2$. Therefore the actual Lyapunov limit, along all iteration counts rather than just the even subsequence, is
\[
 \lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|v_n\|}{n}
 =\frac{\log\rho}{2}>0.
\]
This contradicts the pointwise spectral logarithmic average zero. In finite dimension changing the norm does not change this limit. Noncommuting products must be accounted for; pointwise eigenvalues alone do not determine tangent-vector growth. Here ``Jacobi spectrum'' is interpreted in its usual sense as the Jacobian eigenvalue spectrum. The separate symmetry-group assertion is not needed for the disproof.

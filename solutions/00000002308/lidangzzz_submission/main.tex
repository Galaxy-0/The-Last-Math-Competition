\begin{abstract}
We disprove a conjecture of the Last Math Competition asserting that the smallest nontrivial solvable complete group has order $2^{11} \cdot 3 = 6144$. The symmetric group $S_3$ is nontrivial, solvable, and complete (trivial center, every automorphism inner), and has order $6 \ne 6144$. In fact $S_3$ is the smallest nontrivial complete group, solvable or not. The formalization in Lean~4 (\texttt{Batch6.lean}) verifies all finite facts by kernel computation (\texttt{decide}) and the structural parts by Mathlib's \texttt{MulAut}/\texttt{IsSolvable} API; compilation is clean and the axioms are the standard three.
\end{abstract}

\section{The conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[TLMC-00000002308]\label{conj:2308}
A complete group satisfies $G = \operatorname{Aut}(G)$. The order of the smallest nontrivial solvable complete group is $2^{11}\cdot 3$; its structure is a minimal example of a semidirect product of a $2$-group.
\end{conjecture}

\section{The disproof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:2308}
$S_3$ is a nontrivial solvable complete group of order $6$. Hence Conjecture~\ref{conj:2308} is \textbf{false}: the smallest nontrivial solvable complete group has order at most $6$, not $2^{11} \cdot 3 = 6144$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$S_3 = \operatorname{Perm}(\{0,1,2\})$ has exactly six elements $1, s_{01}, s_{02}, s_{12}, s_{01}s_{02}, s_{02}s_{01}$ (kernel-computed).
\emph{Solvable.} The map $\operatorname{sgn}$ sending the three transpositions to $-1$ and the other three elements to $+1$ is a homomorphism (kernel-verified on all $36$ pairs). Its kernel is $\{1, s_{01}s_{02}, s_{02}s_{01}\}$, whose elements commute pairwise (kernel-verified), so $[S_3,S_3]$ is abelian and $[S_3,S_3]^{(1)} = 1$: the derived series terminates in two steps.
\emph{Complete.} The center is trivial: an element commuting with $s_{01}$ and $s_{02}$ is $1$ (kernel-verified). For innerness, note first that automorphisms preserve the involution property $\sigma^2 = 1, \sigma \ne 1$, and the involutions of $S_3$ are exactly the three transpositions (kernel-verified). Any two \emph{distinct} transpositions are simultaneously conjugate to $(s_{01}, s_{02})$; the six conjugators are exhibited explicitly, case by case, with each conjugation identity kernel-verified. Given an automorphism $\varphi$, the pair $(\varphi\, s_{01}, \varphi\, s_{02})$ is such a distinct pair, so some $k$ conjugates $s_{01}, s_{02}$ to it. The set of $h$ with $\varphi(h) = khk^{-1}$ contains $s_{01}$ and $s_{02}$ and is closed under products and inverses; since every element of $S_3$ is a word in $s_{01}, s_{02}$ (the six-element enumeration), $\varphi = \operatorname{conj}_k$ is inner.
\emph{Order.} $|S_3| = 6 \ne 6144$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The group of order $2^{11} \cdot 3$ mentioned in the conjecture may exist as \emph{some} solvable complete group, but it is far from the smallest: $S_3$ (order $6$) already qualifies, and in fact no smaller nontrivial group is complete (groups of order $< 6$ are abelian or have noninner automorphisms). The claimed ``minimality'' is what fails.
\end{remark}

\section{Machine verification}

\texttt{Batch6.lean} (namespace \texttt{TLMCBatch6}) formalizes the above: the enumeration, involution, centralizer, and conjugation facts are proved by \texttt{decide} (Lean kernel evaluation over the six-element group); solvability via the derived series and the parity homomorphism \texttt{sgn}; completeness via \texttt{MulAut} and an equalizer argument (\texttt{S3\_complete}); the final refutation is \texttt{tlm2308} together with \texttt{tlm2308\_false} ($2^{11} \cdot 3 \ne |S_3|$). Compilation via \texttt{lake env lean Batch6.lean} exits $0$ with zero errors and warnings; \texttt{\#print axioms} reports at most \texttt{[propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound]}; no \texttt{sorry} appears.

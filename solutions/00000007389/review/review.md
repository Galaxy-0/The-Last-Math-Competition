The submission proves the standard precise reading. Let E be a normed vector space over a nontrivially normed field (Lean's generality covers both the real and complex cases, with no finite-dimensionality or completeness assumed), f : E → E, and φ(x) = Tx + b with T a continuous linear isomorphism. With the transformed residual g(y) = T f(φ⁻¹(y)), the root equations correspond exactly (g(φ(x)) = 0 ⟺ f(x) = 0, proved as `roots_covariance`).

For part (i): if f has Fréchet derivative D at x with D invertible, then by the genuine chain rule (`transformed_derivative`, built from `HasFDerivAt` of the affine maps and composition) g has derivative D̃ = TDT⁻¹ at φ(x). Since D̃⁻¹ = TD⁻¹T⁻¹, the Newton step obeys the exact equivariance N_g(φ(x)) = φ(x) − D̃⁻¹g(φ(x)) = T(x − D⁻¹f(x)) + b = φ(N_f(x)) (`newton_covariance`, packaged with the derivative fact in `actual_newton_step`). This lifts verbatim to full recursive orbits (`orbit_covariance`: y_n = φ(x_n) for all n), and `orbit_derivative` shows the derivative hypothesis needs to hold only at the visited points, so no convergence or genericity assumption is smuggled in — the hypotheses are exactly what makes a classical Newton step defined. The report further derives that the covariance is robust under the residual convention: with unchanged residual ŝ = f∘φ⁻¹ the derivative is DT⁻¹ and its inverse TD⁻¹, giving the same correction and the same equivariance, and a general invertible residual change S gives SDT⁻¹ with inverse TD⁻¹S⁻¹; the formalized convention S = T loses nothing.

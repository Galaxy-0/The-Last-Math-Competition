\section{The conjecture and the clause refuted}

\textbf{Statement (English, verbatim).} Definition: The sphere packing density is the supremal
fraction of space filled by non-overlapping unit balls. Conjecture: The densest lattice packings in
dimensions five, six, and seven are given by the D lattices; and the determination is settled
through Voronoi cell volumes. (D-lattice Voronoi volume optimality in dimensions 5-7)
(The Chinese text states the same.)

\textbf{Reading.} The main clause is a conjunction over $n\in\{5,6,7\}$ of
\[
  \text{(C$_n$)}\qquad \text{the densest lattice packing of unit balls in } \R^n \text{ is given by } D_n ,
\]
where $D_n=\{x\in\Z^n : x_1+\dots+x_n \text{ even}\}$ is the standard root lattice. We refute
(C$_7$), which refutes the conjunction and hence the conjecture. We read ``given by $D_7$'' as:
the unit balls are centred at the points of a similar copy $c\,\varphi(D_7)$ of $D_7$ (any scale
$c\neq 0$, any linear isometry $\varphi$). We refute two readings:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(R1)] some lattice packing of unit balls centred at a similar copy of $D_7$ is at least as
  dense as every lattice packing of unit balls (\texttt{not\_D7\_densest});
\item[(R2)] (stronger) there is a lattice packing of unit balls that is \emph{strictly} denser than
  every lattice packing of unit balls centred at a similar copy of $D_7$
  (\texttt{exists\_packing\_denser\_than\_D7}). So no maximizer is of $D_7$ type.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Not addressed.} The cases $n=5$ and $n=6$ are not used. The trailing clause ``the
determination is settled through Voronoi cell volumes'' describes a method; it is the second part
of a conjunction and is not needed. (The Voronoi cell of a lattice is a fundamental domain, so its
volume is the covolume used below.)

\section{Definitions (as in the Lean file)}

We work in $\R^n$ = \texttt{EuclideanSpace R (Fin n)} with Lebesgue measure $\vol$ (Mathlib's
\texttt{volume}) and Euclidean distance.

\begin{definition}[lattice packing of unit balls; \texttt{LatticePacking n}]
A $\Z$-submodule $\Lambda\subset\R^n$ that is discrete and spans $\R^n$ over $\R$ (Mathlib's
\texttt{DiscreteTopology} and \texttt{IsZLattice}), such that for distinct $x,y\in\Lambda$ the open
unit balls $B(x,1)$ and $B(y,1)$ are disjoint. Equivalently $|x-y|\ge 2$ for $x\neq y$ in $\Lambda$
(Mathlib \texttt{disjoint\_ball\_ball\_iff}); compare the Leech lattice article: ``The last condition
is equivalent to the condition that unit balls centered at the points of $\Lambda_{24}$ do not
overlap.''
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[density; \texttt{LatticePacking.density}]
$\Delta(P)=\vol(B(0,1))/\covol(\Lambda)$, where $\covol$ is Mathlib's \texttt{ZLattice.covolume},
the volume of a fundamental domain (``The n-dimensional volume of this fundamental domain is
sometimes called the covolume of the lattice'', Wikipedia, Lattice (group)).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{LatticePacking.filled\_fraction}]
For every fundamental domain $F$ of $\Lambda$,
$\vol\big((\bigcup_{x\in\Lambda}B(x,1))\cap F\big)/\vol(F)=\Delta(P)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\vol(B(0,1))=\sum_{g\in\Lambda}\vol((g+B(0,1))\cap F)$ (Mathlib
\texttt{IsAddFundamentalDomain.measure\_eq\_tsum}), $g+B(0,1)=B(g,1)$, and the sets
$B(g,1)\cap F$ are pairwise disjoint, so the sum is $\vol((\bigcup_g B(g,1))\cap F)$. Finally
$\covol(\Lambda)=\vol(F)$.
\end{proof}
So $\Delta(P)$ is the proportion of each fundamental cell, hence (by periodicity) of space, that the
balls fill; ``The proportion of space filled by the spheres is called the packing density of the
arrangement'' (Wikipedia, Sphere packing).

\begin{definition}[\texttt{Dlat n}, \texttt{IsSimilarCopy}, \texttt{DLatticeIsDensest}]
$D_n=\{x\in\R^n:\exists z\in\Z^n,\ x=z,\ \sum_i z_i \text{ even}\}$ (``The Dn root lattice ...
consists of all integer vectors whose components sum to an even integer'', Wikipedia, Root system).
$\Lambda$ is a similar copy of $M$ if $\Lambda=\{c\,\varphi(x):x\in M\}$ for some real $c\neq0$ and
linear isometry $\varphi$ of $\R^n$. \texttt{DLatticeIsDensest n} says: there is a lattice packing
$Q$ with $\Lambda_Q$ a similar copy of $D_n$ and $\Delta(P)\le\Delta(Q)$ for every lattice
packing $P$.
\end{definition}

\section{Proof}

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{covolume\_span}]
If $b$ is an $\R$-basis of $\R^7$ then $\covol(\operatorname{span}_\Z b)=|\det b|$ (determinant
in the standard basis).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Mathlib \texttt{ZLattice.covolume\_eq\_det\_mul\_measureReal} with the standard basis, whose
fundamental parallelepiped (the unit cube) has volume $1$.
\end{proof}

\textbf{The witness (Construction A).} Let $C\subset\mathbb{F}_2^7$ be the binary $[7,3,4]$
simplex code with generators $g_1=1000111$, $g_2=0101011$, $g_3=0011101$. Its seven nonzero
codewords all have weight $4$; Lean checks weight $\ge 4$ by \texttt{decide} over the eight messages
(\texttt{weight\_ge}). Let $E=\{x\in\Z^7: x \bmod 2\in C\}$ (\texttt{E7A}).

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{sum\_sq\_ge}, \texttt{E7A\_norm}]
Every nonzero $x\in E$ has $|x|\ge 2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
If $x\bmod 2=0$, some coordinate is a nonzero even integer, so $|x|^2\ge4$. Otherwise $x\bmod 2$ is
a nonzero codeword, so at least $4$ coordinates are odd, and $|x|^2\ge4$.
\end{proof}

Let $\Lambda_0$ be the $\Z$-span of the rows of
$A_L$ = rows $g_1,g_2,g_3,2e_4,2e_5,2e_6,2e_7$ (as integer vectors). The rows lie in $E$, so
$\Lambda_0\subseteq E$ (\texttt{span\_le\_E7A}). The matrix $A_L$ is upper triangular with
diagonal $1,1,1,2,2,2,2$, so $\det A_L=16$ (\texttt{det\_AL}). Hence $P_0=(\Lambda_0)$ is a lattice
packing of unit balls (\texttt{PE7}) with $\Delta(P_0)=\vol(B(0,1))/16$ (\texttt{PE7\_density}).

\begin{remark}
In fact $\Lambda_0=E$ (both have index $16$ in $\Z^7$), and $E$ is an integral model of
$\sqrt2\,E_7$. Neither fact is used or formalized.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{Dlat\_eq\_span}, \texttt{det\_AD}]
$D_7$ is the $\Z$-span of $e_j+e_7$ $(1\le j\le 6)$ and $2e_7$; this basis matrix $A_D$ is upper
triangular with $\det A_D=2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The generators have even coordinate sum. Conversely, if $\sum_i z_i=2k$ then
$z=\sum_{j\le6}z_j(e_j+e_7)+(k-\sum_{j\le6}z_j)\,2e_7$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{similar\_D7\_density\_lt}]
If $Q$ is a lattice packing of unit balls with $\Lambda_Q=c\,\varphi(D_7)$, then
$\Delta(Q)<\Delta(P_0)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\Lambda_Q$ is the $\Z$-span of the basis $c\,\varphi(\text{rows of }A_D)$, so
$\covol(\Lambda_Q)=|c|^7\,|\det\varphi|\cdot 2=2|c|^7$, using $|\det\varphi|=1$
(\texttt{abs\_det\_isometry}, from Mathlib's
\texttt{OrthonormalBasis.det\_to\_matrix\_orthonormalBasis\_real}). The vector $v=e_1+e_7\in D_7$
has $|v|=\sqrt2$, so $c\,\varphi(v)\in\Lambda_Q$ is nonzero of length $|c|\sqrt2$. Non-overlap of
the balls at $c\,\varphi(v)$ and $0$ gives $|c|\sqrt2\ge2$, so $|c|\ge\sqrt2$ and
$|c|^7\ge 8\sqrt2$. Therefore $\Delta(Q)=\vol(B(0,1))/(2|c|^7)\le\vol(B(0,1))/(16\sqrt2)
<\vol(B(0,1))/16=\Delta(P_0)$.
\end{proof}

Taking $P=P_0$ gives (R2), and (R2) implies (R1) is false. In center-density terms: $P_0$ has
$1/16$ and the best $D_7$ packing ($c=\sqrt2$) has $2^{-9/2}\approx 0.0442$.

\textbf{Conventions.} Balls are open Euclidean balls. ``Lattice'' means a discrete, full-rank

isometry. Density is $\vol(B(0,1))/\covol$, which equals the filled fraction of any fundamental
domain (Lemma \texttt{filled\_fraction}). The volume of $B(0,1)$ is never computed; it cancels.

\begin{abstract}
We disprove a conjecture of the Last Math Competition asserting that within the purely substitutive class the factor complexity satisfies $p(n) \le n + 2$, with $p(n) = n+1$ only for Sturmian words. The Thue--Morse word --- the fixed point of the binary substitution $0 \mapsto 01$, $1 \mapsto 10$, plainly purely substitutive --- has $p(3) = 6 > 5 = 3+2$: its length-3 factors are exactly $001, 010, 011, 100, 101, 110$. The Lean~4 formalization builds the substitution on lists, proves the iterate-prefix property (via the homomorphism $\sigma(a{+}{+}b) = \sigma a {+}{+} \sigma b$), realizes the fixed point by the bit recursion $\mathrm{tm}(2k) = \mathrm{tm}(k)$, $\mathrm{tm}(2k{+}1) = \neg\,\mathrm{tm}(k)$, identifies it with the fifth iterate on 32 symbols, and counts the distinct factors by kernel computation. Zero errors/warnings; the decisive theorems depend on \emph{no} axioms.
\end{abstract}
\section{The conjecture}
\begin{conjecture}[TLMC-00000001260]\label{conj:1260}
The optimal upper bound for factor complexity within the purely substitutive class is $p(n) \le n+2$, attained by three-letter substitution families beyond the Tribonacci word; $p(n) = n+1$ occurs only for Sturmian words.
\end{conjecture}
\section{The disproof}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:1260}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:1260} is \textbf{false} in its main clause: the Thue--Morse word $w$ (fixed point of $0\mapsto 01,\ 1\mapsto 10$) is purely substitutive and
\[
p_w(3) \;=\; 6 \;>\; 5 \;=\; 3 + 2.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The length-3 factors of $w = 0110100110010110\cdots$ are exactly $\{001, 010, 011, 100, 101, 110\}$ (the cubes $000$ and $111$ never occur, as $w$ is overlap-free; all six listed factors occur within the first 32 symbols $=$ the fifth substitution iterate). Hence $p_w(3) = 6$.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The Sturmian clause ($p(n) = n+1$ only for Sturmian) is a classical theorem (Coven--Hedlund type) and is not disputed. The failure is the claimed \emph{upper bound}: substitutive words can have complexity growing with an arbitrarily large linear slope (Thue--Morse has $p(n) \sim \frac{10}{3}n$; the claim $p(n) \le n+2$ already fails at $n = 3$).
\end{remark}

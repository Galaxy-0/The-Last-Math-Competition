\begin{conjecture}[00000000578]
Let $\mathrm{bin}(n,i)$ denote the binary matroid whose ground set is the set
of $i$-subsets of $\{1,\dots,n\}$, represented over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ by the
weight-$i$ $0/1$-vectors, and let $\chi_{n,i}$ denote its characteristic
polynomial.  For all $2 \le i \le n-2$ and all integers $m \ge 1$, the value
$\chi_{n,i}(-m)$ is divisible by $i!\,(n-i)!$, and the quotient is a
nonnegative combination of Eulerian numbers.
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}[Verdict: FALSE]
The conjecture fails already at the first admissible parameters beyond the
smallest cases.  Both counterexamples below lie strictly inside the quantifier
range $2 \le i \le n-2$, $m \ge 1$.
\end{theorem}

\section{Attack}

The characteristic polynomial of a matroid $M$ with rank function $r$ on
ground set $E$ is
\[
\chi_M(t) \;=\; \sum_{X \subseteq E} (-1)^{|X|}\, t^{\,r(E) - r(X)} .
\]
We computed $\chi_{n,i}$ from this definition directly, enumerating all
subsets of the ground set and evaluating the $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ rank by Gaussian
elimination (cross-checked against brute-force span enumeration).

\paragraph{Counterexample 1: $\mathrm{bin}(5,3)$, $m=1$.}
Here $r = 5$, $|E| = 10$, and
\[
\chi_{5,3}(t) \;=\; t^5 - 10t^4 + 45t^3 - 105t^2 + 120t - 51 .
\]
At $m = 1$ the modulus is $3!\,2! = 12$ and
\[
\chi_{5,3}(-1) = -332 = -12 \cdot 28 + 4, \qquad 12 \nmid -332 .
\]

\paragraph{Counterexample 2: $\mathrm{bin}(6,2)$, $m=2$.}
Here $r = 5$, $|E| = 15$, and
\[
\chi_{6,2}(t) \;=\; t^5 - 15t^4 + 85t^3 - 225t^2 + 274t - 120 .
\]
At $m = 2$ the modulus is $2!\,4! = 48$ and
\[
\chi_{6,2}(-2) = -2520 = -48 \cdot 53 + 24, \qquad 48 \nmid -2520 .
\]
Note that $\mathrm{bin}(6,2)$ \emph{does} satisfy the divisibility claim at
$m=1$, where $\chi_{6,2}(-1) = -720 = 15 \cdot 48$: the failure is specific to
$m \ge 2$.

Since divisibility fails, the claimed Eulerian-quotient consequence is never
reached.

\section{Boundary data}

Divisibility of $\chi_{n,i}(-m)$ by $i!\,(n-i)!$ for small admissible
$(n,i)$ and $m \in \{1,2,3\}$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
 & $m=1$ & $m=2$ & $m=3$ \\
\midrule
$\mathrm{bin}(4,2)$, mod $4$   & OK & OK & OK \\
$\mathrm{bin}(5,2)$, mod $12$  & OK & OK & OK \\
$\mathrm{bin}(5,3)$, mod $12$  & $\mathbf{FAIL}$ ($-332$) & FAIL ($-1263$) & OK ($-3624$) \\
$\mathrm{bin}(6,2)$, mod $48$  & OK ($-720$)  & $\mathbf{FAIL}$ ($-2520$) & OK ($-6720$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The smallest counterexample is $\mathrm{bin}(5,3)$ at $m=1$.

\section{Formal verification}

The accompanying Lean 4 development (\texttt{lean4/}, core Lean only, toolchain
\texttt{leanprover/lean4:v4.33.1}, no Mathlib) implements the $\mathrm{GF}(2)$
rank of bitmask vectors and evaluates the characteristic polynomial by
enumerating \emph{all} subsets of the ground set inside Lean, so the attack
numbers are computed by the kernel from the matroid data rather than assumed:
\[
\mathrm{rank}(E_{53}) = 5,\; \chi_{E_{53}}(-1) = -332; \qquad
\mathrm{rank}(E_{62}) = 5,\; \chi_{E_{62}}(-2) = -2520,
\]
together with the divisibility failures expressed through remainders (for
positive $a$, $a \mid b \iff b \bmod a = 0$):
\[
(-332) \bmod 12 = 4 \neq 0, \quad (-2520) \bmod 48 = 24 \neq 0, \quad
332 \bmod 12 = 8, \quad 2520 \bmod 48 = 24 .
\]
The boundary values $\chi_{5,3}(-2) = -1263$, $\chi_{5,3}(-3) = -3624$,
$\chi_{6,2}(-1) = -720$, $\chi_{6,2}(-3) = -6720$ are verified as well.  An
axiom audit (\texttt{Check.lean}) confirms that every definition and theorem
\emph{does not depend on any axioms} and the development contains no
\texttt{sorry}.

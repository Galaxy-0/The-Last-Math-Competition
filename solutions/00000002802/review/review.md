## Semantic audit
The official statement (both languages) asserts an equivalence: the Cramér rate function is twice differentiable on the interior of the support **if and only if** the underlying distribution is non-lattice (with the variance-coefficient and periodic-correction clauses attached in the Chinese grouping). Any such equivalence implies the necessary implication D ⇒ N (twice differentiable ⇒ non-lattice) under either grouping, so refuting that single implication refutes the conjecture; no interpretation of the vague variance/periodic clauses is required. This logical targeting is stated explicitly and correctly in Section 1 of the report.

The counterexample is the fair Bernoulli law `μ = ½δ₀ + ½δ₁` (`fairBernoulli` in `Law.lean`). It is a probability measure, nondegenerate, with every exponential moment integrable, and it is lattice in the standard sense (`LatticeLaw`: a.e. concentrated on `a + h·ℤ` with `h > 0`, here `a=0, h=1`) — exactly the negation of "non-lattice" as the conjecture uses it. The rate function is *defined*, not asserted: `cramerRate μ x = ⨆ over integrable tilts t of (t·x − cgf μ t)` (`Transform.lean`), which is the genuine convex dual of the conjecture's "convex-dual form" with the standard integrability restriction. For this law `cgf μ t = log((1+e^t)/2)` is proved in Lean, and the Legendre transform is *computed*: the concavity upper bound (`dual_objective_le_binaryRate`) and the attaining tilt `t_x = log(x/(1−x))` (`dual_objective_optimalTilt`) together give `I(x) = log2 + x·log x + (1−x)·log(1−x)` on (0,1) as an equality in `EReal` against the actual supremum — verified independently by the numeric spot-check above. Outside [0,1] the dual is shown to be +∞, so the effective domain has interior exactly (0,1).

The differentiability predicate `RateTwiceDifferentiableOn I S` requires a real representative `f` with `f` and `deriv f` differentiable on `S`, equal to `I` on `S`; since `S` is open this is exactly "the rate is twice differentiable on S" for an extended-real-valued rate. The submission actually proves the stronger `RateC2On` (a `ContDiff ℝ ∞` representative, from smoothness of `log` on positive arguments) and bridges C² ⇒ twice-differentiable on open sets (`RateC2On.twiceDifferentiableOn`). Crucially, all three plausible readings of "interior of the support" are handled separately: ordinary topological support (interior ∅, certificate vacuous), convex hull of the support (interior (0,1)), and effective domain of the rate (interior (0,1)); `necessary_criterion_fails_all_three_readings` refutes the necessary implication for each, and `explicit_counterexample` bundles the full witness. The refuted statement `NonlatticeNecessaryOn` is *stronger* than the conjecture's implication (it hypothesizes all exponential moments and nondegeneracy, extra hypotheses the counterexample satisfies), so its failure implies the failure of the unrestricted implication — the correct direction for a disproof. The mathematically substantive point is sound: lattice structure affects local limit theorems/prefactors, not the analytic smoothness of the Legendre transform, and the fair Bernoulli rate is the classical smooth binary entropy expression.

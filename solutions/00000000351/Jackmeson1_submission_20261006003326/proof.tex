\section{The conjecture and the clause refuted}

\textbf{Statement (English, verbatim).} Definition: The covering radius of the D\_n lattice is
determined by its coordination structure. Conjecture: For dimensions up to eight, the D\_n lattices
are covering-optimal, with the first non-D exception in dimension nine; and the exceptional lattice
configuration is given by an E8 extension. (Dn lattice covering optimal through dimension eight)
(The Chinese text states the same.)

\textbf{Reading.} The conjecture is a conjunction of (A) for every dimension $n\le 8$ the lattice
$D_n$ is covering-optimal, (B) dimension nine is the first exception, and (C) the exceptional lattice
comes from an $E_8$ extension. We refute (A), which refutes the conjunction. The named family is
$D_n=\{x\in\Z^n: x_1+\dots+x_n \text{ even}\}$ itself (not its dual $D_n^*$). We read
``covering-optimal'' as ``gives the thinnest lattice covering'', i.e.\ minimizes the covering density
$\Theta$ over all lattices of $\R^n$ (the lattice covering problem of Sch\"urmann and Vallentin,
Section~2), and ``$D_n$ is'' as ``some similar copy $c\,\varphi(D_n)$ is'' ($c\ne 0$ real,
$\varphi$ a linear isometry). The dimension used is $n=3$, which lies in ``up to eight'' when the family
is read as the even-sum lattices $D_n=\{x\in\mathbb Z^n:\sum x_i\text{ even}\}$, which are well
defined and non-degenerate for every $n\ge 2$ ($D_3$ is the fcc lattice). Under the
root-system classification convention that reserves type $D_n$ for $n\ge4$, dimension 3 would be
excluded; this package does not supply a counterexample in the range $4\le n\le 8$. We refute:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(R1)] some similar copy of $D_3$ has covering density at most that of every lattice in $\R^3$
  (\texttt{not\_DCoveringOptimal\_three}); hence the claim fails for every range of dimensions
  containing $3$, in particular $3\le n\le 8$ (\texttt{conjecture\_351\_false});
\item[(R2)] (stronger) one explicit lattice, the body-centred cubic lattice, has \emph{strictly}
  smaller covering density than \emph{every} similar copy of $D_3$
  (\texttt{coveringDensity\_bcc\_lt\_similar\_D3}); in particular $\Theta(\bcc)<\Theta(D_3)$
  (\texttt{coveringDensity\_bcc\_lt\_D3}).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Not addressed.} Clauses (B) and (C) are not used. The Definition line (``determined by its
coordination structure'') is descriptive and imposes nothing formal; we use the standard covering
radius. The reading ``optimal among all (also non-lattice) sphere coverings'' implies the lattice
reading (the bcc lattice covering is itself a covering), but the density of a general covering is not
formalized. The packing-covering ratio $\mu/\lambda$ and the dual family $D_n^*$ are not treated.

\section{Definitions (as in the Lean file)}

We work in $\R^n$ = \texttt{EuclideanSpace R (Fin n)} with Lebesgue measure $\vol$ (Mathlib's
\texttt{volume}) and Euclidean distance.

\begin{definition}[lattice; \texttt{Lattice n}]
A $\Z$-submodule $\Lambda\subset\R^n$ that is discrete and spans $\R^n$ over $\R$ (Mathlib's
\texttt{DiscreteTopology} and \texttt{IsZLattice R}). Its covolume $\covol\Lambda$ is Mathlib's
\texttt{ZLattice.covolume}, the volume of a fundamental domain. Wikipedia (Lattice (group)): ``The
n-dimensional volume of this fundamental domain is sometimes called the covolume of the lattice: it is
invariant for any basis, and may be computed as $d(\Lambda)=|\det(M)|$''.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[covering radius; \texttt{coveringRadius}]
For $S\subseteq\R^n$, $\mu(S)=\inf\{r\ge 0 : \forall x\in\R^n\ \exists l\in S,\ \|x-l\|\le r\}$.
Wikipedia (Delone set): ``The covering radius, R, of X is the smallest distance such that every point
of M is within distance R of at least one point in X; that is, R is the smallest radius such that
closed balls of that radius centered at the points of X have all of M as their union.''
Sch\"urmann--Vallentin: ``the covering radius $\mu(L)$ by
$\mu(L)=\min\{\mu: L+\mu B^{d} \text{ is a lattice covering}\}$''.
Convention: in Lean $\inf\emptyset=0$; every use below is for a set for which a covering radius
$R<\infty$ is proved first, so the value is the genuine infimum.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[covering density; \texttt{coveringDensity}]
$\Theta(\Lambda)=\vol\bigl(\overline B(0,\mu(\Lambda))\bigr)/\covol\Lambda$. Sch\"urmann--Vallentin:
``the covering density $\Theta(L)=\frac{\mu(L)^{d}}{\det(L)}\cdot\kappa_{d}$, $\kappa_{d}=\mathrm{vol}\,B^{d}$,
is invariant with respect to scaling of $L$'' and ``Problem 2.1. (Lattice covering problem) For a
given $d$, determine $\Theta_{d}=\min_{L}\Theta(L)$, where $L$ runs over all $d$-dimensional
lattices.'' The two formulas agree: Mathlib's \texttt{EuclideanSpace.volume\_closedBall\_fin\_three}
gives $\vol\overline B(0,r)=r^3\cdot 4\pi/3$ (lemma \texttt{vol\_closedBall}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[$D_n$, similar copies, optimality]
\texttt{Dlat n} $=\{x\in\Z^n:\sum x_i\text{ even}\}$. Wikipedia (Root system): ``The Dn root lattice
-- that is, the lattice generated by the Dn roots -- consists of all integer vectors whose components
sum to an even integer.'' \texttt{IsSimilarCopy M Q}: $Q=\{c\,\varphi(x):x\in M\}$ as sets, for some
real $c\neq 0$ and linear isometry $\varphi$. \texttt{DCoveringOptimal n}: there is a $\Z$-submodule
$Q$ that is a similar copy of $D_n$ with $\Theta(Q)\le\Theta(L)$ for every lattice $L$ of $\R^n$.
\end{definition}

The body-centred cubic lattice \texttt{bcc} is the $\Z$-span of $e_1,e_2,h=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$;
Lean proves $\bcc=\{z+\tfrac e2(1,1,1): z\in\Z^3, e\in\Z\}=\Z^3\cup(\Z^3+h)$ (\texttt{mem\_bcc\_iff}).

\section{Proof}

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{D3\_covers}, \texttt{D3\_deep\_hole}, \texttt{similar\_D3\_bounds}]\label{lem:D3}
(a) Every $y\in\R^3$ is within distance $2$ of $D_3$. (b) $\|(1,0,0)-m\|\ge 1$ for all $m\in D_3$.
(c) If $Q=c\,\varphi(D_3)$ then $\mu(Q)\ge|c|$ and $\covol Q=2|c|^3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) Take $m_i=2\,\mathrm{round}(y_i/2)$; then $\sum m_i$ is even and $|y_i-m_i|\le 1$, so
$\|y-m\|^2\le 3\le 4$. (b) Write $m=z\in\Z^3$ with $z_1+z_2+z_3$ even. If $z_2\ne0$ or $z_3\ne0$ then
$\|(1,0,0)-z\|^2\ge 1$; otherwise $z_1$ is even, so $z_1\ne 1$ and $(1-z_1)^2\ge1$.
(c) Distances scale: $\|c\varphi a-c\varphi b\|=|c|\,\|a-b\|$. By (a), balls of radius $2|c|$ around
$Q$ cover $\R^3$ (apply (a) to $\varphi^{-1}(c^{-1}x)$), so $\mu(Q)$ is a genuine infimum; by (b) the
point $c\varphi(1,0,0)$ has distance $\ge|c|$ from every point of $Q$, so every admissible radius is
$\ge|c|$. For the covolume, $D_3$ is the $\Z$-span of the rows of
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&1\\0&1&1\\0&0&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ (\texttt{Dlat\_eq\_span}), of
determinant $2$; $Q$ is the $\Z$-span of their images, of determinant $c^3\det\varphi\cdot 2$ with
$|\det\varphi|=1$ (\texttt{abs\_det\_isometry}), and $\covol(\Z\text{-span of a basis }b)=|\det b|$
(\texttt{covolume\_span}, from \texttt{ZLattice.covolume\_eq\_det\_mul\_measureReal}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{round\_pair}, \texttt{bcc\_covers}, \texttt{covolume\_bcc}]\label{lem:bcc}
Every $x\in\R^3$ is within distance $5/8$ of \texttt{bcc}, and $\covol(\bcc)=\tfrac12$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $y\in\R$ let $u=y-\lfloor y\rfloor\in[0,1)$. If $u\le\frac12$ take $a=b=\lfloor y\rfloor$: then
$(y-a)^2+(y-b-\frac12)^2=2u^2-u+\frac14\le\frac14$. Otherwise take $a=\lfloor y\rfloor+1$,
$b=\lfloor y\rfloor$: the sum is $2u^2-3u+\frac54\le\frac14$ since $(u-\frac12)(1-u)\ge0$. Apply this to
each coordinate of $x$ to get $A=(a_i)\in\Z^3$ and $B=(b_i+\frac12)\in\Z^3+h$, both in \texttt{bcc}
(\texttt{mem\_bcc}), with $\|x-A\|^2+\|x-B\|^2\le\frac34$. Hence one of them is at squared distance
$\le\frac38\le\frac{25}{64}$. The basis matrix with rows $e_1,e_2,h$ has determinant $\frac12$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{coveringDensity\_bcc\_le}, \texttt{coveringDensity\_similar\_D3\_ge},
\texttt{coveringDensity\_bcc\_lt\_similar\_D3}]
$\Theta(\bcc)\le\frac{125\pi}{192}<\frac{128\pi}{192}=\frac{2\pi}{3}\le\Theta(Q)$ for every similar
copy $Q$ of $D_3$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:bcc}, $0\le\mu(\bcc)\le\frac58$, so $\Theta(\bcc)\le\frac{(5/8)^3\cdot 4\pi/3}{1/2}=\frac{125\pi}{192}$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:D3}(c), $\Theta(Q)=\frac{\mu(Q)^3\cdot 4\pi/3}{2|c|^3}\ge\frac{|c|^3\cdot4\pi/3}{2|c|^3}=\frac{2\pi}{3}$.
\end{proof}

Taking $c=1$, $\varphi=\mathrm{id}$ gives $\Theta(\bcc)<\Theta(D_3)$. If some similar copy $Q$ of
$D_3$ satisfied $\Theta(Q)\le\Theta(L)$ for every lattice $L$, then $L=\bcc$ would contradict the
theorem. So $D_3$ is not covering-optimal, and (A) fails. This is consistent with the literature:
Sch\"urmann--Vallentin write ``The optimality of the body centered cubic lattice [\dots] was first
proven by Bambah [Bam54b]''. That result is not used; the argument above is self-contained.

\title{Disproof of Conjecture 00000007672:\\
$\gcd(F_{55},F_{11})=1$ and $\gcd(F_6,F_3)=1$ in $\mathbb Z[q]$}
\author{jilint777}
\date{2026-10-04}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Conjecture 00000007672 asserts that for the $q$-Fibonacci polynomials
$F_n(q)=\sum_j\qbinom{n-1-j}{j}q^{j^2}$, the greatest common divisor
$\gcd(F_n,F_m)$ in $\mathbb Z[q]$ equals $F_{\gcd(n,m)}$ times a correction
factor. We give two counterexamples. First, $\gcd(F_6,F_3)=1$ in
$\mathbb Z[q]$ while $F_{\gcd(6,3)}=F_3=1+q$. Second, and robust under every reading in which the
exceptional set $E$ depends only on primes $p\equiv\pm2\pmod5$:
$\gcd(F_{55},F_{11})=1$ while
$F_{\gcd(11,55)}=F_{11}$ is a monic polynomial of degree $25$ with no
cyclotomic factor. Here all prime factors of $11$ and $55$ are $5$ or $11$,
none $\equiv\pm2\pmod 5$. Lean~4 (core library only) proves the $(6,3)$
counterexample completely, including the gcd computation, and proves that
$F_{11}$ does not divide $F_{55}$. The facts that $\gcd(F_{55},F_{11})=1$ and
that $F_{11}$ has no cyclotomic factor are certified computationally. Finally,
$F_d\nmid F_{5d}$ for every odd $d\ge11$ with $d\ne15$, so the failures cannot be
confined to finitely many indices either.
\end{abstract}

\section{The conjecture}

The statement (English version) reads:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Definition:} The $q$-Fibonacci polynomials are
$F_n(q)=\sum\,[n-1-j \text{ choose } j]_q\, q^{j^2}$, with Gaussian binomial
coefficients. \emph{Conjecture:} $\gcd(F_n(q),F_m(q))$ in $\mathbb Z[q]$ equals
$F_{\gcd(n,m)}(q)$ times a correction factor from a finite exceptional set $E$
depending only on primes $p\equiv\pm2 \pmod 5$, and the count of
$E\cap[1,x]$ is $O(\log\log x)$.
\end{quote}
The Chinese version says the same thing. The conjecture is a conjunction, so
refuting its first clause refutes it. That clause is:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:clause}
\gcd(F_n,F_m)=F_{\gcd(n,m)}\cdot c_{n,m}\qquad\text{for some polynomial } c_{n,m}.
\end{equation}
The ``correction factor'' multiplies a polynomial and is supposed to yield the
gcd, which lies in $\mathbb Z[q]$. So it is a polynomial, in $\mathbb Z[q]$ or at
least in $\mathbb Q[q]$. Our argument works for $c_{n,m}\in\mathbb Q[q]$ too
(Remark~\ref{rem:Q}). If $c_{n,m}$ were allowed to be an arbitrary rational
function, \eqref{eq:clause} would hold for any polynomial in place of
$\gcd(F_n,F_m)$ and would say nothing.

\paragraph{The exceptional set $E$.} The phrase ``a correction factor from a
finite exceptional set $E$ depending only on primes $p\equiv\pm2\pmod5$'' is
vague. A charitable reading lets the correction factor divide out a few
cyclotomic factors $\Phi_p(q)$, or integer factors $p$, attached to finitely
many exceptional primes $p\equiv\pm2\pmod 5$, or excuses finitely many
exceptional indices. Under that reading the counterexample $(6,3)$ could be
explained away. There the missing factor is $1+q=\Phi_2(q)$, and $2\equiv2\pmod5$.
The counterexample $(11,55)$ of Section~\ref{sec:11} is not affected. Its
indices involve only the primes $5$ and $11$. The missing factor is all of
$F_{11}$, which has degree $25$ and no cyclotomic factor. No correction built
from finitely many primes or cyclotomic polynomials can turn $F_{11}$ into
$\gcd(F_{55},F_{11})=1$.

\section{Definitions and small values}

The Gaussian binomial coefficient is
$\qbinom{n}{k}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1-q^{n-k+i}}{1-q^{i}}$ for $0\le k\le n$, and
$0$ otherwise. It satisfies the $q$-Pascal rule
$\qbinom{n+1}{k+1}=\qbinom{n}{k}+q^{k+1}\qbinom{n}{k+1}$. Only the terms with
$0\le j\le n-1-j$ contribute to $F_n$. We get
\begin{align*}
F_3&=\qbinom{2}{0}+\qbinom{1}{1}q=1+q,\\
F_6&=\qbinom{5}{0}+\qbinom{4}{1}q+\qbinom{3}{2}q^4
 =1+(q+q^2+q^3+q^4)+(q^4+q^5+q^6)\\
&=1+q+q^2+q^3+2q^4+q^5+q^6 .
\end{align*}
As a sanity check, $F_n(1)=\sum_j\binom{n-1-j}{j}$ is the Fibonacci number
$1,1,2,3,5,8,\dots$. These are the classical $q$-Fibonacci polynomials of
Schur, Carlitz and Andrews \cite{carlitz,andrews}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:values}
$F_3(-1)=0$ and $F_6(-1)=2$. Moreover $F_6=F_3\cdot(-1+2q-q^2+2q^3+q^5)+2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Direct computation. $F_6(-1)=1-1+1-1+2-1+1=2$.
\end{proof}

\section{\texorpdfstring{The gcd of $F_6$ and $F_3$ in $\mathbb Z[q]$}{The gcd of F6 and F3 in Z[q]}}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:gcd}
$1$ is a greatest common divisor of $F_6$ and $F_3$ in $\mathbb Z[q]$. That is,
every $D\in\mathbb Z[q]$ dividing both $F_6$ and $F_3$ divides $1$, i.e.\
$D=\pm1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $DA=F_3=1+q$ and $DB=F_6$ with $A,B\in\mathbb Z[q]$. Degrees add in
$\mathbb Z[q]$, because the top coefficient of a product is the product of the
top coefficients and $\mathbb Z$ has no zero divisors. Hence
$\deg D+\deg A=1$, so $D$ or $A$ is constant.

If $A=a$ is constant, then $D(0)a=1$ gives $a\ne0$, and $D(-1)a=F_3(-1)=0$
gives $D(-1)=0$. But then $F_6(-1)=D(-1)B(-1)=0$, contradicting
$F_6(-1)=2$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:values}).

If $D=d$ is constant, then $d\cdot A(0)=1$ forces $d=\pm1$, so $D\cdot d=1$
and $D\mid 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
For $(n,m)=(6,3)$ the clause \eqref{eq:clause} fails. Conjecture 00000007672 is
false.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\gcd(6,3)=3$ and $F_3=1+q$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:gcd} the gcd of $F_6$ and
$F_3$ in $\mathbb Z[q]$ is $\pm1$, which is unique up to units. If
$\pm1=(1+q)\,c$ for a polynomial $c$, then evaluating at $q=-1$ gives
$\pm1=0$, a contradiction. More generally, \emph{no} divisor $G$ of $F_6$ is a
multiple of $F_3$. If $G=F_3c$ and $GH=F_6$, then
$2=F_6(-1)=F_3(-1)c(-1)H(-1)=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:Q}
The same evaluation argument works if the correction factor $c$ is allowed to
lie in $\mathbb Q[q]$, or even to be any rational function without a pole at
$q=-1$. Over $\mathbb Q[q]$ the gcd is also $1$, because $F_6\equiv 2 \pmod{1+q}$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The family $(3,3k)$]
The polynomials $F_n$ satisfy Schur's recurrence
$F_n=F_{n-1}+q^{n-2}F_{n-2}$ for $n\ge3$ \cite{andrews}. To see this, apply
$\qbinom{N}{j}=\qbinom{N-1}{j}+q^{N-j}\qbinom{N-1}{j-1}$ with $N=n-1-j$. The
first terms sum to $F_{n-1}$. In the second, put $i=j-1$: the exponent
$j^2+(n-1-2j)$ becomes $i^2+n-2$, so these terms sum to $q^{n-2}F_{n-2}$.
Put $f_n=F_n(-1)$, so $f_0=0$ and $f_1=f_2=1$. Then
$f_{2k}=f_{2k-1}+f_{2k-2}$ and $f_{2k+1}=f_{2k}-f_{2k-1}=f_{2k-2}$. Hence
$f_{2k}=f_{2k-2}+f_{2k-4}$ for $k\ge2$, with $f_2=f_4=1$, so
$f_{2k}=\mathrm{Fib}_k$ and $f_{2k+1}=\mathrm{Fib}_{k-1}$. In particular
$f_n>0$ for all $n\ge1$ with $n\ne3$. Thus $1+q=F_3$ divides no $F_{3k}$ with
$k\ge2$, and the argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} refutes \eqref{eq:clause}
for every pair $(3,3k)$, $k\ge2$. (Lean checks $f_{3k}$ for $k=2,\dots,6$.) All
of these pairs share the missing factor $\Phi_2=1+q$, so this family alone
does not rule out the cyclotomic reading of $E$. The next section does.
\end{remark}

\section{A counterexample avoiding the exceptional primes}\label{sec:11}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:11}
$\gcd(11,55)=11$, and no divisor of $F_{55}$ in $\mathbb Z[q]$ is a multiple of
$F_{11}$. In fact $\gcd(F_{55},F_{11})=1$ in $\mathbb Z[q]$, while
\[
F_{11}=1+q+q^2+q^3+2q^4+2q^5+3q^6+3q^7+4q^8+5q^9+5q^{10}+5q^{11}+6q^{12}+\dots+q^{25}
\]
is monic of degree $25$ and is divisible by no cyclotomic polynomial.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
From the definition, $F_{11}(-1)=3$ and $F_{55}(-1)=121393=\mathrm{Fib}_{26}$
(also from $f_{2k+1}=\mathrm{Fib}_{k-1}$ above). If $G=F_{11}c$ and $GH=F_{55}$
with $c,H\in\mathbb Z[q]$, then $121393=3\,c(-1)H(-1)$. This is impossible
because $121393\equiv1\pmod 3$. Since $c\in\mathbb Q[q]$ with $F_{11}c\in\mathbb
Z[q]$ forces $c\in\mathbb Z[q]$ by Gauss's lemma ($F_{11}$ is monic), the same
holds over $\mathbb Q[q]$.

The remaining statements are computational and are checked in
\texttt{verify.py}. Both $F_{11}$ and $F_{55}$ are monic, so a nontrivial common
factor over $\mathbb Q$ would be a monic integer polynomial, and its reduction
modulo the prime $2^{61}-1$ would divide both reductions. The Euclidean
algorithm modulo $2^{61}-1$ gives a gcd of degree $0$, so
$\gcd(F_{55},F_{11})=1$. A cyclotomic factor $\Phi_k$ of $F_{11}$ would need
$\varphi(k)\le25$, hence $k\le1250$ (since $\varphi(k)\ge\sqrt{k/2}$). None of
the $53$ such $\Phi_k$ divides $F_{11}$.
\end{proof}

Thus for $(n,m)=(11,55)$ the gcd is $1=F_{11}\cdot c$ only for $c=1/F_{11}$,
which is not a polynomial. It is also not a correction made of finitely many
primes $\equiv\pm2\pmod5$ or of their cyclotomic factors. Failures are common:
$F_d\nmid F_{2d}$ for every $3\le d\le30$ except $d=5$.

\begin{remark}[Infinitely many indices]\label{rem:5d}
Even a reading in which $E$ is an arbitrary finite set of exceptional
indices does not rescue the conjecture. Let $d=2a+1$ be odd. By the formulas
above, $F_d(-1)=\mathrm{Fib}_{a-1}$ and $F_{5d}(-1)=F_{2(5a+2)+1}(-1)=\mathrm{Fib}_{5a+1}$.
For $a-1\ge3$ we have $\mathrm{Fib}_{a-1}\mid\mathrm{Fib}_{5a+1}$ if and only if
$(a-1)\mid(5a+1)$, which holds if and only if $(a-1)\mid 6$. So for every odd
$d\ge11$ with $d\ne15$, $F_d(-1)\nmid F_{5d}(-1)$. Hence no divisor of $F_{5d}$ is a
multiple of $F_d=F_{\gcd(d,5d)}$, and \eqref{eq:clause} fails for $(d,5d)$. This
gives infinitely many failures with unbounded $\gcd$, including every prime
$d\equiv\pm1\pmod5$. (\texttt{verify.py} checks this for odd $d<200$.)
\end{remark}

\section{Lean formalization}

The directory \texttt{lean4/} is a self-contained Lean~4.19.0 project that uses
only the core library (no Mathlib).
\begin{itemize}
\item Polynomials in $\mathbb Z[q]$ are coefficient lists. \texttt{PEq} means
equal coefficients, \texttt{Dvd a b} is $\exists c,\ a\cdot c=b$ in $\mathbb Z[q]$,
and \texttt{IsGcd G a b} is the standard definition of a greatest common
divisor (a common divisor that every common divisor divides).
\item \texttt{gauss} defines Gaussian binomials by the $q$-Pascal rule.
\texttt{gauss\_product\_formula} checks
$\qbinom{n}{k}(q;q)_k(q;q)_{n-k}=(q;q)_n$ for all $k\le n\le7$.
\texttt{qfib n} is $\sum_{j<n}\qbinom{n-1-j}{j}q^{j^2}$ exactly as in the
conjecture. \texttt{qfib\_at\_one} checks $F_n(1)=\mathrm{Fib}_n$ for $n\le15$.
\item \texttt{common\_divisor\_dvd\_one} and \texttt{isGcd\_one} formalize
Proposition~\ref{prop:gcd}. They use the degree argument
(\texttt{coeff\_mulL\_top}, \texttt{const\_or\_const}) and evaluation
(\texttt{evalL\_mulL}, \texttt{evalL\_congr}).
\item \texttt{GcdClause} is the clause \eqref{eq:clause}: for all $n,m\ge1$ and
every gcd $G$ of $F_n,F_m$ in $\mathbb Z[q]$, $G=F_{\gcd(n,m)}\cdot c$ for some
$c\in\mathbb Z[q]$. \texttt{conjecture\_00000007672\_false} proves
$\neg$\texttt{GcdClause} using $(6,3)$.
\texttt{no\_divisor\_of\_F6\_is\_multiple\_of\_F3} proves the stronger statement
from the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\item Since a gcd in $\mathbb Z[q]$ always exists and is unique up to sign, the
universal form (``every gcd is $F_{\gcd(n,m)}\cdot c$'') and the existential
form (``some gcd is $F_{\gcd(n,m)}\cdot c$'') of \eqref{eq:clause} are
equivalent. The Lean project refutes both.
\texttt{GcdClauseExists} is the existential form: for all $n,m\ge1$ there
are a gcd $G$ and $c\in\mathbb Z[q]$ with $G=F_{\gcd(n,m)}\cdot c$.
\texttt{conjecture\_00000007672\_false\_exists\_6\_3} and
\texttt{conjecture\_00000007672\_false\_11\_55} prove
$\neg$\texttt{GcdClauseExists} using $(6,3)$ and $(11,55)$; the latter goes via
\texttt{no\_divisor\_of\_F55\_is\_multiple\_of\_F11}. $F_{55}(-1)$ is computed
with a row-by-row evaluation of Gaussian binomials (\texttt{row},
\texttt{fastF}), proved equal to evaluating the polynomial
(\texttt{evalL\_qfib}).
\item Lean takes the correction factor in $\mathbb Z[q]$. The extension to
$\mathbb Q[q]$ (Gauss's lemma) and the facts that $\gcd(F_{55},F_{11})=1$ and that
$F_{11}$ has no cyclotomic factor are shown in this report and in
\texttt{verify.py}, not in Lean.
\end{itemize}
\texttt{lake build} succeeds. There is no \texttt{sorry}, no
\texttt{native\_decide}, and no added axioms. \texttt{\#print axioms} shows at
most \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}. The
numerical facts are proved by \texttt{decide}. \texttt{qfib\_mult3\_at\_neg\_one}
and \texttt{qfib\_55\_at\_neg\_one} use \texttt{decide +kernel}, which is
checked by the Lean kernel.

\section{Reproduction}
\begin{verbatim}
cd lean4 && lake build       # Lean 4.19.0, core only
python3 verify.py            # standard library only
pdflatex report.tex
\end{verbatim}
\texttt{verify.py} computes Gaussian binomials from the product formula by exact
division, which is independent of the $q$-Pascal rule used in Lean. It
recomputes $F_n$, finds $\gcd(F_6,F_3)=1$ by the Euclidean algorithm in
$\mathbb Q[q]$ (and in $\mathbb Z[q]$ by Gauss's lemma, since $F_3$ is
primitive), and lists all $76$ pairs $n<m\le30$ for which
$F_{\gcd(n,m)}\nmid F_n$ or $F_{\gcd(n,m)}\nmid F_m$. It also performs all the
checks for $(11,55)$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:11} and verifies Schur's recurrence
for $n\le30$.

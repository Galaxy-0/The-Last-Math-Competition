\title{Infinitely many quadratic PCF polynomials\\
  A disproof of the first assertion of conjecture 00000007755}
\author{earthking11}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

The first assertion of conjecture 00000007755 says that, for each fixed degree
$d$, there are only finitely many postcritically finite (PCF) polynomials with
algebraic coefficients. Here a polynomial is PCF when every critical point is
preperiodic under iteration. We disprove this assertion in degree $2$.

Let $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ be an algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q$. For each
$a\in\overline{\mathbb Q}$, put
\[
  f_a(X)=(X-a)^2+a=X^2-2aX+(a^2+a).
\]
This polynomial has degree $2$ and all its coefficients are algebraic. Its
derivative is $f_a'(X)=2(X-a)$, so its only critical point in
$\overline{\mathbb Q}$ is $a$. Moreover $f_a(a)=a$: the critical point is fixed,
and hence preperiodic. Therefore $f_a$ is PCF.

The polynomials are pairwise distinct. Indeed, $f_a=f_b$ implies equality of
their derivatives. Evaluating at $a$ gives $0=f_b'(a)=2(a-b)$, so $a=b$.
Because $\overline{\mathbb Q}$ is infinite, the set of quadratic PCF
polynomials with algebraic coefficients is infinite. This contradicts the
first assertion of the conjecture, and thus disproves the conjunction as
written. No claim about the separate height asymptotic is necessary.

\paragraph{Formalization.}
The Lean file defines $K=\operatorname{AlgebraicClosure}(\mathbb Q)$,
$f_a\in K[X]$, preperiodicity as repetition in the evaluation orbit, and PCF
using all roots of the formal derivative in $K$. It proves the degree,
critical-point identity, PCF property, and injectivity of $a\mapsto f_a$.
The final theorem \texttt{TLMC7755.infinite\_quadratic\_pcf} states that the
actual set of degree-$2$ PCF polynomials in $K[X]$ is infinite. Its axiom audit
contains only \texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, and
\texttt{Quot.sound}.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The conjecture quantifies over polynomials, without taking affine-conjugacy
classes or imposing a monic-centered normalization. All the examples above
are affine conjugate to $X^2$; this argument does not settle a differently
worded finiteness assertion modulo affine conjugacy.

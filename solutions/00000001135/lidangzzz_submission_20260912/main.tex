\begin{abstract}
We disprove the divisibility clause of a conjecture of the Last Math Competition on the Betti-number generating functions of Bott--Samelson varieties: the conjecture asserts that for a reduced word $s_1\cdots s_k$ the generating polynomial $P(t)$ is divisible by $(1+t)^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}$ with monic quotient. For the reduced word $(2,1,3,2,3)$ of the smooth permutation $w = 3421 \in S_4$, the Betti generating function is $P(t) = 1+3t+5t^2+5t^3+3t^4+t^5$ (via the Schubert basis of the smooth Schubert variety $X_w$, using $H^*(BS(\mathbf{s})) \cong H^*(X_w)$), and $P(t) = (1+t)(1+2t+3t^2+2t^3+t^4)$ with the quotient equal to $1 \ne 0$ at $t = -1$: $(1+t)^2 = (1+t)^{\lfloor 5/2\rfloor}$ does not divide $P$. The subword computation, the reducedness of the word, the pattern-avoidance smoothness of $w$, and the polynomial arithmetic are all kernel-verified in Lean~4.
\end{abstract}
\section{The conjecture}
\begin{conjecture}[TLMC-00000001135]\label{conj:1135}
For $P(t) = \sum b_{2i}t^i$ the Betti generating function of $BS(s_1,\dots,s_k)$: the coefficients of $P$ are strictly increasing up to the middle level, $P$ is divisible by $(1+t)^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}$ with monic quotient, and the quotient has no internal zeros when $s_1\cdots s_k$ is a reduced word.
\end{conjecture}
\section{The disproof}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:1135}
Conjecture~\ref{conj:1135} is \textbf{false} in its divisibility clause. For the reduced word $\mathbf{s} = (2,1,3,2,3)$ (a reduced word of $w = 3421 \in S_4$, which avoids the patterns $4231$ and $3412$, so $X_w$ is smooth),
\[
P(t) \;=\; \sum_j \#\{v \le w : \ell(v) = j\}\, t^j \;=\; 1 + 3t + 5t^2 + 5t^3 + 3t^4 + t^5,
\]
and $(1+t)^2 \nmid P(t)$ although $\lfloor k/2 \rfloor = 2$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since $\mathbf{s}$ is a reduced word of $w$, the Bott--Samelson resolution $BS(\mathbf{s}) \to X_w$ is birational with rationally singular target, giving $H^*(BS(\mathbf{s})) \cong H^*(X_w)$; as $X_w$ is smooth its cohomology has the Schubert basis $\{[X_v] : v \le w\}$, so $b_j = \#\{v \le w\ \text{with a reduced subword of } \mathbf{s},\ \ell(v) = j\}$. Enumerating the 32 subwords (kernel-verified in Lean: \texttt{length\_profile}) gives the six counts $1,3,5,5,3,1$. Then
\[
P(t) = (1+t)\underbrace{(1+2t+3t^2+2t^3+t^4)}_{Q(t)}, \qquad Q(-1) = 1 \ne 0,
\]
so $(1+t)^2 \nmid P$. (The strict-increase clause $1 < 3 < 5$ holds here; the failure is exactly the divisibility clause.)
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
The identification $H^*(BS(\mathbf{s})) \cong H^*(X_w)$ for reduced $\mathbf{s}$ and the Schubert basis of smooth Schubert varieties are classical theorems cited here; the reducedness of the word, the smoothness of $3421$ (pattern avoidance), the subword enumeration, and the divisibility arithmetic are all machine-checked (\texttt{Batch10.lean}, zero errors/warnings, standard axioms only, no \texttt{sorry}).
\end{remark}

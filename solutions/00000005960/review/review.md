## Semantic audit
The official conjecture asserts, in both languages, the **existence** of two ensembles with the same eigenvalue spectrum but different eigenvector statistics, with the separation realized by an explicit free parametrization that preserves the spectrum while changing orientation. The submission proves exactly this, in an explicitly witnessed and mildly strengthened form. The parametrization is M(t) = Q(t)·diag(1,3)·Q(t)ᵀ with the rational rotation Q(t) built from the half-angle substitution c(t) = (1−t²)/(1+t²), s(t) = 2t/(1+t²) (denominator strictly positive for all real t, so the family is defined and continuous on all of ℝ). Orthogonality of Q(t) is proved from c²+s²=1, and the characteristic polynomial identity det(XI−M(t)) = (X−1)(X−3) follows either by orthogonal conjugation or, as in Lean, from trace 4 and determinant 3; each eigenvalue is simple. Injectivity on [0,1] is proved: the (0,0) entry is 3−2c(t)², c ≥ 0 on [0,1], and c(t)=c(r) reduces to (1−t²)(1+r²)=(1−r²)(1+t²), giving t=r. So the family genuinely changes orientation while preserving the spectrum — the conjecture's "explicit free parametrization" clause.

The two ensembles are fair two-point laws: A on {M(0), M(1)} = {diag(1,3), diag(3,1)} and B on {M(1/2), M(−1/2)} = (1/25)·{[[57,−24],[−24,43]], [[57,24],[24,43]]}, each proved nonconstant (non-Dirac). Their spectral laws are identical — in fact both deterministic: Law(spectrum∘A) = Law(spectrum∘B) = δ_{{1,3}}, and likewise for the characteristic polynomial. The eigenvector statistic is S(N) = ‖Π_{E₁(N)} e₀‖², the squared Euclidean norm of the orthogonal projection of the fixed coordinate vector onto the eigenvalue-1 eigenspace of N. This is intrinsic (no eigenvector sign choice), and the file proves it equals the squared first coordinate of **any** unit eigenvector for that simple eigenvalue (`statistic_eq_normalized_eigenvector`), matching the standard squared-modulus eigenvector observables of the eigenvector-distribution literature. Since E₁(M(t)) = ℝ·(c(t), s(t)) (proved: the eigenvector is exhibited, and every eigenvector is a multiple via a hands-on linear-algebra lemma), S(M(t)) = c(t)², giving S(A(0))=1, S(A(1))=0, S(B(0))=S(B(1))=9/25. Hence Law(S∘A) = ½δ₀ + ½δ₁ versus Law(S∘B) = δ_{9/25}, which differ (Lean exhibits the separating point 0, with masses ½ and 0), while the spectrum laws coincide.

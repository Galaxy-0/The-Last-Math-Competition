\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Definition:} the elasticity $\rho(R)$ is the ratio of longest
to shortest factorizations. \textit{Conjecture:} for imaginary
quadratic integer rings $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-d}]$, $\rho$ has an
explicit table of the type (smallest prime factor of $d$).
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

Take $d = 6$ and $d = 14$. Both are even, and $2$ is the smallest
prime, so $\mathrm{spf}(6) = \mathrm{spf}(14) = 2$. Both
$\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-6}]$ and $\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-14}]$ are full rings of
integers (discriminants $-24$ and $-56$). The reduced positive-definite
binary quadratic forms give the class numbers
\[
h(-24) = 2:\quad (1,0,6),\ (2,0,3);
\qquad
h(-56) = 4:\quad (1,0,14),\ (2,0,7),\ (3,2,5),\ (3,-2,5),
\]
the form $(3,2,5)$ having $0 < |b| < a < c$ and hence a distinct
inverse class $(3,-2,5)$. By Carlitz's theorem (1960), a ring of
integers is half-factorial iff its class number is at most $2$:
\[
\rho(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-6}]) = 1 \ne \rho(\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-14}]) > 1 .
\]
A function of $\mathrm{spf}(d)$ takes equal values at $6$ and $14$;
$\rho$ does not. The conjectured table does not exist.

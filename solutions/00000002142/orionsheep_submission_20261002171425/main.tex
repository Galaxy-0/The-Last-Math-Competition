\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} The number of mates with parameters $(460,153,32,57)$
is explicit; and the kernel of the number is a biplane count.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

Every strongly regular graph with parameters $(v,k,\lambda,\mu)$ satisfies
the classical two-path identity
\[
  k\,(k-\lambda-1) \;=\; (v-k-1)\,\mu,
\]
obtained by counting, from a fixed vertex $x$, the length-2 paths to the
vertices non-adjacent to $x$: each of the $k$ neighbors of $x$ has
$k-\lambda-1$ further neighbors, and each of the $v-k-1$ non-neighbors of
$x$ is reached exactly $\mu$ times.

At $(460,153,32,57)$:
\[
  153\cdot 120 = 18360 \;\ne\; 17442 = 306 \cdot 57 .
\]
The necessary identity fails, so \textbf{no} SRG$(460,153,32,57)$ exists:
there are no cospectral mates with these parameters, and the conjectured
explicit count with its biplane kernel has no subject matter.

\section{Boundary}

Literal layer: the mate count is $0$ --- no positive explicit enumeration or
biplane kernel exists. Definitional layer: the generator selected an
infeasible parameter set, a pathology for \texttt{well\_definedness\_level}
(cf.\ \#154).

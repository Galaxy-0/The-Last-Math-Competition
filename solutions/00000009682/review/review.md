## Semantic audit

The conjecture is a conjunction (C1 ∧ C2 ∧ C3). The submission refutes C3 — 'the dimension of algebraically independent subsets of U numbers is exactly 1, with no two-dimensional U families' — by constructing two algebraically independent U-numbers, over ℝ and over ℂ. The witness x is `liouvilleNumber 10`; the proof that it is a U-number is from scratch: m(x,n,H) is the minimum of a finite nonempty set of positive values, the good Liouville approximations b (with b ≥ max(N, 2, ⌈|x|⌉+1)) give polynomials bX − a of degree ≤ 1 and height ≤ H = max(b, |a|) with |P(x)| < b^{1−k}, so −log m > (k−1)·log b while log H ≤ 2 log b, whence ω(x,1,H) ≥ (k−1)/2 along arbitrarily large H and ω(x,1) = ⊤. The second witness y is picked residually (Baire): Liouville numbers are comeager, and the elements of ℝ (resp. ℂ) algebraic over the countable field ℚ(x) are countable, so a residual set of y is simultaneously Liouville and transcendental over ℚ(x); `{x, y}` is then algebraically independent over ℚ by the insert-into-transcendental lemma.

The Mahler classification is formalized faithfully from the standard reference (m, ω(x,n,H), ω(x,n) as limsup in EReal, U = transcendental with some ω(x,n) = ∞ for n ≥ 1, S = transcendental with all ω(x,n) bounded). 'No two-dimensional U families' is read as the nonexistence of a 2-element algebraically independent subset of U-numbers and 'dimension exactly 1' as the corresponding supremum equal to 1; both readings are the natural ones given the sentence, both are refuted separately (the same witness gives `encard = 2` and `2 ≤ iSup`), and the refutation is carried out for the real and the complex readings of Lebesgue measure. Since the conjecture is a conjunction, refuting C3 refutes it; C1 and C2 (which are classically true) are not used, as the report states. The honestly-noted unrefuted reading — a restriction to U-numbers of one fixed degree n ≥ 2 — is not part of the text.

I checked the key estimate (k−1)·log b / log H ≥ w by hand, including the |a| ≤ b(|x|+1) and H ≤ b(|x|+1) ≤ b² steps, and the insert lemma (an element transcendental over the field generated by an independent set preserves independence; y ≠ x because x is algebraic over ℚ[x]). Both the Liouville-subset-of-U conclusion and the genericity construction are classical and correctly machine-checked.

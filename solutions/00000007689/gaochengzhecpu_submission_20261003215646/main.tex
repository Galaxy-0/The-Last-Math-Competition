The conjecture asserts both $a_{r,s}=D(r,s)$, the Delannoy numbers, and
$a_{m,m}\sim c\,3^m m^{-1/2}$. These assertions are incompatible. The
contradiction does not depend on any interpretation of the Bailey sliding
construction: no array can satisfy the two asserted properties together.

\paragraph{A lower bound for all diagonal entries.}
The Delannoy numbers are characterized by
\[
 D(0,s)=D(r,0)=1,\qquad
 D(r+1,s+1)=D(r,s+1)+D(r+1,s)+D(r,s).
\]
They count paths using steps $(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)$, by conditioning on the
last step. They are nondecreasing in each coordinate. Set $x=D(r,s)$.
The recurrence and this monotonicity give
\[
 D(r+1,s+1)\ge3x,\qquad
 D(r+1,s+2)\ge5x,\qquad D(r+2,s+1)\ge5x.
\]
Thus $D(r+2,s+2)\ge13D(r,s)$, and induction gives
\[D(2n,2n)\ge13^n\qquad(n\ge0).\]

In fact, the diagonal is not even $O(3^m)$. For a completely elementary
estimate, induction on $n$ proves
\[(4n+9)9^n\le9\,13^n.\]
The induction step follows from
$9(4n+13)\le13(4n+9)$. If some integers $C,N\ge0$ satisfied
$D(m,m)\le C3^m$ for all $m\ge N$, take $n=9C+N+1$ and $m=2n$.
Then
\[(4n+9)9^n\le9\,13^n\le9C9^n,\]
so cancellation of the positive number $9^n$ gives $4n+9\le9C$,
contrary to the chosen $n$.

\paragraph{Why the stated asymptotic is excluded.}
The usual meaning of $a_{m,m}\sim c3^m/\sqrt m$ has a finite positive
constant $c$. For all sufficiently large $m$, it entails
$a_{m,m}\le2c3^m/\sqrt m\le2c3^m$. Choose an integer $C\ge2c$ and enlarge
the integer threshold $N$ to at least one. This gives precisely the
eventual bound just disproved. A nonpositive $c$ cannot supply an
asymptotic equivalent of the positive Delannoy diagonal either.

\paragraph{Formal verification and scope.}
Lean defines the full two-variable sequence by the displayed terminating
recurrence, and proves uniqueness among every array satisfying the two
boundary conditions and the recurrence. The monotonicity, block-growth
estimate, and diagonal lower bound are theorems for arbitrary natural
indices. There is no extrapolation from a finite table.

The theorem \texttt{no\_eventual\_three\_power\_bound} negates the existence
of every integer bound $C$ and every threshold $N$. The theorem
\texttt{conjecture7689\_counterexample} then negates, for any array $a$,
the conjunction of equality to Delannoy numbers and this necessary
eventual bound. The preceding paragraph supplies explicitly the elementary
real-asymptotic implication to that integer statement; the Lean file
formalizes its integer consequence rather than introducing a new or
nonstandard definition of asymptotic equivalence. The other assertion
about product-side factors is not needed to refute the conjunction.

The program uses Lean 4.19.0 and \texttt{Std} only, with no admitted lemmas,
custom axioms, or native evaluation proofs.

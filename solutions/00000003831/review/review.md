## Semantic audit
The conjecture's clause (C1) is a universally quantified statement over "any finite crystal" (对任意有限晶体). In the standard references (Kashiwara; Bump–Schilling Ch. 2) "crystal" is the abstract notion — seminormality and normality are *additional* conditions — so the literal reading includes the submission's `B₇`, which is moreover seminormal and connected, the strongest hypothesis short of normality. `B₇` arises from the honest highest-weight crystal `B(2,1,0)` (8 tableaux, character `s₂₁`) by identifying the two vertices of weight `(1,1,1)` (the tableaux `12/3` and `13/2`, which lie on `f₁`- and `f₂`-strings respectively) into a single vertex, keeping all eight edges. Every vertex still lies on a unique `i`-string per colour with the correct weight shifts, so Kashiwara's axioms hold with ε, φ the string lengths; the character loses one `x₁x₂x₃` term, giving `ch(B₇) = m₂₁ + m₁₁₁ = s₂₁ − s₁₁₁` with the `s₁₁₁`-coefficient `−1 < 0`. Since `ch(B₇)` is symmetric, the Weyl symmetrizer `J(x^ρ f)/J(x^ρ)` fixes it (proved for this `f` in Lean: `weylSym_B7`), the orbit sum multiplies by 6, and the average fixes it — every reading of "Weyl symmetrization" gives a negative coefficient.

The Lean formalization is faithful and robust. `ClaimWeyl` quantifies over all seminormal crystals and all `q` satisfying the defining equation `q·J(x^ρ) = J(x^ρ ch B)` and all degree-3 Schur expansions (sufficiency of degree 3 is legitimate: `ch(B₇)` is homogeneous of degree 3, and `schur3_indep` proves uniqueness of degree-3 expansions). Crucially, `weyl_forced` proves that *any* `q` satisfying the symmetrizer equation and *any* expansion is forced to coefficients `(0, 1, −1)`, so the counterexample does not depend on normalization choices in the informal statement.

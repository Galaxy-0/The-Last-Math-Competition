\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} the Julia dimensions of compositions of
polynomials satisfy $\dim J(f\circ g) \ge \max(\dim J(f), \dim
J(g))$, \emph{with strict inequality when $f, g$ are noncommuting};
the parameter conditions for the strict family are explicit.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

Take $f(z) = z^2$ and $g(z) = 3z^2$.  The monomial composition rule
$(c\,z^d)\circ(e\,z^k) = (c\,e^d)\,z^{dk}$ gives
\[
f\circ g = 9z^4 \ne 3z^4 = g\circ f :
\]
the two polynomials do \emph{not} commute (kernel-certified:
$(9,4) \ne (3,4)$).  But $z \mapsto a\,z^n$ leaves the circle
$|z| = |a|^{-1/(n-1)}$ completely invariant ($|a||z|^n = |z|
\iff |z|^{n-1} = |a|^{-1}$), so all four Julia sets are exact
circles: $J(z^2)$ ($|z| = 1$), $J(3z^2)$ ($|z| = 1/3$),
$J(9z^4)$ ($|z| = 9^{-1/3}$), $J(3z^4)$ ($|z| = 3^{-1/3}$), each of
Hausdorff and box dimension exactly $1$.  Hence
\[
\dim J(f\circ g) = \max(\dim J(f), \dim J(g)) = 1
\]
with \emph{no} strict inequality despite noncommutation --- the
conjecture's strict clause would demand $1 > 1$, which the kernel
certifies false.  The ``explicit parameter conditions'' for a strict
family cannot hold on this noncommuting pair; box-counting on the
analytic filled sets confirms $\approx 1$ for all four circles.

\section{Verification}

\texttt{reproduce.py} checks the symbolic noncommutation with
numerical spot values and box-counts the four Julia circles from
their analytic filled sets (two-scale max-pooling counts; all
$\approx 1$).  The Lean package (core Lean~4, v4.33.1) certifies the
monomial arithmetic: \texttt{fg} ($f\circ g = (9,4)$), \texttt{gf}
($g\circ f = (3,4)$), \texttt{noncommuting}, \texttt{not\_strict}
($\neg(1 > 1)$), and \texttt{conjecture\_refuted}.  All five audited
theorems report \texttt{does not depend on any axioms}.

\section{Boundary}

The kernel certifies the exact monomial compositions, their
inequality (noncommutation), and the failure of the strict dimension
inequality at the common value $1$; that $z \mapsto az^n$ has a
completely invariant circle of dimension $1$ is classical complex
dynamics, cited in prose and confirmed numerically.  The
strict-inequality clause is refuted; no claim is made about the
non-strict inequality in general.

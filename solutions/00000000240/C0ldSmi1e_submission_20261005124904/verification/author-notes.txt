## Polar

## Excluding both classes of linear images

Geometry240 proves a generic l1 unit ball is the convex hull of its signed coordinate axes. For any point x in that ball, weights |x_i| are assigned to signed axes, with remaining weight 1-sum_i|x_i| assigned to zero; zero itself is a convex combination of opposite axes. Every signed axis is extreme: saturation of a coordinate at +1 or -1 forces all other coordinates to vanish. Mathlib's extremePoints_convexHull_subset gives the converse. The signed-axis map from Bool × iota is proved injective, hence there are exactly 2*card(iota) extreme points.

The one-dimensional interval has exactly the two endpoints as extreme points. The product theorem for extreme points gives K exactly 2*6 = 12 extreme points. The cube's extreme points are proved to be all sign vectors, hence Cube has16; Cross has8.

The image exclusions quantify over every real linear map E -> E, without assuming it invertible. The theorem bijective_of_image_K first proves that a linear map whose image of any set is K has range with nonempty interior. A proper linear subspace cannot have nonempty interior, so its range is all E. In finite dimension, surjectivity implies injectivity. It therefore induces a linear equivalence, under which the set of extreme points is transported bijectively. The counts12 versus16 and8 give contradictions. There is no hidden nonsingularity assumption.

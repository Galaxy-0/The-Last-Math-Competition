\begin{abstract}
The product of a segment and a three-dimensional cross-polytope is a
four-dimensional origin-symmetric convex body with Mahler volume $32/3=4^4/4!$.
It has twelve extreme points, whereas a four-dimensional cube and
cross-polytope have sixteen and eight. Thus it attains the stated bound
without belonging to either claimed equality class. The accompanying Lean
project proves the actual body, polar, Lebesgue volumes and obstruction to
every linear image. The conclusion concerns the equality classification in
the repository's statement; it makes no claim to resolve the general
Mahler lower-bound problem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Statement and scope}
For a positive integer $n$ and an origin-symmetric convex body $S\subset\R^n$, let
\[
 S^\circ=\{y\in\R^n:\langle x,y\rangle\leq1\text{ for every }x\in S\},
 \qquad M(S)=\vol_n(S)\vol_n(S^\circ).
\]
A convex body here is compact, convex, and has nonempty interior in the
ambient space. Volume is ordinary Lebesgue volume, with unit coordinate
boxes having volume one. Put
\[
 C_n=[-1,1]^n,\qquad
 B_1^n=\left\{x\in\R^n:\sum_{i=1}^n|x_i|\leq1\right\}
       =\conv\{\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_n\}.
\]
The source asserts $M(S)\geq4^n/n!$ and gives only linear images of $C_n$
or $B_1^n$ as equality cases. The English text asserts the necessary
direction of this classification; the Chinese text states it as an
if-and-only-if. We refute their common necessary direction. A false
four-dimensional instance refutes either universal assertion, including its
conjunction with the lower bound. The source's parenthetical reference to
dimensions at most three does not restrict its general claim.

\section{The body and its polar}
Use coordinates $(s,x_1,x_2,x_3)$ and define
\[
 K=[-1,1]\times B_1^3
   =\{(s,x): |s|\leq1,\ |x_1|+|x_2|+|x_3|\leq1\}.
\]
Both factors are compact, convex and symmetric about zero. The open set
\[
 \{(s,x):|s|<1,\ |x_1|+|x_2|+|x_3|<1\}
\]
contains zero and lies in $K$. Consequently $K$ is a genuine
four-dimensional origin-symmetric convex body.

\begin{proposition}
The actual polar of $K$ is
\[
 P=\{(t,y):|t|+|y_i|\leq1\text{ for }i=1,2,3\}
   =\{(t,y):|t|+\max_i|y_i|\leq1\}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $(t,y)\in K^\circ$, test its defining inequality against
$(\operatorname{sgn}(t),\operatorname{sgn}(y_i)e_i)\in K$, choosing either
sign when a coordinate is zero. This gives $|t|+|y_i|\leq1$ for every $i$.

Conversely, suppose these inequalities hold. In particular $|t|\leq1$.
For $(s,x)\in K$,
\begin{align*}
 st+\sum_i x_i y_i
 &\leq |s||t|+\sum_i|x_i||y_i|\\
 &\leq |t|+(1-|t|)\sum_i|x_i|\leq1.
\end{align*}
Thus $(t,y)\in K^\circ$.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact Lebesgue volumes}
The three-dimensional cross-polytope consists of eight orthant simplices,
each of volume
\[
 \int_0^1\int_0^{1-u}\int_0^{1-u-v}dw\,dv\,du=\frac16.
\]
Intersections along coordinate planes have zero three-dimensional measure.
Hence $\vol_3(B_1^3)=4/3$ and the product formula gives
\[
 \vol_4(K)=2\cdot\frac43=\frac83.
\]
The Lean proof obtains the same actual cross-polytope volume from Mathlib's
proved formula for the volume of $\ell_p$ balls, at $p=1$ and dimension
three, with the Gamma factors evaluated exactly.

At a fixed $t$ with $|t|\leq1$, the section of $P$ is the coordinate cube
\[
 [-(1-|t|),\,1-|t|]^3,
\]
of volume $8(1-|t|)^3$. If $|t|>1$, that section is empty.
The set $P$ is closed and therefore measurable. The product-measure
formula, followed by an ordinary integral on a compact interval, yields
\[
 \vol_4(P)=\int_{-1}^1 8(1-|t|)^3\,dt
          =16\int_0^1(1-t)^3\,dt=4.
\]
In particular both volume factors are finite and positive. Since $P=K^\circ$,
\[
 \boxed{\ M(K)=\frac83\cdot4=\frac{32}{3}
                   =\frac{4^4}{4!}.\ }
\]

\section{Excluding both classes of linear images}
The extreme points of $B_1^n$ are exactly the $2n$ signed coordinate
vectors. Here is a direct justification. The inequality defining $B_1^n$
is convex. Every $x\in B_1^n$ is the convex combination
\[
 x=\sum_i |x_i|\bigl(\operatorname{sgn}(x_i)e_i\bigr)
        +\left(1-\sum_i|x_i|\right)0,
\]
and zero itself is the midpoint of any opposite pair of coordinate vectors.
Thus $B_1^n$ is their convex hull. Each signed coordinate vector is extreme:
a convex combination attaining its coordinate value $1$ or $-1$ forces
that value in both summands, and the $\ell_1$ bound forces their remaining
coordinates to vanish. A finite convex hull has no other extreme points.

The interval $[-1,1]$ has precisely the extreme points $-1$ and $1$.
Extreme points of a Cartesian product are the products of extreme points.
Consequently
\[
 |\ext K|=2\cdot6=12,\qquad
 |\ext C_4|=2^4=16,\qquad
 |\ext B_1^4|=2\cdot4=8.
\]
For the cube, the product rule equivalently says that every coordinate of
an extreme point must be an endpoint of its interval.

Every invertible linear map carries extreme points bijectively onto the
extreme points of the image. Thus $K$ is neither an invertible linear
image of $C_4$ nor of $B_1^4$. Allowing arbitrary linear maps does not
enlarge the possible image class here: if $T(S)=K$, then the linear
subspace $\operatorname{range}T$ contains a set with nonempty interior.
It must be all of $\R^4$. A surjective endomorphism of a finite-dimensional
space is invertible. This also rules out every singular map.

The equality and the two non-image conclusions contradict the necessary
equality condition asserted in both language versions. Therefore
conjecture 00000000240, with its stated equality classification, is false.

\section{Formalization and reproducible checks}
The Lean project uses Lean 4.19.0 and Mathlib v4.19.0, with the exact
dependency revisions recorded in its manifest. Its ambient space is
$E=\R\times(\operatorname{Fin}3\to\R)$, with ordinary product
Lebesgue measure. The four coordinates are indexed by
\texttt{Option (Fin 3)}: the distinguished coordinate is the first factor,
and the other three are the second factor. The code proves the ambient
dimension, expands the coordinate map and Euclidean dot product, and
identifies the transported cube and cross-polytope with the inequalities
used above. The coordinate equivalence is also proved to preserve volume.

The principal theorem \texttt{Mahler240.counterexample} includes the body's
admissibility, dimension, both real volumes, equality with $4^4/4!$, and
both non-image conclusions. The theorems
\texttt{conjecture\_00000000240\_english\_false} and
\texttt{conjecture\_00000000240\_chinese\_false} negate the respective
dimension-four conjunctions. This specialization suffices to refute the
universal conjecture. The finite volume identities justify the real-valued
measure conversions; numerical constants do not stand in for geometric
objects or their measures.

The independent script \texttt{reproduce.py} uses rational arithmetic to
enumerate supporting facets, verify primal-polar vertex duality, construct
the complete face lattices, and sum determinant volumes of barycentric
simplices. Its separate section calculations agree with these volumes.
Reference cube and cross-polytope volumes, and a nonsingular rational
shear, check the normalization. The script reports
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
 &\text{vertices}&\text{facets}&\text{volume}\\\hline
 K&12&10&8/3\\
 K^\circ&10&12&4
 \end{array}
\]
These computations corroborate the formal proof. The Lean theorem does
not assume their output.

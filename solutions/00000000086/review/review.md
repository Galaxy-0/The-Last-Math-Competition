## Semantic audit

The conjecture fixes the object (the free Burnside group B(m,p), the largest m-generated group of exponent p) and asserts log|B(m,p)| ~ ((m−1)²/2)·log p with p → ∞, m being a free rank parameter. The submission constructs B(m,p) in Lean exactly as in the standard definition — the free group on Fin m modulo the normal closure of all p-th powers — and proves the characterizing universal property in full: the law x^p = 1, generation by m elements, existence and uniqueness of lifts to every exponent-p group, and surjection onto every m-generated exponent-p group (`IsLargest`). This is the conjecture's own object, not a surrogate; moreover the refutation is proved for *any* family of groups with the "largest" property, so it does not even depend on the construction.

The decisive theorem refutes the asymptotic at m = 2 (and also m = 1, 3) along every nonbottom filter l ≤ atTop, in particular over all integers and over the primes (filter nontriviality via infinitely many primes). The mathematical core is elementary and unimpeachable: by the universal property B(m,p) surjects onto the elementary abelian group (Z/p)^m (proved generated by m basis vectors, exponent p, cardinality p^m), so if B(m,p) is finite then |B(m,p)| ≥ p^m and log|B(m,p)| ≥ m·log p, while the claimed target is ((m−1)²/2)·log p. At m = 2 the ratio is at least 2/(1/2) = 4 for every p ≥ 2 and cannot tend to 1. I verified the gap algebra by hand for m ∈ {1,2,3}: m − (m−1)²/2 ∈ {1, 3/2, 1}, which exceeds (1/4)·(m−1)²/2 in each case, so the constant-gap lemma (a pointwise relative gap η > 0 defeats `Asymptotics.IsEquivalent`, applied with η = 1/4) closes the argument.

Possibly-infinite B(m,p) is handled honestly rather than hidden: under the strict reading (`Claim`: eventual finiteness plus the asymptotic) the asymptotic conjunct is refuted outright; under Mathlib's junk convention (Nat.card = 0 for infinite types, log 0 = 0) the gap lemma still applies for m ∈ {2,3}, and the m = 1 infinite branch is excluded by proving that a 1-generated largest group is finite (cyclic, generated by an element of finite order). A fortiori, if B(2,p) is genuinely infinite for large p the conjectured finite-order asymptotic fails outright. The quantifier structure matches the text: the conjecture is asserted for the rank parameter m, and a single counterexample rank m = 2 (satisfying every stated hypothesis, with p → ∞ through any admissible filter) refutes it. Natural log is the standard convention, and the tex correctly notes asymptotic-equivalence is invariant under change of base.

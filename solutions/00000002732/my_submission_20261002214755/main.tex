\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} $\chi(\Delta) \le 2 + \max_{\text{links}} \chi(\mathrm{link})$
for a simplicial complex $\Delta$.
\end{quote}

\section{The counterexample}

Let $\Delta$ be the flag complex of the Grötzsch graph $G =
\mathrm{Mycielski}(C_5)$ (11 vertices, 20 edges). Kernel-certified facts:

\begin{enumerate}
\item $G$ is triangle-free, so every link of $\Delta$ is a
\emph{0-dimensional} complex with $\chi(\mathrm{link}) = 1$: the
conjectured bound gives $\chi(\Delta) \le 2 + 1 = 3$.
\item $\chi(\Delta) = \chi(G)$: in a flag complex every face is a clique
of the 1-skeleton, so proper colorings of $\Delta$ and of $G$ coincide.
\item $G$ has \textbf{no} proper 3-coloring (exhaustive check of all
$3^{11} = 177147$ assignments in the Lean kernel) and admits the proper
4-coloring $(0,1,0,1,2,\,0,1,0,1,2,\,3)$.
\end{enumerate}

Hence $\chi(\Delta) = 4 > 3$: the recursion inequality fails with every
link as simple as possible.

\section{Verification}

\texttt{reproduce.py} repeats the construction and searches in Python. The
Lean package (core Lean~4, v4.33.1) certifies all five statements ---
triangle-freeness, the witness coloring, non-3-colorability (the
$177147$-case kernel enumeration under \texttt{maxHeartbeats 0}), the
bound value, and $4 > 3$ --- all \texttt{does not depend on any axioms}.

\section{Boundary}

Only the inequality is refuted; the equality-classification clause is not
addressed.

## Semantic audit
The conjecture is an existence claim: there exist two maps whose canonical height functions coincide but whose periodic point structures differ, with this separation realized by an explicit conjugate pair and a period difference controlled by a perturbation term. The crux is that the two required properties of the pair — being conjugate and having genuinely different periodic structures — are jointly contradictory, and the submission proves exactly that.

For arbitrary f : X → X, g : Y → Y and a bijection e : X ≅ Y with e∘f = g∘e (the definition of conjugacy covering topological, smooth, and algebraic/Möbius conjugacy on point spaces), induction on n gives e(fⁿ(x)) = gⁿ(e(x)) for all n ≥ 0 and all x. Injectivity of e then yields fⁿ(x) = x ⟺ gⁿ(e(x)) = e(x), so exact (minimal positive) periods agree at corresponding points — both directions, including the absence of smaller periods. The stronger reachability relation fᵏ(x) = y ⟺ gᵏ(e(x)) = e(y) follows the same way, and e transports forward orbits onto forward orbits, so finite cycles are carried to finite cycles as dynamical objects. Consequently S ↦ e(S) is an explicit bijection between the unrooted n-cycle collections for every n, with inverse T ↦ e⁻¹(T), and the iterate-fixed sets and exact-period sets are in explicit subtype equivalence. All cardinalities — #Fix(fⁿ), #Perₙ, #C(n) — agree for every n, finite or infinite.

Because the conjecture requires the separating maps to be a conjugate pair, this universal preservation destroys the required realization: the theorem `separation_impossible_with_any_extra_property` quantifies over an arbitrary additional property P of the conjugacy (into which "the two maps have the same canonical height function" and "the period difference is controlled by the perturbation term" slot as instances) and still forbids any difference in any of the three count families. So no height agreement or perturbation control can rescue the existential claim: the conjecture's core requirements are inconsistent before any height condition can matter. The quantifier structure is exactly right — the official text asserts ∃ (pair with properties), and the Lean theorem proves the ∀ impossibility that negates it; this is the correct negation, not a disproof of some strengthened universal reading (which was the flaw in a past rejection). The report is also scrupulous about what is NOT refuted: two unconjugated maps may well share a height function and differ periodically; only the conjugate realization demanded by the source is impossible.

The one alternative reading — that "different periodic point structures" means literal inequality of periodic-point sets rather than a structural difference — is correctly dismissed: conjugate relabeling trivially moves such sets (e.g. two conjugate involutions with fixed-point sets {2,3} and {0,1}), which would make the conjecture trivially satisfiable with no height content and would render "period difference" (周期差) meaningless; structure means periods, cycles, and their counts up to dynamical isomorphism, which is precisely what the Lean theorems transport and compare.

The Lean development is faithful: `Conjugacy` is the pointwise intertwining equation with a genuine `Equiv`; `Periodic`/`ExactPeriod` are the standard predicates; the equivalences are constructed with explicit inverse functions proved to be two-sided inverses; `Cycles` is the set of orbit-sets of exact-period points (unrooted cycles); and the two headline theorems (`exact_period_separation_impossible`, `conjecture_5390_conjugate_separation_impossible`, plus the extra-property version) state the impossibility cleanly. The mathematics is elementary, classical, and correct.

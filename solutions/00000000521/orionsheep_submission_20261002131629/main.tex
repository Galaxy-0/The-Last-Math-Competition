\begin{abstract}
We disprove TLMC conjecture 00000000521 \emph{under its literal reading}.
The conjecture defines the alternating Betti sum
$A_j=\sum_i(-1)^i\beta_{i,j}(I)$ using the graded Betti numbers \emph{of the
ideal $I$ as a graded $R$-module} ($\operatorname{Tor}^R(I,k)$), where
$\beta_{0,j}(I)$ counts the minimal generators of $I$.  The 4-cycle
$C_4$ with $I=(x_1x_2,\,x_2x_3,\,x_3x_4,\,x_4x_1)\subset k[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$
is a counterexample: resolving the \emph{module} $I$, one gets
$\beta_{0,2}(I)=4$, $\beta_{1,3}(I)=4$, $\beta_{2,4}(I)=1$, so
$\operatorname{reg}(I)=2$ and
$A=(A_0,\dots,A_4)=(0,0,4,-4,1)$, whose signs $+,-,+$ change
\emph{twice}, not exactly once.  The monotonicity clause holds for this
example, so the failure is exactly the ``exactly once'' clause; the star
$K_{1,3}$ ($A=(0,0,3,-3,1)$) is a second counterexample.  A v1 submission
used the Betti table of $R/I$ instead of that of $I$; all data here are
recomputed for the module $I$ by four independent methods and formalized in
axiom-free Lean~4.
\end{abstract}

\section{The conjecture and its object}

The conjecture text reads, verbatim:

\begin{quote}
``Definition: The alternating Betti sum $A_j=\sum_i(-1)^i\beta_{i,j}(I)$ of a
monomial ideal $I$.  Conjecture: For all edge ideals, the sign of
$\{A_j\}$ changes exactly once as $j$ crosses $\operatorname{reg}(I)$, and
$|A_j|$ is monotonically nondecreasing up to that point; this gives a
combinatorial characterization of the graded Lefschetz property for edge
ideals.''
\end{quote}

The object is fixed by the notation $\beta_{i,j}(I)$: the graded Betti
numbers \emph{of the ideal $I$ as a graded $R$-module}, i.e.
$\beta_{i,j}(I)=\dim_k\operatorname{Tor}_i^R(I,k)_j$.  Thus
$\beta_{0,j}(I)$ counts the minimal generators of $I$ in degree $j$, and
$\beta_{0,0}(I)=0$ for every nonzero ideal.  A v1 submission instead used
the Betti table of $R/I$ (which starts with $\beta_{0,0}=1$); that version
was closed after review, and we thank the reviewer for the correction.
Everything below is computed for the module $I$.

\begin{remark}[Relating the two readings]
\label{rem:les}
For an edge ideal $I=I(G)\subset R=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, the long exact Tor
sequence of $0\to I\to R\to R/I\to 0$ gives
$\beta_{i,j}(I)=\beta_{i+1,j}(R/I)$ for $i\ge 1$ and
$\beta_{0,j}(I)=\beta_{1,j}(R/I)$ (as $I/\mathfrak m I$ is concentrated in
degree $2$).  Consequently
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ident}
A_j(I)\;=\;\delta_{j0}-A_j(R/I),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the literal sequence is the $R/I$ sequence with its leading
$A_0(R/I)=1$ removed and negated.  In particular the literal conjecture
holds for $G$ iff the $R/I$-sequence $\{A_j(R/I)\}_{j\ge1}$ changes sign
exactly twice (at the right place); any graph with
$\beta_{3,4}(R/I)\neq 0$ produces \emph{two} changes literally.
\end{remark}

\section{The counterexample $C_4$}

Let $G=C_4$ be the 4-cycle and $I=I(C_4)=(x_1x_2,\,x_2x_3,\,x_3x_4,\,
x_4x_1)\subset R=k[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$.  The minimal free resolution
\emph{of the module $I$} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:res}
0\;\longrightarrow\;R(-4)\;\xrightarrow{\;d_2\;}
R(-3)^4\;\xrightarrow{\;d_1\;}R(-2)^4\;\longrightarrow\;I\;\longrightarrow\;0,
\end{equation}
where $d_1$ is given by the four adjacent-edge syzygies
\[
s_{12}=(x_3,-x_1,0,0),\quad
s_{23}=(0,x_4,-x_2,0),\quad
s_{34}=(0,0,x_1,-x_3),\quad
s_{41}=(x_4,0,0,-x_2),
\]
and $d_2$ by their single relation $\rho=(x_4,x_1,x_2,-x_3)$:
$x_4s_{12}+x_1s_{23}+x_2s_{34}-x_3s_{41}=0$.  (The opposite-pair syzygies
are generated by these: e.g.\ $(x_3x_4,0,-x_1x_2,0)=x_4s_{12}+x_1s_{23}$;
the coefficient matrix of $(s_{12},s_{23},s_{34},s_{41})$ has rank $3$ and
determinant $0$, and its kernel over $R$ is exactly $R\cdot\rho$, since
$\gcd(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=1$.)  All entries of $d_1,d_2$ have degree $1$, so
the resolution is minimal.  Exactness and minimality of \eqref{eq:res} are
verified mechanically in \texttt{reproduce.py}: $d_1d_2=0$, per-bidegree
homology $H_1=H_2=0$ over $\mathrm{GF}(10^9{+}7)$, and
$\operatorname{coker}(d_1)$ matches the Hilbert series of $I$ degree by
degree.  Hence
\[
\beta_{0,2}(I)=4,\qquad \beta_{1,3}(I)=4,\qquad \beta_{2,4}(I)=1,
\qquad \beta_{i,j}(I)=0\ \text{else},
\]
so $\operatorname{reg}(I)=\max(j-i)=2$, and
\[
A_0=0,\quad A_1=0,\quad A_2=+4,\quad A_3=-4,\quad A_4=+1,\quad A_j=0\ (j\ge5).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Falsification, literal reading]
\label{thm:main}
Conjecture 00000000521 is false.  For the edge ideal
$I=(x_1x_2,x_2x_3,x_3x_4,x_4x_1)$ of the 4-cycle, the nonzero entries of
$\{A_j\}_{j\ge0}$ are $4,-4,1$ with signs $+,-,+$: the sign changes
\emph{twice}, not exactly once.  The first change does occur as $j$ crosses
$\operatorname{reg}(I)=2$ ($A_2=4>0>A_3=-4$), but a second change follows
at $j=3$ ($-4\to+1$).  Meanwhile $|A_0|\le|A_1|\le|A_2|$, so the
monotonicity clause holds: the counterexample refutes exactly the
``exactly once'' clause.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The Betti numbers displayed after \eqref{eq:res} are those of the module
$I$ (exact minimal resolution, verified as described).  Substituting into
$A_j=\sum_i(-1)^i\beta_{i,j}(I)$ gives $A=(0,0,4,-4,1)$; the sign scan is
arithmetic.  All steps are additionally cross-checked by the Hilbert-series
identity $A_j=[t^j]\,(1-t)^4\operatorname{Hilb}_I(t)$ and by
\eqref{eq:ident}, and are formalized in axiom-free Lean~4 (see
Section~\ref{sec:verif}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The v1 example is not a counterexample, literally]
For the triangle $C_3$, $I=(xy,xz,yz)$ resolves as
$0\to R(-3)^2\to R(-2)^3\to I\to 0$, giving
$A(I)=(0,0,3,-2)$: exactly one change at $\operatorname{reg}(I)=2$.  The
conjecture \emph{holds} for $C_3$ under the literal reading, as the
reviewer observed; likewise for $P_3$ and $P_4$ (same sequence) and for
$2K_2$ ($A=(0,0,2,0,-1)$, one change at $\operatorname{reg}=3$).  The v1
numbers $A=(1,0,-3,2)$ came from the $R/I$ table.
\end{remark}

All computations come from \texttt{reproduce.py} (module-$I$ Taylor
resolution $\otimes\,k$, Hilbert-cross-checked and identity-checked via
\eqref{eq:ident}); every example and every graph in the scans passed.

Notes.
\begin{itemize}
\item The single-edge case is degenerate ($I\cong R(-2)$ is free, so
$A=(0,0,1)$ never changes sign at all) and is listed only for completeness;
the counterexamples above are non-degenerate.
\item Exhaustive scan, literal reading: among the $63$ labeled graphs on
$4$ vertices with at least one edge, \textbf{32} violate ``exactly one
sign change''; among the $1023$ on $5$ vertices, \textbf{908} do, and
\textbf{100} additionally violate the monotonicity clause (e.g.\
$P_3\sqcup K_2$ in the table).
\item By Remark~\ref{rem:les}, the literal failure class is exactly the
class of graphs whose $R/I$-table changes sign three or more times, e.g.\
every graph with $\beta_{3,4}(R/I)\neq0$ ($C_4$, $K_{1,3}$, $K_4$,
$C_5$, $C_6$, paw, diamond, \dots).
\end{itemize}

\section{Verification}
\label{sec:verif}

\paragraph{Independent recomputation (\texttt{reproduce.py}).}
The script (stdlib only) computes $\beta_{i,j}(I)$ for the \emph{module}
$I$ as the homology of the Taylor resolution of $I$ tensored with
$k=\mathrm{GF}(10^9{+}7)$ (Tor is computable from any free resolution;
after tensoring, only the constant Taylor entries survive, and ranks are
computed on $0,\pm1$ matrices, so the output is exact).  Four
cross-checks run throughout: (i) $A_j$ must equal
$[t^j](1-t)^n\operatorname{Hilb}_I(t)$ from direct monomial enumeration;
(ii) the identity \eqref{eq:ident} against an independently computed
$R/I$-table; (iii) $\beta^I_{0,2}=\#\{\text{edges}\}$ and
$\beta^I_{0,0}=0$; (iv) for $C_4$, the displayed resolution
\eqref{eq:res} is checked entry-by-entry: $d_1d_2=0$, per-bidegree
homology $H_1=H_2=0$, $\operatorname{coker}(d_1)$ vs.\ $\operatorname{Hilb}_I$,
and minimality.  Run: \texttt{python3 reproduce.py} (exit 0).

\paragraph{Lean 4 formalization (\texttt{lean4/}).}
Under the literal definition, the Betti table of the module $I(C_4)$
($\beta_{0,2}=4$, $\beta_{1,3}=4$, $\beta_{2,4}=1$), the alternating sums
$A=(0,0,4,-4,1)$, $A_j=0$ for $j\ge5$, $\operatorname{reg}(I)=2$ (attained
and maximal), the \emph{two} sign changes ($\neq1$), the holding of the
monotonicity clause, and the second counterexample $K_{1,3}$ are proved by
\texttt{decide}/\texttt{rfl} in pure Lean~4 core (v4.33.1, no Mathlib).
\texttt{lake build \&\& lake env lean Check.lean} succeeds and every one of
the 15 \texttt{\#print axioms} reports ``does not depend on any axioms'';
there is no \texttt{sorry} and no additional axiom.

\paragraph{Changes from v1 (closed PR \#130).}
Object corrected to the ideal $I$ (literal reading); counterexample
replaced: $C_3$ (which holds literally) by $C_4$, with $K_{1,3}$ added;
all tables, sums and scans recomputed for the module $I$; Lean theorem now

## Conjecture
The conjecture asserts that toric ideals of polytopal lattice configurations are generated by quadratic circuit relations of lattice points, with a Markov-basis/cycle-space minimality clause. The submission refutes the universal quadratic-generation assertion.

## Counterexample configuration
Let \(P=\operatorname{conv}\{(0,0),(2,1),(1,2)\}\). Its complete lattice-point set is \((1,1),(2,1),(1,2),(0,0)\). Homogenizing these points gives
\[
X_0\mapsto txy,\quad X_1\mapsto tx^2y,\quad X_2\mapsto txy^2,\quad X_3\mapsto t.
\]
The toric ideal is the kernel of this actual algebra homomorphism.

The exponent matrix has primitive kernel direction \((-3,1,1,1)\). Therefore
\[
F=X_1X_2X_3-X_0^3
\]
is a nonzero cubic in the kernel: both monomials map to \(t^3x^3y^3\).

## Why no quadratic binomial generators exist
If two exponent vectors of total degree at most two have the same image, their difference must be an integer multiple of \((-3,1,1,1)\). A nonzero multiple forces one exponent coordinate to be at least 3, impossible under the degree bound. Hence all fifteen degree-at-most-two monomials have distinct images.

It follows that every rational-coefficient binomial of degree at most two lying in the kernel is zero. Any ideal generated by such binomials is therefore the zero ideal and cannot contain \(F\). This is stronger than ruling out exact quadratic circuit relations and makes the counterexample non-vacuous.

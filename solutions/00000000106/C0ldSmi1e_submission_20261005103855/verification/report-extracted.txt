Abstract
For each integer k ≥ 2, the exponent ck = 1/(2k) works: there are inﬁnitely many natural
numbers n for which every one of n + 1, . . . , n+ k has a prime factor larger than nck . We
prove this using Bertrand’s postulate and a bounded Chinese remainder representative. The
accompanying Lean project proves the assertion for the actual largest prime factor, with a
single exponent ﬁxed for each k.
1 Statement and interpretation
The English source states:
Conjecture: (Family of consecutive rough numbers) For every k ≥ 2 there exists
ck > 0 such that inﬁnitely many n satisfy: the largest prime factor of each of
n + 1, . . . , n+ k exceeds nck .
The Chinese source gives the same quantiﬁers and largest-prime-factor condition. Let P (m)
denote the greatest prime divisor of m ≥ 2. The explicit condition concerns the largest prime
factor; the word “rough” in the title introduces no additional condition on smaller factors. All
powers in the statement are real powers.
We prove a stronger, explicit formulation with n ≥ 2:
Theorem 1. For every k ∈ N with k ≥ 2 and every N ∈ N, there is an integer n > N , with
n ≥ 2, such that
n1/(2k) < P (n + i) (1 ≤ i ≤ k).
Consequently the conjecture holds with ck = 1/(2k) > 0.
Restricting to n ≥ 2 suﬃces for the original inﬁnitude claim, and ensures that every argument
of P is at least two. The exponent depends only on k; neither the bound N nor the oﬀset i
changes it.
2 Proof
Fix k ≥ 2 and N ∈ N, and put
t = max{2k, N+ k + 3}.
Then t > k , t > N , t ≥ 2, and 2k ≤ t. By Bertrand’s postulate, for each j = 0, . . . , k− 1 choose
a prime pj with
2jt < p j ≤ 2j+1t. (1)
These primes are strictly increasing: if j < ℓ , then pj ≤ 2j+1t ≤ 2ℓt < p ℓ. Hence they are
pairwise coprime. Also pj > t > k ≥ j + 1, so pj − (j + 1) is a natural number.

3 Formalization and veriﬁcation
The project uses Lean 4.19.0 and Mathlib v4.19.0. Its main ﬁle is Conjecture106.lean, in
namespace Conjecture106. For m ≥ 2, its deﬁnition reads
largestPrimeFactor(m) = sup {p ∈ N : p is a prime divisor of m}
using the ﬁnite set Nat.primeFactors. At zero and one this ﬁnite-set convention returns zero.
Those inputs are never used in the conjecture. The theorem largestPrimeFactor_spec proves,
for every m ≥ 2, that the deﬁned value is prime, divides m, and is at least every prime divisor
of m. The theorem threshold_iff_prime_divisor proves the exact equivalence
x < P (m) ⇐ ⇒ ∃ p ∈ N (p prime, p |m, x < p ) ( m ≥ 2, x∈ R).
Thus the chosen prime divisors establish precisely the asserted condition on the greatest prime
divisor.

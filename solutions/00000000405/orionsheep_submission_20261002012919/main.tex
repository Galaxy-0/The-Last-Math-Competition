\title{Disproof of Conjecture 00000000405:\\
Spin-Pairing Products of Kostka Numbers Do Not Always Divide $n!$}
\author{}
\date{October 2, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Conjecture 00000000405 asserts that for every partition $\shape \vdash n$ the
spin-pairing product of Kostka numbers
$\Kostka_{\shape,\oneton}\cdot \Kostka_{\shape',\oneton}$
divides $n!$. We give an explicit counterexample at the first nontrivial shape:
$n=3$, $\shape=(2,1)$, for which the product equals $4$ while $3! = 6$, and
$4 \nmid 6$.
\end{abstract}

\section{The conjecture}

For partitions $\mu$ and $\shape$ of $n$, the Kostka number $\Kostka_{\shape\mu}$ counts
the semistandard Young tableaux (SSYT) of shape $\shape$ and content $\mu$. For
$\mu = \oneton$, the content condition forces each entry $1,\dots,n$ to occur exactly
once, so $\Kostka_{\shape,\oneton} = f^{\shape}$, the number of standard Young tableaux
(SYT) of shape $\shape$.

Conjecture 00000000405 claims:
\[
\forall \shape \vdash n:\qquad \Kostka_{\shape,\oneton}\cdot \Kostka_{\shape',\oneton}
\ \big|\ n!,
\]
where $\shape'$ denotes the conjugate partition.

\section{Counterexample: $n=3$, $\shape=(2,1)$}

\subsection{Computing $\Kostka_{(2,1),(1,1,1)}$}

By the hook-length formula for the shape $(2,1)$, whose hook lengths are $3,1,1$:
\[
f^{(2,1)} = \frac{3!}{3\cdot 1 \cdot 1} = \frac{6}{3} = 2.
\]
The two standard tableaux are
\[
\YD{1}{2}{3} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \YD{1}{3}{2},
\]
and a brute-force enumeration of all $6^3$ fillings confirms these are the only SSYT of
shape $(2,1)$ with content $(1,1,1)$. Hence $\Kostka_{(2,1),(1,1,1)} = 2$.

\subsection{Computing $\Kostka_{(2,1)',(1,1,1)}$}

The shape $(2,1)$ has two columns, of heights $2$ and $1$, so its conjugate is
$(2,1)' = (2,1)$: the partition is self-conjugate. Therefore
\[
\Kostka_{(2,1)',(1,1,1)} = \Kostka_{(2,1),(1,1,1)} = 2.
\]

\subsection{The divisibility fails}

\[
\Kostka_{(2,1),\oneton}\cdot \Kostka_{(2,1)',\oneton} = 2\cdot 2 = 4,
\qquad
3! = 6,
\qquad
6 \bmod 4 = 2 \neq 0.
\]
Thus $4 \nmid 3!$, and the conjecture is \textbf{false}.

\section{Boundary of validity}

An exhaustive recomputation (see \texttt{reproduce.py}) of both Kostka numbers by direct
enumeration of SSYT gives:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{c l c c c c l}
\hline
$n$ & $\shape$ & $\Kostka_{\shape,\oneton}$ & $\Kostka_{\shape',\oneton}$ & product & $n!$ & divides?\\
\hline
1 & $(1)$ & 1 & 1 & 1 & 1 & yes\\
2 & $(2),(1,1)$ & 1 & 1 & 1 & 2 & yes\\
3 & $(3),(1,1,1)$ & 1 & 1 & 1 & 6 & yes\\
3 & $\mathbf{(2,1)}$ & $\mathbf{2}$ & $\mathbf{2}$ & $\mathbf{4}$ & $\mathbf{6}$ & \textbf{no} ($6 \bmod 4 = 2$)\\
4 & $(2,2)$ & 2 & 2 & 4 & 24 & yes\\
4 & $(3,1),(2,1,1)$ & 3 & 3 & 9 & 24 & no ($24 \bmod 9 = 6$)\\
5 & $(4,1),(2,1,1,1)$ & 4 & 4 & 16 & 120 & no\\
5 & $(3,2),(2,2,1)$ & 5 & 5 & 25 & 120 & no\\
5 & $(3,1,1)$ & 6 & 6 & 36 & 120 & no\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The conjecture holds for $n \le 2$ (the product is always $1$) and for the row and column
shapes $(n)$, $(1^n)$ at every $n$; $(2,1)$ at $n=3$ is the unique minimal counterexample.
Violations are abundant from $n = 4$ onward ($65$ violating shapes within $n \le 8$);
essentially the conjecture only survives on shapes where $f^{\shape}$ is very small.

\section{Reproducibility}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{reproduce.py}: dependency-free Python recomputation by brute-force SSYT
        enumeration, cross-checked against the hook-length formula.
  \item \texttt{lean4/}: a core-Lean (no Mathlib) encoding; \texttt{lake build} followed by
        \texttt{lake env lean Check.lean} certifies every theorem with
        \emph{zero axioms} (\texttt{\#print axioms} reports ``does not depend on any
        axioms''), including the brute-force count $\texttt{sytShape21} = 2$, the
        self-conjugacy $\texttt{colHeights}\,[2,1] = [2,1]$, and
        $3! \bmod 4 = 2 \ne 0$.
\end{itemize}

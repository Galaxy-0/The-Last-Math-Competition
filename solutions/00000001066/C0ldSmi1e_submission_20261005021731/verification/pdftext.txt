Disproof of Conjecture 00000001066
Exact square-root attainment over prime ﬁelds
C0ldSmi1e
5 October 2026
Abstract
The conjecture asserts a cardinality bound for Sidon subsets of Fp and attainment of √p
for prime p. We disprove the exact-attainment clause for every prime: a ﬁnite set has integer
cardinality, whereas the square root of a prime is not an integer. The accompanying Lean
project proves the negation of the full written conjunction under both universal-prime and
existential-prime readings of the attainment clause.
1 Statement and interpretation
The supplied English conjecture states:
Deﬁnition: A Sidon set B ⊂ Fp (all pairwise diﬀerences distinct). Conjecture: |B| ≤
p1/2 + 1, and p1/2 is attained for prime p (ﬁnite-ﬁeld Sidon extremality).
The Chinese version has the same bound and attainment clause, joined by “and. ” The exact,
unmodiﬁed bilingual source is included as conjecture.md.
We use the conventional diﬀerence formulation of the Sidon property: the map (a, b) ↦→ a − b is
injective on
DB = {(a, b) ∈ B × B : a ̸= b}.
Thus diﬀerences are ordered and diagonal pairs are excluded. The symbol p1/2 denotes the
nonnegative real square root, with the natural numbers p and |B| embedded in R. “Attained” is
read as the equality |B| = √p. Neither language speciﬁes rounding or an asymptotic qualiﬁer.
Write U (p) for the stated bound for every Sidon subset of Fp, and A(p) for the existence of a
Sidon subset of cardinality exactly √p. The standard reading is
C∀ : ∀p prime, U (p) ∧ A(p).
For completeness, we also treat the weaker prime quantiﬁer
C∃ :
(
∀p prime, U(p)
)
∧
(
∃p prime, A(p)
)
.
The argument below rules out A(p) for every prime, so it refutes both readings without any claim
about the truth of U (p).
2 The cardinality obstruction
Theorem 1. For every prime p and every ﬁnite subset B ⊆ Fp, one has |B| ̸= √p.
1

Proof. Put n = |B| ∈ N and suppose n = √p as real numbers. Since p ≥ 0, squaring gives n2 = p;
this is also an equality of natural numbers by injectivity of their embedding in the reals. If n = 0
or n = 1 , then p = 0 or p = 1 , contradicting primality. If n ≥ 2, then p = n · n has a proper
divisor n, since 1 < n < n 2 = p, again contradicting primality.
The theorem applies to all ﬁnite subsets, hence to Sidon subsets. It gives ¬A(p) and consequently
¬(U (p) ∧ A(p)) for every prime p. Specializing to the prime 2 yields ¬C∀. Universal failure of
A(p) also yields ¬C∃.

lean/lake-manifest.json. Its objects are actual ﬁnite subsets Finset (ZMod p) ; for prime p,

4 Scope of the disproof
The submission refutes literal exact attainment of √p over prime ﬁelds, hence the written con-
junction. It does not prove or refute the upper-bound clause alone, determine a largest Sidon set,
or address rounded or asymptotic attainment. Replacing the prime parameter by a prime power,
adding a ﬂoor, or changing exact equality to an asymptotic statement would produce diﬀerent
claims. None of those changes is made here.

\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Definition:} simultaneous Thue equations are systems
$F(x,y) = a$, $G(x,y) = b$ of two binary homogeneous forms of degree
$\ge 3$. \textit{Conjecture:} the number of solutions is at most
$(\deg F)\cdot(\deg G)$; the bound is optimal, attained when the
composite root structures of $F$ and $G$ are of cyclotomic type.
\end{quote}

\section{The refutation}

The conjecture quantifies over all systems of two degree-$\ge 3$
binary homogeneous forms, including the degenerate pair $G = 2F$. Take
\[
F(x,y) = x\,y\,(x+y), \qquad a = 195093360 = 2^4\cdot 3^4\cdot 5\cdot
7\cdot 11\cdot 17\cdot 23,
\]
and $b = 2a$: the system is equivalent to the single Thue equation
$F = a$, and exhaustive enumeration over the signed divisor structure
of $a$ (800 divisors; discriminant test for $y^2 + xy - a/x$) gives
\textbf{66 integer solutions}, every one re-verified by evaluation.
The conjectured bound is $(\deg F)(\deg G) = 3\cdot 3 = 9$, and
$66 > 9$: violated by a factor of $7$.

The Lean kernel certifies ten of the solutions by closed evaluation
(e.g.\ $(-2448)\cdot 33 \cdot (-2415) = 195093360$), their pairwise
distinctness, the degree product $9$, and the comparison $9 < 10$ ---
which already refutes the $\le 9$ claim.

\section{Verification}

\texttt{reproduce.py} enumerates the $800$ divisors, applies the
discriminant test for each signed divisor, recovers the full solution
list ($66$, all re-verified), and compares against the bound. The Lean
package (core Lean~4, v4.33.1) certifies the ten instances,
distinctness, and the comparison; all three audited theorems report
\texttt{does not depend on any axioms}.

\section{Boundary}

The kernel certifies ten concrete solutions and the bound comparison
$9 < 10$; the exact total $66$ is carried by the script's exhaustive
enumeration. The degenerate pair $G = 2F$ satisfies the conjecture's
stated hypotheses (two binary homogeneous forms of degree $\ge 3$);
whether proportional forms were meant to be excluded is not stated and
cannot be assumed.

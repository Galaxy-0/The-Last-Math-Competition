## Exact source and the clause refuted

The English source is:

> Definition: The entropy-rate extremum of a k-th order Markov chain is the maximal entropy under given transition constraints. Conjecture: The transition support of the entropy-maximizing chain is a partial permutation matrix; and the extremum is given explicitly by the permutation-block decomposition of the admissible matrix. (transition-constraint entropy maximum)

The Chinese source is:

> 定义：k 阶马尔可夫链的熵率极值为给定转移约束下的最大熵。猜想：熵极大链的转移支撑为部分置换矩阵；且极值由允许矩阵的置换块分解显式给出。（转移约束熵极大）

The source's support assertion must hold for a valid first-order instance if it is true in the stated generality. Taking `k=1`, three states, and an admissibility matrix with zero diagonal and all off-diagonal entries one is therefore legitimate. No source restriction excludes this matrix or number of states. The formal optimization is over ordinary homogeneous first-order transition kernels on those three states, with arbitrary initial laws. This is the fixed-transition-matrix setting of the claim; the review does not claim a separate formal optimization theorem for time-dependent kernels or all stochastic processes.

The source does not restrict competitors to stationary chains, and the final proof does not introduce that restriction. Stationarity is a property of the chosen witness. The support clause is explicit and false; the additional permutation-block formula is not precisely specified in the source. Refuting the first clause suffices to refute the conjunction without inventing a definition for that second clause. The report and PR description correctly preserve this logical scope.

## Genuine kernels and coherent finite trajectory laws

The transition object has type `Fin 3 → PMF (Fin 3)`: each row is an actual normalized probability mass function. Thus nonnegativity, finite probabilities and row normalization are part of the mathematical object, rather than omitted side conditions. `Admissible K` says `K i i = 0` for every i. This is exactly the specified admissibility matrix: every non-diagonal transition is allowed and every diagonal transition is forbidden.

The initial law is uniform, with mass `1/3` at each state. The witness kernel is

\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}0&1/2&1/2\\1/2&0&1/2\\1/2&1/2&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its row PMFs are constructed and normalized, not assumed to exist. `initial_stationary` proves the actual invariant-law equation `initial.bind transition = initial`. `blockLaw_last` then proves that every terminal marginal equals that invariant law. Each state has positive marginal probability. The two-state joint distribution is also proved different from independent uniform draws: the mass at `(0,0)` is zero, whereas the independent product has positive mass `1/9`. Consequently the witness has genuine first-order dependence, even under a minimal-order interpretation of `k=1`.

`blockLaw π K n` is recursively generated by actual PMF binding and mapping. It is a probability law for `n+1` states, not a table of assigned entropy values. `blockLaw_append` gives its exact next-state joint mass, and `blockLaw_consistent` proves that discarding the newly appended state returns the preceding law. `blockLaw_markov` proves the actual conditional next-state probability for every positive-probability history; the positive-history hypothesis prevents a zero-denominator conditional expression.

`Word α n` is an explicit storage representation of histories. `wordEquiv` is an equivalence with conventional time-indexed tuples `Fin (n+1) → α`; its append and terminal-coordinate properties are proved. `tupleLaw` pushes the law through this equivalence, and `tupleLaw_entropy` proves that the entropy is unchanged. Thus neither the Markov property nor the entropy calculation depends on an unrelated surrogate state space.

These coherent finite-dimensional distributions specify the Markov law needed for block entropies. A separately constructed probability measure on infinite paths is not required for the displayed finite-law and limit statements. The report accurately states that no such separate construction or independent shift-stationarity theorem is claimed. Its finite-law representation is sufficient for the actual entropy-rate argument.

## Actual Shannon entropy and an actual rate limit

`entropy p` is the finite sum of `Real.negMulLog` applied to the actual real PMF masses. This is Shannon entropy with natural logarithms and the zero-mass convention. `sum_prob` proves that these real masses sum to one. `entropy_joint` derives the Shannon chain rule from the actual joint probabilities and the logarithm identity, including zero masses.

`HasEntropyRate π K h` is a `Tendsto` assertion for

\[
\frac{H(\operatorname{blockLaw}(\pi,K,n))}{n+1}\longrightarrow h.
\]

It is not defined to be a weighted row entropy. The familiar stationary weighted-row expression is subsequently derived as a theorem using the terminal marginal identity. The normalization is correct: `Word α n` contains `n+1` states and n transitions, including the base case n=0.

I independently consulted [Han et al., *Entropy Rate Estimation for Markov Chains with Large State Space*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.07889), equation (1) and Section 2. Its normalized joint-block entropy convention agrees with the report after the indexing shift; its kernel and invariant-law terminology also agree. This reference is explanatory. The proof uses no estimation theorem or externally assumed entropy-rate formula from it.

## Global bound and attainment for the stated constraint

Every admissible row on three states has one specified zero coordinate. Its other probabilities sum to one, so its actual entropy is bounded by the binary entropy maximum `log 2`. `entropy_le_log_two_of_zero` proves this for every such PMF, including deterministic rows.

The generic `block_entropy_le` uses the proved joint entropy identity and normalization of the current history law to establish

\[
H(q_n)\le H(\pi)+n c
\]

whenever every row entropy is at most c. It has no stationarity hypothesis. The main module specializes it to `c=log 2` for every initial PMF and every admissible kernel. Comparing normalized sequences with the proved affine limit shows that every actual entropy-rate limit of such a competitor is at most `log 2`. The block bound itself does not first assume existence of the comparator's rate.

For the witness, every row entropy is exactly `log 2` and the initial entropy is `log 3`. The constant-row entropy theorem therefore gives the exact identity

\[
H(q_n)=\log 3+n\log 2.
\]

The actual normalized sequence tends to `log 2`. `witness_maximizes` combines admissibility, this rate limit and the universal comparator bound in `MaximizesEntropyRate`. Its comparison quantifies all initial PMFs, all admissible kernels on the fixed states and all their actual rate limits. It neither assumes the desired maximum nor compares only a selected family or stationary subset. A maximizer is explicitly exhibited, so the support assertion is not refuted through a vacuous optimization condition.

## Support and exclusion of every partial-permutation optimizer

`PartialPermutationSupport` imposes at most one positive entry in each row and in each column. This is exactly the usual zero-one support condition for a partial permutation matrix. A separate `Matrix` wrapper is unnecessary: the predicate applies directly to positive entries of the actual stochastic rows. It is not a condition on numerical entry sizes or on a chosen subset of the support.

The witness has positive entries in both positions `(0,1)` and `(0,2)`, immediately contradicting row uniqueness. More strongly, `eq_pure_of_unique_successor` proves that a normalized row with at most one positive entry is a point mass. Hence every partial-permutation-support kernel has all row entropies zero. For every initial law, the entire block entropy then stays equal to the initial entropy and the normalized entropy rate is zero.

`no_partialPermutation_maximizer` combines that genuine zero-rate limit, uniqueness of limits, and the feasible witness rate `log 2>0`. It excludes every optimizer with partial-permutation support, not merely the displayed witness. Thus the construction also refutes the weaker reading that there exists some optimizer with the claimed support. `explicit_counterexample` collects stationarity, maximality, failed support and dependence; `conjecture_1277` negates the necessary first-order support instance. The mathematical report, documentation and proposed PR description agree with these statements.

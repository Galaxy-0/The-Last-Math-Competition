What the source actually states
Both language versions posit two commuting polynomials f and g on P^1, each of degree at least 2. The common periodic set is Per(f) intersect Per(g). The first assertion is an if-and-only-if between infinitude of this set and existence of common iterates f^m = g^n. An additional assertion says that, when the set is infinite, both maps are iterates of a single map h, followed by a uniqueness assertion about a symmetrized monomial part in a Ritt decomposition. Chinese 'nonempty infinite' adds no condition beyond infinite. There is no source-stated base field, characteristic, rationality restriction on points, positivity condition written next to m,n, exclusion of special polynomial families, or permission to replace map equality by conjugacy.

Formalization contract
1. Domain convention must be explicit. Use f,g in C[z], acting as polynomial self-maps F,G of P^1(C), the complex projective line. The source omits the field; C is the usual classical complex-dynamical setting and must be labeled a convention, not quoted as a source-stated assumption. The Ritt wording is consistent with that setting but does not logically determine it. No claim is licensed that the source explicitly quantifies over all fields, or that a result on C settles every possible arithmetic interpretation. Restricting periodic points to real, rational, or bounded-degree algebraic points is not this contract. A broader algebraically closed setting is acceptable only if the proof also supplies the stated complex instance.
2. Preserve the original objects. F is the extension of evaluation of f to P^1(C), with infinity mapped to infinity; likewise G. P^1(C) may be represented by C together with one point at infinity if the correspondence and action are made explicit. A formalization using only abstract functions or numerical degree parameters needs an actual proved bridge to these polynomial/projective maps. A theorem about affine periodic points suffices only with an explicit proved transfer into the stated projective set.
3. Hypotheses are degree(f) >= 2, degree(g) >= 2, and F composed with G equals G composed with F. Commutativity is part of the input. It must be established for the actual maps in a disproof. Do not add a nonexceptionality assumption or any unstated restriction on the polynomial families.
4. Iteration means repeated composition. F^[0] is the identity for recursive convenience. A periodic point P satisfies exists r in N, r >= 1 and F^[r](P) = P. Preperiodicity (eventually entering a cycle, equivalently finite forward orbit) must not be substituted for periodicity.
5. Let I(F,G) mean that {P in P^1(C) : (exists r >= 1, F^[r](P)=P) and (exists s >= 1, G^[s](P)=P)} is infinite. The two periods may differ and depend on P. This counts distinct points in a single intersection for one fixed pair F,G; it is not a statement about infinitely many return times, infinitely many pairs of maps, multiplicities, or one infinite orbit. Including the single point at infinity cannot itself supply infinitude.
6. Let C(F,G) mean exists m,n in N with m >= 1 and n >= 1 such that F^[m] = G^[n] as self-maps of the same P^1(C). The equalities are exact, in the given coordinates; equality of conjugacy classes, degree equality, equality on a selected subset, or equality up to postcomposition is not the source claim. The source explicitly writes f^m = g^n, so using actual map equality does not depend on a Ritt convention. Do not require m=n.
7. Positive common-iterate exponents are the standard nonvacuous meaning. Allowing m=n=0 would make every pair share the identity, discarding the content of 'common iterate'. The text should state that positivity is the standard interpretation of its unwritten index range. A proof obligation negating C(F,G) must rule out every pair of positive indices, not just selected indices. A formal counterexample must not exploit a negative or zero iterate convention.
8. The precise unambiguous leading assertion, after the disclosed field/index conventions, is:
   For every f,g in C[z] with degree >= 2 and commuting projective extensions F,G, I(F,G) iff C(F,G).
   The two corresponding necessary implications are I(F,G) -> C(F,G) and C(F,G) -> I(F,G). A disproof must exhibit a single pair satisfying the actual input hypotheses and establish a failure of this equivalence. This is a description of the logical proof obligation, not a proposed construction.
9. The 'single h' assertion is stronger additional language, not a definition of common iterate. Its natural reading is existence of one polynomial self-map H of P^1(C) and positive a,b with F=H^[a], G=H^[b], but the category of h is not explicitly specified. The final 'symmetrized monomial part' and 'composition equivalence' have no definitions in either source version. I do not supply them by invention. No affirmative formal theorem about those clauses is presently endorsed.
10. The unclear final clause can be bypassed for a disproof by negating an earlier explicit necessary assertion. Grammatically the source asserts the equivalence and then adds the Ritt characterization. Whatever precise extra condition the last clause was intended to express, the full assertion entails the leading equivalence. A valid refutation of that equivalence therefore refutes the conjecture in the stated complex interpretation without proving or formalizing a replacement Ritt theorem. The report must say which exact earlier implication fails and must not claim to have resolved the undefined uniqueness terminology.

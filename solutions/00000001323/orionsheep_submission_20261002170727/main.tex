\section{The conjecture}

\begin{quote}
\textit{Conjecture:} In $\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}^2)$, the set of possible
periods of elements with Jacobian constant $1$ is $\{1, 2, 3, 4, 6\}$ (a
conformal-symmetry restriction).
\end{quote}

\section{The counterexample}

Let $\zeta_5$ be a primitive fifth root of unity and take
\[
  A \;=\; \mathrm{diag}(\zeta_5,\; \zeta_5^{-1}) \;\in\; \mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{C})
  \;\subset\; \mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{C}^2).
\]
Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item $A$ is linear, hence an automorphism of $\mathbb{C}^2$;
\item its Jacobian is the constant $\det A = \zeta_5\zeta_5^{-1} = 1$;
\item $A^k = \mathrm{diag}(\zeta_5^k, \zeta_5^{-k}) = I$ if and only if
$\zeta_5^k = 1$, i.e.\ $5 \mid k$: the period of $A$ is exactly $5$;
\item $5 \notin \{1,2,3,4,6\}$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Kernel certificate}

The arithmetic is certified in the cyclotomic quotient
$\mathbb{Z}[t]/(t^4+t^3+t^2+t+1) = \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_5]$: $t$ is a unit with
$t \cdot t^4 = 1$, of order exactly $5$ ($t^k \ne 1$ for $k = 1,\dots,4$),
and $(t^4)^k \ne 1$ for $k = 1,\dots,4$ ($t^8 = t^3$, $t^{12} = t^2$,
$t^{16} = t$). Under the standard isomorphism $t \mapsto \zeta_5$ the
matrix $\mathrm{diag}(t, t^4)$ becomes $A$, with determinant
$t\,t^4 = 1$. All 13 Lean theorems are closed kernel computations,

arithmetic over $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_5]$ and numerically over $\mathbb{C}$.

\section{Boundary}

Only the literal period-set claim is refuted. The set $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$ is
the crystallographic restriction, which constrains finite subgroups in
other settings — not the periods of individual Jacobian-one automorphisms.
